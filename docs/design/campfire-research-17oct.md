# What group text games teach the Campfire (research, 17 Oct, for Fake Guru)

Fake Guru: "Research other mobile text-based games (like Telegram games). Will this free-play organic campfire work? Or does Garrick always bring the banter back to the mystery? How does it work? And this happens async and live, like the dynamics of a real group chat."

Sources are guides, reviews and designers' own accounts, not formal studies; they agree with one another.

## What works elsewhere

**1. Free chat needs a reason and a decision.** The group chat games that thrive (Werewolf and Mafia bots on Telegram and Discord, Among Us) give the talk a shape: something new happens, everyone debates, then a vote that changes the game, often against a clock. Debate exists to reach the vote. ([MuffiaBot](https://github.com/gekas145/MuffiaBot); [Murder Mystery bot](https://top.gg/bot/590980247801954304); [Among Us critique](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/04/23/critical-play-bluffing-judging-and-getting-vulnerable-among-us/))

**2. Short live timers reward the loudest.** Among Us's short meetings reward whoever speaks first and most confidently over careful reasoning ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/04/23/critical-play-bluffing-judging-and-getting-vulnerable-among-us/)). A slow deadline (dawn) suits a thinking game better.

**3. Mystery and intrigue suit slow, async play.** Play-by-post game masters find that fast action dies in async play while intrigue thrives ([New School Revolution](https://newschoolrevolution.com/2024/12/06/how-to-pbp)). Online mystery threads follow one pattern: the host posts the scene, players argue freely in one thread, they ask characters questions with an @ to draw out clues, and each may make one formal accusation ([17th Shard mystery game](https://www.17thshard.com/topic/119378-qf65-more-deception-murder-in-the-cosmere/); [E4 Halloween mystery](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/e4-halloween-murder-mystery-confessions-begin-11-8/44395/1.md)).

**4. Async needs a rhythm, recaps and a host who keeps moving.** Play-by-post game masters set a posting rhythm, restate "the case so far" for latecomers, keep in-character and out-of-character talk apart, carry the story on when someone goes quiet, and hand out rewards often because slow play is otherwise thankless ([New School Revolution](https://newschoolrevolution.com/2024/12/06/how-to-pbp); [Unwinnable](https://unwinnable.com/?p=105268); [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/for-those-of-you-who-have-set-up-play-by-post-games-how-do-you-do-it.713319/); [David Blandy](https://davidblandy.substack.com/p/how-to-run-a-play-by-post-game)). Recap posts help players who lose track ([Pelgrane's play-by-post recap](https://www.backerkit.com/c/projects/pelgrane-press/merryshire-detective-club/updates/50252)).

**5. Notifications work when they carry a decision.** Lifeline, a text game told through phone alerts, pulled players back because each alert asked for a meaningful choice; the waiting felt like part of the story; and catching up later carried no penalty ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-a-narrative-out-of-push-notifications-in-i-lifeline-i-); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/linked/ive-been-texting-with-an-astronaut/)).

**6. An AI storyteller in a group gets chaotic.** AI Dungeon's own help pages say group games "can get a little crazy" with overlapping actions; players report the AI acting for other players; the host has to supervise and edit ([Latitude](https://latitudegames.notion.site/Do-you-support-Multiplayer-29135a51e051431390bcac895d2c39f2); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Dungeon)).

## What it means for the fire
- **Free talk works only around something to talk about and a decision that counts.** Today's fire has the first (finds, pins) but not the second: a pin changes nothing.
- **Garrick should host, not police.** Banter is the glue of a friends' chat; he lets it run and steps in at set moments: when something new lands, when friends disagree, as dawn nears, and at the close.
- **Design for async first.** Most nights friends will not overlap. Dawn is the deadline; a "case so far" waits for each latecomer; "ask Garrick with an @" gives everyone a way to draw him in.
- **Make the night's call count,** like the vote in Werewolf: at dawn the fire's leaning becomes the party's call, read back when the story answers it.
- **Keep AI away from the story,** as the AI Dungeon evidence warns; use it at most for Garrick's voice in replies.
