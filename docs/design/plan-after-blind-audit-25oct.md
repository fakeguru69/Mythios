# The plan after the audits (25 Oct 2026)

Fake Guru: "With your audit and also other AI audit (use your best judgement), decide a plan on what we should work on."

The plan draws on:
- the three blind reviewers;
- Claude's play test;
- the two earlier read-mode reports;
- the 24 Oct full-game audit.

The detail is in `docs/audits/blind-play-and-score-25oct.md`.

## How the plan was decided

1. **Fix what real friends will hit first.** Week 1, and the screens around it, are what Mei, Dev and Arjun will see in their first days. A slip there costs the most.
2. **Prove each design fix on one week before doing six.** Every reviewer says the same three things are missing:
   - choices that change the story;
   - a fire whose clues and calls pay off;
   - a Gathering that answers the week.

   We fix all three in Week 1 only, play it with real friends, and roll out week by week only what works.
3. **Story text and numbers stay Fake Guru's.** Claude drafts, he reads, then Claude builds. Screen-only fixes go straight in.
4. **The finale waits until the pattern is proven.** It is the biggest story decision, and it is six weeks away for any party.

## Phase 1: the screens (no story text; Claude builds now)

1. **One tap from the cover to a party, and one from an invite to joining.** Today a new player taps "Start a party" twice, and an invited friend taps JOIN twice.
2. **Calmer big moments in Week 1.** In a small party every first is a crown, so the first shared fire is flooded with nine crown and badge notices. In Week 1 they collapse into one line per friend ("Ana took four crowns."). Each is shown on its own from Week 2.
3. **The floating "↓ The fire" button** no longer covers the day summary's text.
4. **The doubled heading** "The last light · the list, by the last light".
5. **"One breath", the screen half.** The other friends tap one of the options as advice. The chosen friend sees "Mei and Dev say: GO OUT NOW" and still chooses alone. No numbers change yet.

## Phase 2: the story slips (Claude drafts, Fake Guru reads, then built)

1. **The 14 slips the reviewers found.** For example:
   - "Show her your empty hands" with the horse in hand;
   - the Week 5 countdown is one night off;
   - the "no fire" night that has a fire;
   - the river both rising and low;
   - the linen on wrists, not palms;
   - Meira named dead too early in a recap;
   - Gathering 4's echo of "This was never about you";
   - one plain line that the dark moon is an eclipse.
2. **Two-friend versions of the recaps.** Any recap that assumes more than one other friend ("each of them") gets a version for a party of two. The check that keeps short-party lines honest is extended to the recaps.
3. **Garrick's fire lines that use game words or fall flat.** "On Day 2 a horn button" becomes "At the mill a horn button". The W3D3 and W4D2 openers are rewritten, kept true to the Deception Table, and sent for approval.

One document, one read.

## Phase 3: Week 1 as the pilot (story and numbers; drafted, read, built, then played)

Everything the reviewers agree is missing, done properly for Week 1 only:

1. **One question a night at the fire.** The italic sub-question goes, and each night's click bears on the pinned question.
2. **A click every night, and one that joins a road find to a lead.** What a costly road earns then matters at the fire. Garrick brings the missing half in a party of two (already built). The click stops stating the answer; it lays the two side by side and asks.
3. **The choices that change nothing:**
   - each is cut, or made a real trade;
   - Day 6's map becomes [READ IT ALOUD] or [PASS IT HAND TO HAND, IN SILENCE]: aloud is quicker and every friend gets the find; in silence nobody overhears, and the courier is not there at Gathering 1;
   - no road always pays a Relic.
4. **The week reaches the Gathering:** two to four lines at Gathering 1 that only appear because of what the party chose or called that week. For example, the fire's call on Day 6, or a friend who caught the courier.
5. **"One breath", the numbers half.** A small edge for each option, which Fake Guru picks. For example:
   - the bold option gives +2 on the first roll;
   - the clever option takes 10 off the thing in the way;
   - holding still lets nobody use a Relic, but everyone gets +1.

   Following the table's advice adds a little more.
6. **The long read on Day 5 (429 words)** gets one small act in the middle.

**Then a real play-test of Week 1:** Fake Guru and two or three friends, on their phones, with a short questionnaire. We go on only once it is right.

## Phase 4: Weeks 2 to 6, one week at a time

Phase 3's pattern, applied week by week, each drafted and read first:
- one question a night;
- road-find clicks;
- real forks, including each Day 6's map;
- the week answered at its Gathering.

The day-ending long reads are split up along the way (W4D1, W3D1, W4D5, W1D3).

## Phase 5: the finale's agency

Two or three drafted options for Fake Guru to choose from. Each makes the party's last decision change the outcome, and lets the fire's suspicion of Garrick pay off in the hall. Examples:
- [TAKE THE FIFTH] or [HIDE IT IN THE WALL ROOM];
- a Week 6 pin that names him, read back in the hall.

Season 2 picks up whichever way it went.

## Not on the plan for now

- More audits before the Week 1 play-test. Real friends will teach more than another read.
- New features. The game is feature-complete for a season; this plan is about depth.
- Reworking fights beyond what the forks already do. Reviewers noted fights are "one tap a round", but the Gathering's Strike or Cover is enough until the play-test says otherwise.
