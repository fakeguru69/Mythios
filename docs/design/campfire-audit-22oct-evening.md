# Campfire audit, evening of 22 Oct

A fresh pass after the rewrite that landed the same evening (`da5ef73`). Read against the live fire, not the old notes. The earlier audit is `docs/design/campfire-experience-audit-22oct.md`. This one asks whether that rewrite, and the fire as it stands now, actually does the job.

Plain English. Nights are named the way the table would say them: week, then day.

## The short answer

The writing got closer to a night by a fire. The evening the player sits in is still a stack of jobs.

Garrick now opens on the day they just lived. On almost every night, one particular find changes what he asks. The five clicks that used to answer the wrong question have been pointed at the argument. The quiet night in the hillside shaft is actually quiet. That is real progress, and it is in the game the party plays, not only in a draft.

What did not change is the shape of the night. The answer still appears by itself once two cards are on the table. The big question is still a menu pinned over the talk. The old verse is still a box that asks for a paragraph. A friend who arrives late can see that they are needed. They are still not asked to say the thing.

So: the content is now good enough to carry a ritual. The ritual is not quite there.

## Did the fire do what it is for?

Four things were asked of it. Here is each one, after the rewrite.

**Rest after the day.** The new openings earn the chair. At the inn he says four strangers, four torn pages, and an empty chair upstairs. After the Widow's Tooth he names the smoke and the man who bolted. At the start of the second week he names the empty slot in the mine and the cart in the dark. On the night they are spotted at the shaft, he says nothing: he takes the pot off the coals and sits with his back to the rock. Those are the lines of a man who was there.

A few openings still belong to any inn. The night they start toward the warm country, he says he would settle for a warm supper. The night they come down the steep hill behind the hall, he says the hill was steeper than it looked. Small, and easy to fix, because the pattern is now obvious: name the wound, then sit.

The daytime roll does not survive the walk back in a way that feels like a story. The find always comes. If the roll failed, the player also sees the sum written out, with the word passed or failed, and then one of a handful of "you found it late" lines. The lateness is the right idea. The arithmetic is a worksheet brought to the fire. What they chose matters. How well they did it is a caption.

**Solving it with other people.** Two different people still have to lay the matching pair before the realisation appears. That rule is the right one. Three things around it got better.

- If only one half is down, the puzzle box now says the other half is still in someone's hands, or out in the dark. A friend logging in at eleven can see they were waited for.
- When the realisation does appear, it carries the names of the two who laid the cards.
- On thirty-five of the thirty-six nights, one named find swaps Garrick's question for a line about that find. The first night, which is alone, is the only one without this. A card on the table can finally change the evening.

The hole is the same hole. Nobody has to notice. The paragraph arrives finished, under a label that is not his voice. The sharp player then picks a side off a menu and may type nothing. Agree and Doubt are a nod, not a talk. A party of one never gets the realisation, which is fair, but if that one person somehow holds both halves the "other half is still out there" line also disappears, so the night just stops. There is no sentence for "you are holding both, and this table needs another pair of eyes."

Small parties still stall in a quieter way. There are three finds and one pair. Two friends can each bring home a true thing and never be the pair. The new half-laid line only speaks when one of the two matching cards is down. The third find can change his question, which helps, and then the night still feels unfinished.

**Warmth.** He is closer to a companion than he was this morning. He asks a named friend about a named object. The cup nights are still the same stage direction every time: he puts a cup in front of the empty place. It is a good direction. Eight times, unchanged, it becomes a stage direction. The habit the players could learn is "when someone is missing, he talks to the cup." They cannot learn it while it is identical and unlabeled as a habit.

The lines that name a friend by their best trait are still a roll wearing a voice. "He asks the quick one" is fine once. Asking the craftiest friend about a die, and the most charming friend about a knife, with no reason the table can feel, is the machine showing. When the player is alone he drops the name, which is right.

**The book.** The finds were already a book. The repaired sentences joined them. More on that below.

## The day, then the fire

The morning-after page is still the best payoff of a day's choices. It is concrete, it does not spoil the branch, and it ends by pulling them onward. The fire is a second room, not a continuation of that page, and that is fine if his first line is the bridge.

After this rewrite, it usually is. The failures are the nights where he hosts instead of witnesses, listed above, and the roll card, which is the day following them in as a receipt.

Whether they found the thing early or late barely changes the talk. The late line is one sentence, often shared by all three finds of that day. Sitting down, nobody asks "what took you so long out there?" The time of the find is a caption. It could be the first thing he says to that friend.

## Too many pieces on the table

A night can still contain all of this, in this order, more or less:

1. Who is here, and a small "around the fire" card of what people did.
2. The cup, if it is a cup night.
3. His opener, or a silence.
4. A greeting the first time someone arrives, and up to two noticing lines.
5. A gold box titled tonight's puzzle, with the question, and now a line if only half is laid.
6. Your own find, including the dice sum.
7. Each laid card, with Agree and Doubt.
8. The realisation, titled as the game speaking, now with the two names.
9. His nudge, which becomes the special line if the named card is down. The ordinary nudge is thrown away, not added to.
10. His memory of an earlier night.
11. A line if the table is unanimous or split.
12. The verse, once both words are found, then the meaning question, other people's answers, and a box.
13. A closer, after eight.
14. The talk itself.
15. Stuck to the top the whole time: tonight's question, as three buttons. You must pick before you can see what the others picked. You can fold it after.

The riddle is gone from the data. Good. Do not bring it back.

What the rewrite added is the right kind of addition: the half-laid sentence, the names on the realisation, and a bar at the bottom that says what is in your hands and offers one button, lay it on the table. That bar is how a tired player should be asked to contribute. It is the least form-like control on the screen.

What is still fighting him:

- The question menu floats over the stream for the whole night. He also asks a nudge. Those are two hosts.
- The puzzle box asks a second question, often a smaller one, before the cards have even met. On the best nights the realisation is the answer to the night's question and does not need its own heading.
- The verse card is a third host.
- The dice line is a fourth, and it is the only one that should simply go.

**What to cut or fold**

Keep one stream. He sits them down. Finds arrive under the finder's name, laid with that one button. When the pair is half there, he says so, not a caption. When it completes, he does not read the essay. He waits, then says the physical fact if nobody else has. Then he asks the night's question in his own words, once. The two readings are things a person could believe, said by players into the thread, not buttons labeled as a choice. The verse is read once, by him, with no question under it. The week's question stays the fight at the end of the week and the thing the gathering quotes.

The special line, when a particular card is laid, should sit beside his question, not erase it. Right now the sharper nightly question is often the one that vanishes. On the night they hear the picks, his ordinary question is what the diggers think is in the old room. If the stair card is laid, that question is replaced by a fact: nobody has used those stairs. The fact is good. Losing the question is not.

## The finds, and what happens when two of them meet

The finds themselves were not rewritten tonight, and they did not need to be. They are physical. A careful player can do something with them. They do not read as chatter from a passer-by.

The third find is in better shape than it was this morning, because the special line finally gives it a mouth. It is still not part of the realisation. That is fine when the third find is a red herring or a second worry. It is a problem only when the special line merely repeats the card in his voice.

The five repairs, and whether they hold:

- **The mill road, week 2 day 1.** The pair is now the horse that knew the fork, and the wheels that never checked at any other house. The realisation says either the horse knew the mill, or someone walked ahead of it. That is the night's argument, and it refuses to pick a side. The heavy ruts, which used to be wasted inside the wrong answer, are now the thing he asks about if that card is laid. This is the model. One leftover: the puzzle box still asks how the cart travelled in the dark. The realisation does not answer that. It answers why the cart only stopped at the mill. Change the question in the box, or delete the box.

- **The north road, week 4 day 2.** The pair is now the open road and the bare blades, set against the sheep-path that would have hidden them. The realisation says they were daring someone to stop them, or making sure the cart was seen. It does not say what is in the box. The eyes-on-the-box find is what he asks about instead. Both sides of the night's argument can still live. Good.

- **The chalk board, week 5 day 5.** The old realisation proved the camp was permanent, which the day had already shown. The new one says the count was drawn from the start, and that every head lifts with the beat, so the hall is keeping the same time. Calendar and omen both get a fact. The spare bedrolls become his question: where is the rest of the watch? Good.

- **The niche, week 6 day 1.** The old realisation proved no candle could ever show, which nearly killed one side of the argument. The new one says a hidden light burned there for generations, and tonight the niche is cold. The watchman's look at the wall is left as his question, which is the argument. Fair. The last clause, that the Stone in their hands is what used to sit there, is a step past the two cards. The cards prove an old light that the square could not see. They do not prove what the light was for. Cut that clause.

- **The last night.** The realisation finally uses the swept grit at the blank rock, together with every helm turned toward the hall. It tells them the dead garrison will have its back to the Seekers' door. It does not tell them whether bringing the fifth Stone in will bind him or wake him. That is the right size of gift on the last night. The empty posts are his other question. Good.

Quiet nights still have no realisation, on purpose: the village, the shaft, the polite town, the lake tower, the dead street, the barracks. The shaft was the broken one, because he talked and the night was marked quiet. He is silent there now. The dead street is the one that should stay unanswered. Unbroken dust, one scrape of metal, helms that do not match. He can ask. He should not tidy it. The new wording of the "it was only the dusk" side finally uses the dust and the crooked helms, so a player can choose it without feeling foolish.

Two special lines that do too much of the player's work:

- At the ford, if the threads are laid, he says someone carried the child along the bank. That is the realisation, given away off one card.
- Behind the hall, if they listen at the rock, he says the throne is on the other side. The card is the louder heartbeat. The throne is his guess, said as a fact.

Special lines that earn their place, because they ask:

- Fresh notches on the stick. What was he counting?
- She knew him by a knife. How many men carry a blade like that?
- He knelt three paces from the dark mouth. Did he choose that spot?
- Extra bedrolls, and a quiet square. Where is the rest of the watch?

## Did every day teach something new?

The days do. The fire, after this pass, mostly keeps up.

Week 1 still walks from a list, to a missing scholar, to a watched rock, to a chosen child, to a second taking, to a map that was a lie about the cart. Week 2 walks from a named next victim, to a birth-night, to a lock that opens for their hand, to a rite that will not pause, to a name scraped off the world, to an older room. Week 3 is the promise of home, the polite taking, the riders who ignore the marks, a child numbering her own trail, a name cut twice, a lamp and smoke. Week 4 is why they were spared, the box versus the boy, the wait on the water, the word moon-blood, a living child, a listener under the tower. Week 5 is a fifth thing, a heartbeat, a gate barred from inside, soldiers who may have moved, a count, a hidden room. Week 6 is the count, the dead walking, heat low on the wall, a door the Seekers left, the hour, and the choice.

The nights that used to repeat the day were the camp under the hall and the niche. Both now ask a further question. The niche's realisation is a new fact, not a restatement, as long as it stops before naming the Stone. The night they hear the picks no longer asks for a headcount.

The arguments are in good shape from the first week through the drowned town, and again on the dead street, the chalk, the niche, and the last night. Both sides have an object. The weak arguments are the ones where one side is the chapter they just played and the other is a flatter version of it. Those are fewer than they were.

The gathering still does not pay the fire back in full. It can quote a short line the party pinned, on a scattered handful of days, not as a rhythm the table learns. A split is still collapsed into the news that the fire was split. The vindication lands hardest where the next scene cuts both readings at once, as the wall does in the first week. Most other read-backs are a note in the margin. They do not show the object that made the clever reading look small.

## The verses

The verses do the job they were written for. Cold on the day, unbearable later.

- "One stayed close."
- "Morning came. The enemy did not."
- "His hand was bleeding, and he told us to sleep."
- "We made our children the only key."
- "If you are reading this, you are ours."

The fire's job is to make the party say what they think it means, without turning the line into homework, and without handing them the ending. The rewrite fixed some of the homework and broke some of the secrecy.

Questions that are better than this morning, because they ask for a feeling or a motive and do not add a fact:

- "Why leave four to wander and keep one behind?" The line already said four were scattered and one stayed. Asking why is the week's argument.
- "Is a mark like that a gift, or a sentence?"
- "What kind of victory leaves the victors afraid of the morning?"
- "Did he hide the price to spare them, or because they would have refused him?"
- "Did joining the five end the war, or only pause it?"
- "Were they watching to keep him in, or waiting for the night the seal would fail?"
- "What could five who loved him not bring themselves to do?"

Questions that put the ending in the ask:

- Week 1, the lock. The line says some locks already know their key. The question says the lock was shaped for someone not yet born. Nobody at that fire knows that. Take "not yet born" out. Ask why a lock would refuse a hand.
- Week 2, the morning he walked among them. The line says they would have died for him. The question says they became the ones who bound him. That binding is a later blow. Do not ask it yet. Ask what is missing from a morning everyone remembers and nobody describes.
- Week 3, "we locked with what we are." The question says living blood. The line does not. Blood is the gathering's word, or the line's own word on a later day. Ask what "what we are" could mean, if you ask at all.
- Week 3, the grave. The line says they never dug one, and that their blood would have to hold him. The question adds a sealed hall. Ask why a man they loved was left without a grave.
- Week 4, moon-blood. The line already says moon-blood will wake it if it is ever let near. The question offers "their own children returning" as one of the two choices. That is the twist, written as an option. Ask who "let near" is a warning to, and stop.

A few questions are simply about a different line than the one printed over them. "Remember that, when you hear where else we stood" is asked as if the five were begging strangers for forgiveness. "That is how we found the shards" is asked as if the shards answered to them. Stay with the verb the line used.

Even the good questions fail the second test. They appear under a heading that the verse is whole, in a box, after an anagram, asking for a short written answer. Other players' answers are visible without having to commit first. One fluent friend writes the reading. Everyone else agrees with it. He never reads the line. The gathering, which is where the blow is supposed to land, still does not set the verse beside what the party actually said.

The anagram can stay a private toy in the day. It should not be the door you knock on to hear the sentence.

## Does he lead, or does he fill time?

He leads on the nights where the new lines point at an object and stop. The mill. The numbered chips. The lamp that went into the hill. The chalk. The swept grit. Those are a guide.

He fills time where the special line restates the card, and where the live conversation still cannot touch the mystery. Ask him, in the thread, what the list means, and he is not allowed to answer. The steering is entirely the lines written ahead of time. That is safe. It also means the feeling of a man across the embers only exists inside those lines. Outside them he is a companion who goes quiet the moment the talk gets interesting.

He should be the only voice that advances the night. Right now three voices do: him, a narrator who gets the realisation, and the headings. The realisation should be the last thing, and it should be said by someone at the table, or by him, late, as a fact about objects, never as the motive.

## Lines to keep, and lines to change

**Keep**

> Four strangers, four torn pages, and an empty chair upstairs.

> A woman packing in fear does not leave her book in order.

> Round every boot the dust lies in one even skin. The only prints in it are yours.

> Either the horse knew the mill road, or someone walked ahead of it.

> When the Seekers' door opens tomorrow night, the entire garrison will have its back to you.

The reason is the same in each: a thing you could touch, then a conclusion that does not repeat itself.

**Change**

1. The puzzle question on the mill road. It still asks how the cart travelled in the dark. The cards now say something else. Ask, if a question must be shown at all: did anyone have to lead that horse, or did it already know the door?

2. The niche realisation, last sentence. End on the cold niche and the old soot. Do not say the Stone in their hands is what the light was for.

3. The ford, if the threads are laid. Do not tell them the child was carried along the bank. Ask: those threads are at a child's height, and they are not in the ruts. Who was walking where the cart was not?

4. The rock behind the hall. Do not say the throne is on the other side. Ask: the beat is louder with your ear on that stone than anywhere in the crack. What is the wall between you and?

5. Week 1's lock question. "Why would a lock refuse every hand but one?" Not "someone not yet born."

6. Week 2's love question. "He walked among them the morning after, and not one of them says what he looked like. What are they leaving out?" Not "the ones who bound him."

7. Week 3's blood question. Leave it as the line. If he must speak, he says he does not know what "what we are" means, and he asks them.

8. Week 4's moon-blood question. "The line says do not let it near. Near to what, and who is it warning?" Do not offer their children as a choice.

9. The supper opener, week 3 day 1. "They told a stolen child she was going home. Eat while you work out whether that was kindness."

10. The special line on the picks, week 2 day 6. Keep "nobody has used those stairs." Add it. Do not use it to erase "what do they think is waiting in that room?"

## If the fire is going to be the reason they come back

The biggest missed chance is unchanged, and it is closer to being fixed than it was this morning, because the cards, the pair, and the waiting message already exist. The game still will not let the players be the ones who understand it.

Five changes, in the order they would be felt.

1. **The realisation waits.** Highest impact. The pair is already detected, and it already needs two people. Stop posting the paragraph the moment the second card lands. Give the two finders a silence. If someone says the physical fact, he repeats it shorter and stops. If the thread stays empty, he says it himself, late. Credit the names either way. This is the whole difference between a ritual and a cutscene.

2. **One voice, three fewer boxes.** High impact. Take the question menu off the top, take the puzzle heading off the middle, take the meaning box off the verse. He asks once, after the fact, not before. The bar that lays what is in your hands can stay. It is the only control the night needs. Delete the dice sum from this room. Keep the late line, in his mouth: "You were a long time at the ford."

3. **The verse is read, not assigned.** High impact, and it is mostly restraint. He reads the line when it is whole. He does not ask the essay. The six gatherings are where the line is read again, beside the blow, and beside whatever the party actually said on the night they first heard it. Fix the five questions that add a fact, even if the box goes, because those questions will leak into his mouth the moment someone uses them as his prompt.

4. **A laid card adds a question. It does not replace one, and it does not solve itself.** Content only. Go through the thirty-five special lines. Anywhere he announces the meaning, turn it into a question. Anywhere the special line is thinner than the nightly question, say both. The table will trust a man who changes when a find comes in. They will not trust a man who sometimes gets vaguer because a find came in.

5. **Absence and disagreement are quoted by name.** The cup, the bottle, and the dawn call are already a ritual waiting to be one ritual. He speaks the missing friend's note to the cup. A half-laid pair gets one line to the missing hand, from him. In the morning, a split is two sentences with two names, not "the fire was split." A unanimous night is dangerous and he says so, which he already can. Make that the thing the gathering repeats, not a paraphrase.

Do that, and the content you already wrote has somewhere to live. The finds are good. The new openings are good. The repaired clicks are good. The fire is one good night away from being the part they tell their friends about. The missing piece is not another system. It is letting them speak before he does.
