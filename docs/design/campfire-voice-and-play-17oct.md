# The Campfire: Garrick's voice, open play, and who he talks to (17 Oct, for Fake Guru)

Fake Guru: "the rules keep AI out. So no banter? Will keeping to canned message make Garrick sound artificial and give away that they are canned? Also, is this a good place where we allow D&D-style organic play and dialogue? Or is it better to stick to a closed-loop system? Should players be able to talk among themselves with Garrick? Garrick only talks to me? How is that presented in the campfire? Again, it's a redesign, so anything goes. Think it through."

## 1. Stored lines or AI: neither alone works

**Stored lines alone sound canned.** Within a week a player notices the same opener, the same "Pinned.", the same rhythm, and that Garrick never answers what they actually said. A host who cannot respond is a sign, not a person. Filling templates with real names and clue titles helps, but it cannot reply to "Mei, you're wrong, the paper was a map".

**AI alone breaks the game.** Garrick's whole season rests on one rule: he never lies, and he deceives only by true statements and withheld meaning. An AI improvising about the case will sooner or later invent a fact, hint at the secret, or grade a theory. It cannot be trusted to say true things about a story it does not fully hold, and a single slip ("the Order would never…") ruins the finale.

**The middle path: stored lines for anything about the case, AI for the voice around it.**
| Garrick's job at the fire | Who writes it |
|---|---|
| Anything about the case: clues, readings, the fragment, the story's facts, his own past | **stored lines only**, filled with tonight's real names and clue titles |
| Steering: the arc, calling a split, bringing in the quiet, the closer | **stored lines**, chosen by what is happening |
| Replying to what friends say, in his voice, without adding any fact ("Strong words, Mei. Dev, are you going to let that stand?") | **AI**, through the existing guard |
| Banter: food, weather, aches, tired boots, the fire | **AI**, as today's small talk in the party chat |
| "While you were away" | **no AI**: the friends' actual messages since you were last here, gathered under one line ("Six messages since this morning"). An AI summary would put words in real friends' mouths, which the rules forbid (Core Rules: "never puts a spoken line in a named friend's mouth"). |

**The guard** (the party chat's, made stricter at the fire): the AI sees only what the friends who are there have seen (never Garrick's hidden meanings, never the secret, never the hidden clue leans); it may not state a fact about the case, his past, or the future; the existing filter blocks the banned phrases, the gated words ("Malakor" never at the fire) and anything shaped like an answer; it may not praise or judge a theory (no "good thinking", "close", "you're onto something": each is a grade); its reply is one or two short sentences; if it fails the filter, a stored line is used instead. The rules today forbid AI at the fire (Campfire rules §E); this changes that rule, so it is your call. It needs the Gemini key.

**Without the AI (until the key is in, or if you say no):** Garrick speaks less and better: only when something real happens, never the same line twice in a week, and never pretends to have read what a friend typed.

## 2. D&D-style open play, or a closed loop?

**Open talk, fixed truth.** The story is authored and fixed: true statements, a secret, set outcomes each week. A game-master AI that changes the story or invents clues would break exactly what makes the finale land. So:
- **Open:** what friends say. Theories, arguments, in-character talk, jokes, plans for the Gathering, anything.
- **Fixed:** what is true, what the clues are, what Garrick knows and says about the case, and what happens next.
That is how a good mystery night with friends works: the host holds the answer; the table can say whatever it likes.

## 3. Can friends talk among themselves with Garrick?

**Yes, in one shared conversation at the fire.** Friends talk to each other, and Garrick is in the room:
- **To the fire:** everything a friend writes is seen by every friend who has played the day.
- **Garrick speaks to the fire** when something happens (the split, the fragment whole, a quiet friend), and, with the AI, replies in his voice when a friend addresses him by name.
- **Asked about the case at the fire,** he answers from his stored lines for all to see, as in the party chat today. "Malakor" is never said at the fire (the rules).

## 4. Garrick and you alone: how it looks

Two private things, kept apart from the shared conversation so it stays readable:
1. **Your next step:** his guidance to you, one card above the typing box (from the previous design). It is not a chat message; it changes as you go.
2. **A word with Garrick:** a private side conversation, opened from his name at the top of the fire or from the typing box. It slides over the fire like a drawer, marked "Only you see this". It holds your private questions and his answers, as built today.
Nothing private ever appears in the shared conversation, and nothing shared hides in the drawer.

## 5. Decisions for Fake Guru
1. Stored lines for anything about the case; AI only for replies in his voice and banter, behind the stricter guard. Yes / no (no keeps the fire stored-only, with fewer, better lines).
2. Open talk, fixed truth. Yes / change.
3. One shared conversation with Garrick in it; your guidance and private questions kept to you. Yes / change.
