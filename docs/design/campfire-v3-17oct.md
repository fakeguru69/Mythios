# The Campfire, version 3: the redesign (17 Oct, for Fake Guru)

Fake Guru: "Your piece or clue needs to be meaningful: either it helps to reveal a piece of the mystery or maybe offers a plausible and interesting theory … I need you to see the big picture and redesign the campfire from a content (that may even need to start from the clue you get during Fan Out) and gameplay point of view. Besides our strong story, the Campfire might become the most interesting part of our game if we design it well. Look into it deeply, rigorously and creatively."

This document brings together and supersedes the earlier proposals of 17 Oct: `campfire-audit-17oct.md`, `campfire-chat-design-17oct.md`, `campfire-voice-and-play-17oct.md`, `campfire-content-verdict-17oct.md`, `campfire-research-17oct.md`, `campfire-loop-17oct.md`, `campfire-mechanics-17oct.md`. Their reasoning stands; this is the design.

## 1. The big picture: two mysteries at one fire

The season is two mysteries, and the fire is the only place both live.

1. **The case.** Children taken, a list with your names, an Order, a sleeping king, five Stones. The party chases it week by week. Each week has a question ("Why these children, and not any others?") and the Gathering answers it.
2. **The man at the fire.** Garrick never lies. Every line he says is true and means something else (Garrick's Recap holds all of them: the extra cup, "My grandmother taught me that name", the knots in the linen). An attentive player could catch him from his own words. Nobody tells them to try.

Today the fire serves neither. The redesign makes it serve both:
- **The case is played openly at the fire,** night by night, with evidence each friend brings, a question, a call, and the truth coming back to meet it.
- **The man at the fire is never mentioned.** He hosts every night, he hears every theory, and he is never a subject. Players may quietly mark anything that strikes them. After the finale, everything comes back: their calls beside the truth, the lines they marked, and one closing line from MYTHIOS: *Everything you said at the fire, he heard.*

That second layer is what can make the fire the best part of the game: thirty-six nights of friends theorising with the culprit pouring the drinks.

## 1a. Four layers of content, each with its own payoff (Fake Guru: "campfire can serve as daily recap and the larger mystery arc, which is seasonal, but campfire can also deal with weekly or even daily mysteries")

| Layer | What it is | Paid off | How it shows at the fire |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Today** (the recap) | What the party did and found today; each friend saw a different part (a different Notice line, a pass or a fail, a different lead) | at once | **the table** is today's shared memory: everyone's pieces in one place, and "what we did today" |
| **Tonight's puzzle** (the daily mystery) | A small question today raises that the evidence can actually answer: who knew where the boy slept? whose cart was it? | **tonight**, when the right pieces click; if they never do, the next morning's chapter or recap gives it anyway | **no question box:** the puzzle is named at the top of the table, and a click solves it ("Solved: …"), crediting the friends whose pieces did it |
| **Tonight's question** (the weekly mystery, one step) | A theory question the evidence leans on but cannot settle, one rung of the week's question | the next morning, later in the week, or at the Gathering | **the one debated question:** secret pins, the party's call at dawn, read back when the story answers |
| **The long threads** (the season) | The questions that run all season: who put the list in your box? where is Meira? what is the fifth? | at the Gatherings and the finale | **the casebook:** each thread stays open all season; Link pieces attach to it as they turn up; the thread closes, with the party's best calls read back, when the story answers it |

The fifth layer, the man at the fire, is never a thread and never named: only the notebook, and the reckoning after the finale.

**One night, then, has exactly two things to do, each with its own mechanic:** solve tonight's puzzle by laying pieces down (a click), and call tonight's question by pinning before dawn. The recap is the table itself; the season grows quietly in the casebook. Nothing else asks for an answer.

**The rhythm of a week:** Days 1 and 2 open the week with puzzles that settle fast and questions that set its direction; Days 3 to 5 climb the week's question; Day 6's question is the week's question itself (the party's case), answered at the Gathering. Season threads gain a piece most days and close at their Gathering or the finale.

**Holes checked for the layers:** a puzzle never gates the story (unsolved, its answer still arrives with the morning); a puzzle is solved only by a click, so it rewards laying pieces down, and a party of two can still solve most (each puzzle's pair is written across leads and the pass line, which any two friends can cover); the puzzle and the question never ask the same thing; a season thread is never one the story answers the next day.

## 2. What "meaningful" means for a piece

A **piece** is one thing a friend brings to the fire. Every piece must do at least one of these four jobs, or it is cut:

| Job | What it does | Example (from Week 2 Day 2) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reveal** | A true fact the chapter did not give, that the story later confirms | "The paper in his fist": he read the boy's name off a paper (the ledger at Gathering 2) |
| **Tilt** | Evidence that leans the night's question one way | "Straight to the bed": he knew where the boy slept |
| **Turn** | True, but invites a plausible wrong theory, which the story later turns (Garrick's own method, in miniature) | Garrick's "Family habit. We always pour one for whoever can't be with us.": heard as Meira's cup, turned at the finale. The fire's Turn pieces are written the same way. |
| **Link** | Ties today to an earlier day, so the case builds | The horn button with dark thread, which matches the strip of coat on Day 5 |

And every piece records **where it pays off**: a later day, a Gathering, the finale. A piece that pays off nowhere is not meaningful. Clues that lead nowhere today (the notched stick, the worn path, the toy horse) are either given a payoff or cut.

**Turn pieces matter most.** A mystery where every clue points straight at the truth is a quiz. A fair mystery has true clues that tempt a wrong theory, and a reveal that makes the player see the clue again. That is exactly how Garrick deceives the party all season, so the fire teaches players to read true statements carefully, the very skill that could catch him.

## 3. Where pieces come from: the day hands each friend their own

Each friend ends the day holding pieces nobody else holds (the Keep Talking lesson: talk is needed when nobody has the whole picture). The day already makes them; today most are wasted.

| Source | Who holds it | Today | In v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Notice line** | Each friend, the line for their best Attribute (six per day) | shown in the chapter, then forgotten | a piece: **Tilt** or **Reveal** |
| **Pass line** | Only friends who passed the day's Challenge | shared at the fire since 17 Oct | a piece: usually **Reveal** or **Link** (the horn button) |
| **[PRESS ON] detail** | Only friends who pressed on and passed | shown, never used again | a piece: **Link** ("It is the same cart") |
| **Last-light clue** | Each friend, the lead they followed | on the board | a piece, any job; the three leads of a night are written as a set |
| **The day's fragment** | The party, once both words are found | a puzzle, then a side question | when whole, a shared piece: the Order's own scripture |

**The last light, redesigned** (where the content starts, as Fake Guru said):
- The three leads are written as a **set** for the night's question: one Reveal, one Tilt, one Turn, or a Link where the week needs it. Nothing is flavour.
- **You see where friends have gone** ("Mei went to the doorway") before you choose, so a party spreads out without anyone waiting for anyone. A lead nobody takes stays "Nobody went to the yard tonight" on the table, which is itself something to talk about.
- **The roll:** the piece always comes back (canon: evidence is never gated on a roll). A pass adds the fragment's first letter, as now.

## 4. Pieces that belong together: the moment the table clicks

Each night has **one or two pairs**: two pieces from different friends (two leads, or a lead and a pass line) that together show something neither shows alone. When both are on the table, whoever laid them and in whatever order, the fire **clicks**: MYTHIOS (the narrator, not Garrick) states what the two show together, once, in the thread, crediting both friends.

> **The paper in his fist** (Mei) and **Straight to the bed** (Dev): *He read one name off a paper, then walked to one bed without a single wrong turn. He came knowing which child, and where that child slept.*

Why it works:
- **It rewards laying your piece down**, and the friend who completes a pair hours later feels it land (async by nature).
- **It narrows the question without answering it.** Here: the knowledge came from outside the house. *From whom* stays open for the fire to argue, and Gathering 2 answers it.
- **It gives the party small wins every night,** which play-by-post games say slow play needs.

Rules: a click never answers the night's question outright; it is stated by MYTHIOS in plain truth; a party of two may not complete every pair, and that is fine; a pair is never written across two Notice lines (which friend holds which Notice line depends on their best Attribute and cannot be relied on).

## 5. The night: one question, and how it plays

**The question.** One per night, open when the fire opens (never already answered by that day's chapter). It is written as a real question with two strong readings and room for Neither and your own words. It records **where the story answers it**: the next morning, later in the week, the Gathering, or the finale. Never "never".

**How a night plays, for one friend** (about three minutes if that is all they want):
1. **Arrive.** Garrick's card says what happened since you were last here: who has been, what is on the table, any click, anything asked of you.
2. **Lay down your pieces.** Your last-light piece goes down by itself; your Notice line and any pass or press piece are one tap each. Optional rule, to test: you see friends' pieces once you have laid one of yours (BeReal's "give to see").
3. **The table.** Every piece with its friend's name. Any click so far. Tap **agree** or **doubt** on a piece, or reply in the thread.
4. **Pin your reading,** in secret. Then everyone's readings open (pin before peek). Agree or doubt a friend's reading; reply.
5. **Rest for the night.** Garrick's closer, what we did today, the Pull.
You may change your reading until dawn. "Dev changed sides." is posted, never which side.

**Dawn (5:00am): the party's call.** The reading most friends pinned becomes the party's call; even means the fire is split; no pins means no call. Names, never counts.

**When the story answers:** the call is read back at that moment, beside the truth, by name and in the friends' own words: *On Day 2 the fire called it: from a written list. Mei and Jo said so; Dev held out.* The story itself shows what is true. Nobody is graded.

## 6. The week and the season

**The week climbs.** The six nights' questions climb towards the week's question; Day 6's question *is* the week's question, and the party's call on it is **the party's case**. The Gathering opens by reading the case and the week's calls, and the Gathering answers it.

**The casebook.** A page in the Bag that keeps every night: the question, the pieces laid (with names), the clicks, the party's call, and, once the story has answered it, the answer stamped beside it. It grows all season. It is the reward that slow play needs: the party's own investigation, written down.

**The notebook.** Hold any line, anywhere (a chapter, the fire, Garrick, a friend's piece), and **mark it**, with a few words if you like. Private. It lives in the Bag. It is a detective's habit, nothing more; the game never says what to mark.

**After the finale: the reckoning.** Next to Garrick's Recap:
- **The party's calls,** night by night, beside what was true.
- **Your notebook,** with every line you marked that was one of Garrick's (from the Deception Table), and what it meant.
- MYTHIOS's last line on it: *Everything you said at the fire, he heard.*
A friend who marked "Family habit. We always pour one for whoever can't be with us." in Week 1 will see it: the moment that makes people replay a season and tell their friends.

## 7. Garrick at the fire

He hosts; he never investigates, never concludes, never grades. He speaks at set moments, from stored lines filled with real names and piece titles (and, if the AI is approved, replies to banter in his voice, never about the case):
- **The night opens:** the question, and the case so far for latecomers.
- **A piece lands that nobody has answered:** "Dev's piece is on the table: the paper in his fist."
- **The fire splits:** "Mei leans one way, Dev the other. Dev, what would you say to Mei?"
- **Everyone agrees too fast:** "Everyone leans the same way. What would it take to be wrong?"
- **A friend has laid nothing while others talk:** "Jo, you've laid nothing down yet."
- **Last call, in the evening:** "The fire's call closes at dawn."
- **@Garrick:** asked about the case, he answers from stored lines, for all.
- **The close:** his closer.

**Lines with a second meaning, at the fire.** A small number of his fire lines can be written for the Deception Table: true, warm, and different on a second reading. For example, when the fire splits: *"Good. Argue it out. I listen better than I talk."* Each is new text and goes through the Garrick check and Fake Guru's approval like every Deception Table line.

## 8. The screen

The group chat stays the basis. On top of it:
- **Pinned, top:** **The table** (pieces, clicks, agree and doubt), **Tonight's question** (pin, readings, the call), **The fragment** (the puzzle; its text joins the table when whole).
- **The thread:** friends' messages, short event lines (a piece laid, a click, a pin, a change of side), MYTHIOS's clicks, Garrick's moments. One stream, in time order, for live and async alike.
- **Garrick's card,** yours alone, above the typing box: your next step.
- **The typing box:** to the fire, or a private word with Garrick.
- **In the Bag:** the casebook and the notebook.

## 10. What "working" means, and how we will know
Each night, from the game's own records:
1. **Reach:** most friends who played the day come to the fire.
2. **Contribution:** each friend who comes lays at least one piece or pins.
3. **Response:** each friend who comes responds to a friend: agree, doubt, reply, or a changed reading. This is the heart of it.
4. **Clicks:** most nights, at least one pair clicks.
5. **Pull back:** some friends come back the same night after an alert.
6. **Payoff:** friends talk in the thread when a call is read back.
7. **Light:** a friend in a hurry does 1 to 3 in three minutes.
These numbers get logged per night, so the first real party shows what works.

## 12. Holes checked
- **Never grading:** the call is read back beside the story's truth; clicks state facts, not who was right; no gold on theories (canon). Clicks pay no gold either unless Fake Guru sets a number (no number is invented here).
- **Nobody waits on anybody:** every step works alone; clicks fire whenever the second piece lands; the call forms at dawn with whoever pinned.
- **Spoilers and the secret:** pieces are laid only at the fire, behind the "played the day" gate; Garrick is never a subject; the notebook never suggests what to mark; the reckoning opens only after the finale.
- **Turn pieces stay fair:** every Turn piece is true, and the story later turns it in plain sight.
- **A click states only what canon supports:** every click line is checked against the episode files before approval (two lines in this draft's example were corrected for exactly this).
- **Party of two, or alone:** fewer pieces and fewer clicks, but the loop holds; a friend alone still lays, pins and gets the read-back.
- **Hoarding:** allowed; Garrick's quiet-friend line invites; the last-light piece goes down by itself.
- **First-use gates and Garrick's rules:** every new line is checked against both before approval.
- **The fragment race** (Fake Guru's "C") is untouched, and since 17 Oct any friend may find both words, so the fragment opens at the fire sooner.
- **Workload:** this is a content rebuild of 36 nights. That is why Week 1 goes first as a sample, then the rest week by week, as the storybook pass did.

## 13. How it gets built
**Phase 1, mechanics on today's content** (no new story text; can start once approved):
the screen (chat, pins, card); laying pieces from today's Notice, pass, press and last-light lines; agree and doubt; secret pins and side changes; the party's call at dawn; the casebook; the notebook; the alerts.

**Phase 2, content, week by week, each approved before building:**
for each night, the question and where it is answered; the three leads as a set with their jobs and payoffs; the pass and press pieces' jobs; one or two clicks; Garrick's moments; any Deception Table lines; the read-back points. Week 1 first.

**Phase 3, the reckoning:** the party's calls and the notebook beside Garrick's Recap, after the finale.
