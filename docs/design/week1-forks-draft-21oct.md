# Week 1's 7 Forks: Drafted as Readable Trades (Piece E2, 21 Oct 2026)

**Rules followed (`docs/design/next-build-prompt-18oct.md` §4.1–4.2, `docs/design/one-thread-18oct.md`):**
1. **Forks are readable trades, never hidden luck.** Every fork is one of three kinds:
   - **Trade:** the wording before the buttons shows what you gain and what you risk.
   - **Fit:** each road suits a different Attribute; a fork before a Challenge picks which of the Challenge's two Attributes you roll with.
   - **Who you are:** no numbers; your stance shapes the scene and what you notice in the room.
2. **No +2 / −2 on rolls.**
3. **Every path leaves something of its own:** each option gets its own detail in the chapter, its own `⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED` outcome line, and its own **Road find** carried to the fire (waiting behind the `+` button so friends who took different roads can lay their finds on the table).
4. **Stakes (~1 in 3):** 3 of the 7 forks carry a mechanical stake (Fork 1: who strikes first on Day 3; Fork 2: which Attribute you roll on Day 4's Challenge; Fork 5: who strikes first on Day 5).
5. **One Relic reward for a careful reader:** Fork 6 on Day 5 (`[HOLD OUT THE CARVED HORSE]`) awards **1 Might Relic** (no gold, preserving the gear curve in `docs/numbers-pass-15oct.md`) to the reader who connects Day 3's child's carved horse with Day 4's hint about the woman two hours east. The other two roads still get their own clues for the fire.
6. **Canon & first-use gates:** Every existing canon sentence is kept word for word; additions are marked **NEW**. Zero banned or gated words (`four`, `five`, `king`, `the Order`, `Malakor`, `descendant`).

## Fork 1 · Day 3 (The Widow's Tooth) — Trade + Fight Stake

**Why it needed fixing:** Previously, nothing before the buttons warned that crouching at the stone put your back to the bracken, and `[GO TO THE MARKING]` was punished (he strikes first) without giving the player anything of its own.

**Setup passage (signposting the trade):**
> There are tracks at its foot: several people, not long ago, and a cart. **NEW:** The ruts run off toward deep bracken and a thread of smoke beyond; low on the stone, under the overhang, something has been cut into the rock where you would have to crouch with your back to the open ground to read it.

`[GO TO THE MARKING]` `[FOLLOW THE CART-RUTS]`

- **Road A — `GO TO THE MARKING`:**
  > You crouch at the stone and put your fingers to the cut lines. They are cold, and gritty under your touch. **NEW:** In the deepest groove, kept dry by the overhang, a crumb of black charcoal comes away on your thumb. Somebody pressed paper to this stone not many days ago. That is where you are, with your back to the bracken, when the bracken moves.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You found fresh charcoal in the cut stone · Your back was to the bracken: he strikes first.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Charcoal in the cut** — *"In the deepest groove of the cut stone at the Widow's Tooth, dry under the overhang, a crumb of black charcoal came away on your thumb. Somebody pressed paper to this stone not many days ago."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 3: *"Every raised line sits under its line of charcoal, and there is charcoal still, ground into the edges of the stone."*

- **Road B — `FOLLOW THE CART-RUTS`:**
  > The ruts turn off the road toward a line of trees, and a thread of smoke beyond them. **NEW:** Where the left wheel sank deepest in the peat, a twist of grey reed-thatch has shaken loose from the cart-bed. You are looking that way, so you see him first: a man lying flat in the bracken, watching the rock.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You found reed-thatch in the ruts · You spotted him in the bracken: you strike first.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Reed-thatch in the ruts** — *"Where the cart-ruts turned off toward the trees and the smoke, a twist of grey reed-thatch had shaken loose from the cart-bed into the peat."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 4: *"Fenmark sits low at the edge of the fen: reed thatch and peat smoke."*

## Fork 2 · Day 4 (Fenmark, at the well) — Fit (Picks Challenge Attribute)

**Why it needed fixing:** This fork sits directly before the `FIND WHO LEFT IT` Challenge (**Agility** · *follow the fresh prints round the back lanes* vs **Charm** · *coax the neighbour at her half-open door into pointing*), so per the rule, the road you take here chooses which of the Challenge's two Attributes you roll with, and each road leaves its own clue.

`[LOOK FOR FOOTPRINTS]` `[KNOCK NEXT DOOR]`

- **Road A — `LOOK FOR FOOTPRINTS` (Agility fit):**
  > You crouch by the bucket. The mud there has been trodden by every boot in Fenmark, but over the top of it all runs a line of small prints, quick and light, too short in the stride for any grown man. **NEW:** Where they turn between the sheds, a thread of unhemmed grey wool has caught on a nail at the height of a boy's elbow. They go off between the sheds behind the lane, and there you lose them.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You took the back lanes (Agility roll on the Challenge) and found grey wool on the shed nail.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Grey wool on the nail** — *"Where the small footprints turned between the sheds behind the well, a thread of unhemmed grey wool was caught on a nail at the height of a boy's elbow."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 4: *"a boy of about fourteen edges out of the gap behind it, all elbows and held breath."*

- **Road B — `KNOCK NEXT DOOR` (Charm fit):**
  > The door beside the well opens the width of a hand before your knuckles have finished on it. An eye, a grey braid, a thin hand on the latch. **NEW:** Behind her in the dim kitchen, a pot of the same boiled barley is still steaming on the hob — she is the one who filled the bowl by the well. She does not speak, and she does not shut it. Her gaze goes once, quickly, down the lane and back to you.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You knocked next door (Charm roll on the Challenge) and saw the neighbour's barley pot.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The second barley pot** — *"Inside the neighbour's half-open door beside the well, a pot of the same boiled barley was still steaming on the hob. Someone in Fenmark was feeding the boy on the quiet."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 4: *"Behind the well, on an upturned bucket: a bowl of barley, half eaten, the spoon still standing in it... Every shutter on the street is closed but one."*

## Fork 3 · Day 4 (Opening the boy at the well) — Trade

**Why it needed fixing:** All three buttons (`SHOW HIM THE LIST`, `ASK WHO WAS TAKEN`, `SAY NOTHING. WAIT.`) immediately converged on `"For a while he says nothing at all"` with no distinct information gained from how you opened him.

`[SHOW HIM THE LIST]` `[ASK WHO WAS TAKEN]` `[SAY NOTHING. WAIT.]`

- **Road A — `SHOW HIM THE LIST` (trade anonymity for recognition):**
  > You unfold Meira's list and hold it where he can see. He reads it slowly, his lips moving, then looks up from the names to your faces, one after another, as if to make sure you are the people written there. Something in his shoulders lets go.
  >
  > **NEW:** *"She was here,"* he whispers, touching the corner of the leaf with one dirty finger. *"The woman with the satchel. Two weeks back. She asked about the split stone, and she had a book of that same paper."*
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You showed him Meira's list — he recognized her paper from two weeks ago.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The woman with the satchel** — *"The boy at the well recognized the paper of Meira's list. He says a woman with a satchel and a book of that same paper came through Fenmark two weeks back, asking about the split stone."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 2 & Day 3 (Meira has been missing three weeks and made her rubbing at the Widow's Tooth).

- **Road B — `ASK WHO WAS TAKEN` (name the thing the village won't):**
  > You ask him straight out who they took. His chin comes up at that. For three days Fenmark has told him only to keep quiet, and nobody has asked him anything at all.
  >
  > **NEW:** *"Her mother's shut inside with the shutters barred,"* he says, his voice tight. *"They told her if she cried out or went to the road, they'd never bring her back."*
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You asked straight out who was taken — you learned how they silenced Tamsin's mother.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **How they kept Fenmark quiet** — *"The boy at the well says Tamsin's mother is shut behind barred shutters: the men who took Tamsin warned that if anyone cried out or went to the road, they would never bring her back."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 4: *"He is not hiding what happened here. He is terrified of what happens to this village if it is known that somebody talked."*

- **Road C — `SAY NOTHING. WAIT.` (give him quiet to show where his thoughts are):**
  > You say nothing. You lean on the cold lip of the well and let the quiet be, as if you had the whole afternoon to spend on it. The boy watches you do it. He had braced himself for questions.
  >
  > **NEW:** Because you do not press him, his eyes go where his thoughts are: down the lane to the swinging gate, and then to the third house on the left, where a child's stool still sits under an open shutter.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You waited in silence — his gaze gave away Tamsin's house and the gate they used.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Where the boy looked** — *"Given silence at the well, the boy looked first to the third house on the left — where a child's stool sits under the open shutter — and then to the swinging gate at the fen end of the lane."*
  - **Canon backing:** Episode 1 Day 4 lead 1-4-2 (*The covered cup*: *"Under the window, a child's stool is set square to the wall"*) and lead 1-4-3 (*Past three doors*).

## Fork 4 · Day 4 (The village standoff) — Who You Are

**Why it needed fixing:** A pure stance choice (`STAND WHERE YOU ARE`, `STEP BACK, HANDS OPEN`, `PUT YOURSELF IN FRONT OF THE BOY`) that previously changed only two sentences of posture. Now each stance reveals a different truth about the men in the lane.

`[STAND WHERE YOU ARE]` `[STEP BACK, HANDS OPEN]` `[PUT YOURSELF IN FRONT OF THE BOY]`

- **Road A — `STAND WHERE YOU ARE`:**
  > You stay exactly where you are, your feet set on the wet cobbles. The two younger men had expected you to give ground. They glance at the elder, and do not quite know what to do with their hands. **NEW:** Close to, you see that neither of them carries so much as a hedging bill at his belt — they came out with bare hands, hoping numbers alone would turn you from the lane.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You held your ground — you saw the young men came out unarmed, bluffing to turn you back.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Unarmed men** — *"When you stood your ground at the well, the two younger men had nothing at their belts — not even a hedging bill. Fenmark came out to bluff you away, not to fight."*

- **Road B — `STEP BACK, HANDS OPEN`:**
  > You take one slow step back and let them see your empty hands. The nearer of the two young men lets out a breath, and some of the stiffness goes out of the street. **NEW:** As his shoulders drop, his cuff rides up: there is a dark bruise round his wrist, three days old, where a hand gripped him hard when he tried to come out of his door that night.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You stepped back with open hands — you saw the three-day bruise on the young man's wrist.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The bruised wrist** — *"When the nearer young man lowered his guard, his cuff showed a dark bruise round his wrist, three days old — someone held him back when Tamsin was taken."*

- **Road C — `PUT YOURSELF IN FRONT OF THE BOY`:**
  > You step across, between the men and the boy. It is a small movement, and every eye in the lane follows it. The elder's face changes. For a moment he looks less like a man guarding his village than a man ashamed of it. **NEW:** Behind your shoulder, the boy breathes one thing more before the elder reaches you: *"They didn't bring a lantern. They knew where the step was."*
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You stood in front of the boy — he whispered that the men knew the steps of Fenmark in the dark.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **No lantern in the dark** — *"From behind your shoulder in the standoff, the boy whispered one detail more: the men who came in the dark carried no lantern, yet knew where every step in the lane was."*

## Fork 5 · Day 5 (The old charcoal camp, arrival) — Trade + Fight Stake

**Why it needed fixing:** Day 4's Pull already warns the reader: *"He has half a day on you, and he is heading for the only other person alive who has seen that knife."* Arriving at the dark shed on Day 5, the setup now makes the trade explicit: you can inspect the shed itself (`LOOK AT THE STEP`, `LOOK AT THE FIRE`) to see who lives here, or check the approach (`LOOK AT THE PATH`) for the man who came ahead of you.

**Setup passage:**
> You reach it after dark. At the edge of the clearing stands a shed, with no light in it. You knock. Nobody answers. **NEW:** If the runner from Fenmark came this way, he had half a day on you; the step and the firepit are right at your feet, while the path in runs back toward the dark trees.

`[LOOK AT THE STEP]` `[LOOK AT THE FIRE]` `[LOOK AT THE PATH]`

- **Road A — `LOOK AT THE STEP`:**
  > Swept this morning. Somebody lives here, and is proud enough to sweep. **NEW:** Tucked under the threshold stone is a narrow line of dry salt and grey rowan-ash — an old hill-country ward laid down by someone who expects a visitor back. You are still crouched by the door when a blade works into the shutter behind the shed.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You found her rowan-ash ward at the step · You were at the front door: the runner strikes first.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Salt and rowan-ash** — *"Under the swept threshold stone of the charcoal-burner's shed lay a line of dry salt and rowan-ash: an old hill ward laid by someone waiting for a bad visitor to return."*

- **Road B — `LOOK AT THE FIRE`:**
  > Cold on top. Warm underneath, when you push a hand into the ash. Banked, not dead. Somebody means to come back to it. **NEW:** Set on the flat stone beside the pit is a tin cup of herb-tea with the steam barely off it — she slipped into the trees only minutes before your boots came up the road. You are still kneeling by the ash when a blade works into the shutter behind the shed.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You found her warm cup by the banked fire · You were at the firepit: the runner strikes first.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The warm pannikin** — *"Beside the banked firepit stood a tin cup of herb-tea barely cool. The woman of the camp slipped into the trees only minutes before you reached the clearing."*

- **Road C — `LOOK AT THE PATH`:**
  > Two sets of prints on it, both fresh. A woman's, going out. And over them a man's, coming in, at a run. **NEW:** His stride is long and low, and the mud at the turn is still oozing water into the heel-mark.
  >
  > He is here.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You read the fresh prints on the path · You knew he was here: you strike first.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **Prints at a run** — *"On the path into the charcoal camp, a man's running prints overlay the woman's outgoing ones, the mud still oozing water into the heel-marks. He reached the clearing just ahead of you."*

## Fork 6 · Day 5 (The woman with the woodcutter's axe) — Trade + Week 1's Discernment Relic

**Why it needed fixing:** Previously `[SHOW HER YOUR EMPTY HANDS]` and `[TELL HER WHO YOU JUST DROVE OFF]` shared the exact same two-sentence branch, and `[HOLD OUT THE CARVED HORSE]` only shortened the line to *"The axe comes down all at once."* Yet `[HOLD OUT THE CARVED HORSE]` is the clearest discernment test in Week 1: a careful reader who remembers finding the child's carved wooden horse at the Widow's Tooth (Day 3) and the elder's whisper about the woman two hours east who saw the curved knife before (Day 4) knows before she speaks that the carved horse belongs to the child she lost.

`[SHOW HER YOUR EMPTY HANDS]` `[TELL HER WHO YOU JUST DROVE OFF]` `[HOLD OUT THE CARVED HORSE]`

- **Road A — `SHOW HER YOUR EMPTY HANDS`:**
  > The axe comes down by inches. **NEW:** As she lowers it, the starlight catches a fresh white gouge in the ash-wood helve — he came at her among the trees before he ever tried her shutter, and she turned his blade with the haft. Then her eyes go to the carved horse in your other hand, and stop.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You showed empty hands — you saw the fresh blade-nick on her axe-helve from the trees.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The nicked axe-helve** — *"When the woman at the charcoal camp lowered her axe, the haft showed a fresh white gouge where she had turned a knife-blow in the trees before you arrived."*

- **Road B — `TELL HER WHO YOU JUST DROVE OFF`:**
  > The axe comes down by inches. **NEW:** At the word of the man at her shutter, her eyes flick once, sharply, to the flat root-cellar stone behind the woodpile before coming back to your face — whatever she keeps hidden here, it is under that stone and not in the shed. Then her eyes go to the carved horse in your other hand, and stop.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You told her who you drove off — her glance gave away the root-cellar stone behind the woodpile.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The root-cellar stone** — *"When told a man had been prizing at her back shutter, the woman glanced once at the flat root-cellar stone behind the woodpile — her real hiding-place is under the woodpile, not inside the shed."*

- **Road C — `HOLD OUT THE CARVED HORSE` (Discernment reward: +1 Might Relic):**
  > The axe comes down all at once. **NEW:** Before she even asks your names, her fingers go to her belt, unfasten a small river-stone charm bound in iron wire — kept since the winter the boy was taken — and press it into your palm as she takes the little horse from you.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You knew the carved horse was hers and held it out first — she trusted you at once (+1 Might Relic).`
  - **Reward:** **+1 Might Relic** (added to `member_progress.relics` via `pick_choice`; Gathering 1 calls on **Might**).
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The carver's charm** — *"Held out without a word, the carved horse from the Widow's Tooth brought her axe down at once. She pressed an iron-bound river-stone charm into your hand before she sat."*

## Fork 7 · Day 6 (The roadside inn, reading the map) — Trade / Who You Are

**Why it needed fixing:** Over supper in a crowded, smoky roadside inn of drovers and dogs, the party has one assembled map and must get the way by heart. The next paragraph in canon states: *"Across the room a courier who came in for a bed settles his bill without taking one. He is gone within the hour."* Choosing whether to read the map out yourself (`READ IT OUT`) or slide it to Dev (`LET DEV READ IT`) is a natural trade between studying the parchment closely with your own eyes and keeping your eyes on the crowded taproom while your friend reads.

`[READ IT OUT]` `[LET DEV READ IT]`

- **Road A — `READ IT OUT`:**
  > You smooth the map flat between the cups and read it yourself, tracing each line with a fingertip so the others can follow it, from the ford all the way to the mark. **NEW:** Right at the mark, your fingertip catches on a tiny pin-hole punched clean through the old vellum — somebody once pinned this very spot to a board before the map was cut in six.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You read the map out yourself — your fingertip found an old pin-hole punched through the mark.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The pin-hole at the mark** — *"Tracing the assembled map by fingertip over supper, you felt a tiny pin-hole punched right through the mark beyond the ford, from before the map was ever cut in six."*

- **Road B — `LET DEV READ IT`:**
  > You turn the map round and slide it across to Dev, who reads it the way some people read a letter from home: slowly, frowning a little, as if the words might change if they were hurried. **NEW:** Because your own eyes are not on the parchment, you catch a man in a riding-cloak by the chimney stopping with his mug halfway to his mouth when Dev names the bend above the ford.
  - **⚡ WHAT THIS CHANGED:** `You let Dev read the map — with your eyes on the room, you caught the courier listening at the mention of the ford.`
  - **Road find (for the fire):** **The listening courier** — *"While your friend read the map aloud in the roadside inn, a man in a riding-cloak by the chimney held his mug still at the mention of the bend above the ford, then paid his bill without taking a bed."*
