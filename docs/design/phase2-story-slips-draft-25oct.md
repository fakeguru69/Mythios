## A. Clear slips (the text contradicts itself or the page)

**1. Week 1 Day 5: "empty hands", with a horse in them.** Both of the first two options end with the same line.

*Now:* "…Then her eyes go to the carved horse in your other hand, and stop."

*Proposed:* "…Then her eyes go past you to the carved horse at your belt, and stop."

**2. Week 1 Day 5: she has not told you about the boy yet.**

*Now (the horse option):* "…a small river-stone charm bound in iron wire — kept since the winter the boy was taken — and press it into your palm…"

*Proposed:* "…a small river-stone charm bound in iron wire, worn smooth with keeping, and press it into your palm…"

**3. Week 1 Day 5: the asides in dashes in the same three passages.** Make them sentences, with the same facts.

*Now:* "As she lowers it, the starlight catches a fresh white gouge in the ash-wood helve — he came at her among the trees before he ever tried her shutter, and she turned his blade with the haft."

*Proposed:* "As she lowers it, the starlight catches a fresh white gouge in the ash-wood helve. He came at her among the trees before he ever tried her shutter, and she turned his blade with the haft."

The second passage gets the same treatment: "…before coming back to your face. Whatever she keeps hidden here, it is under that stone, and not in the shed."

**4. Week 1 Day 6: the river is rising, but the fragment is "only seen in a low year".**

*Now:* "Under the waterline, at the foot of the ford-stone, where you would only see it in a low year:"

*Proposed:* "Under the waterline, at the foot of the ford-stone, where the current has scoured the rock bare:"

**5. Week 1 Day 3: the charcoal is "not many days" old, but Meira has been gone three weeks.** The rubbing is hers, as the fire's click on that night says.

*Now:* "Somebody pressed paper to this stone not many days ago." This line appears in the chapter and in the road find.

*Proposed:* "Somebody pressed paper to this stone, and not so very long ago."

**6. Gathering 1: "Tamsin is about nine", though she is nine.**

*Now:* "You have been saying her name for three days. Now you can see her. Tamsin is about nine."

*Proposed:* "You have been saying her name for three days. Now here she is: nine years old, small for it, and still counting under her breath."

**7. Gathering 2: one line reads modern and explaining.**

*Now:* "The boy is alive. There's a cut across his palm, clean and shallow, already bound in white linen — someone took the time to bind it. Whatever they needed from him, they'd already taken. The wound is small. The relief in the room is real. It looks, to everyone standing in it, like a win."

*Proposed:* "The boy is alive. There is a cut across his palm, clean and shallow, already bound in white linen. Somebody took the time. Whatever they needed from him, they had already taken. The wound is small, and the relief in the room is real. For a while, in the lamplight, it is enough."

The meaning is unchanged; the `[PLANT]` gap between "arrived" and "arrived in time" survives.

**8. Gathering 2, a United Effort fail line: "Wrong." blames the friend who rolled.** The rule is that the place does the harm.

*Now:* "Wrong. The roof over the hole stays where it is. The roof over your heads lets go a barrow-load of stone."

*Proposed:* "The roof over the hole stays where it is. The roof over your heads lets go a barrow-load of stone."

**9. Gathering 6: the linen was on palms, not wrists.**

*Now:* "…the same white linen from a week ago still knotted at every wrist."

*Proposed:* "…the same white linen from a week ago still knotted across every palm."

## B. My own mistake (I will fix it with the rest)

**10. Gathering 2, "the six, read together".** I wrote "On the dark hillside, before anything else is opened, the week's six are read aloud…". If the party saved the room, they are still in the room. It becomes: "Before anything else is opened, the week's six are read aloud…", the same as Gatherings 3 and 5.

## C. Small additions the reviewers asked for

**11. The dark moon is an eclipse.** All season "the dark moon" reads like the monthly moonless night. In Week 6 it is a full moon going dark. One plain line where the full moon is seen, on Week 6 Day 6:

*Now:* "…Tomorrow night it goes dark."

*Proposed:* "…Tomorrow night it goes dark: a full moon, eaten, the way the sky-watchers wrote it."

**12. Two friends: one recap line counts more friends than there are.** In a party of two, Week 1 Day 2's recap reads "the rest of the list was waiting, each of them holding a torn page".

*Proposed, for a party of two only:* "At the Hollow Wake Inn the other name on the list was waiting, holding a torn page in the same neat hand."

Larger parties keep the line as it is. This is the only recap line that miscounts; I checked them all. The check that keeps the short versions honest will cover the recaps too.

## D. Garrick at the fire (stored lines; his rules are kept)

**13. Week 2 Day 5: game words in his mouth.**

*Now:* "On Day 2 a horn button, on Day 3 a dead stranger. Tonight, a circle."

*Proposed:* "At the mill a horn button, on the river path a dead stranger. Tonight, a circle."

**14. Two flat openers.**

- *Week 3 Day 3, now:* "Mind your hands on that glass, all of you."
  *Proposed:* "Sit, before that glass has the rest of your boots. I've thread for one more pair, and no more."
- *Week 4 Day 2, now:* "Cold road. Put your hands round that cup."
  *Proposed:* "Cold road, and a colder lake ahead of it. Hands round that cup first. The rest will keep."

All of these are true, warm and dry. None promises, predicts or hints at what he is.

Canon keeps it on purpose: Gathering 1's notes say Gathering 4 "turns it over". I would keep it. If you want it sharper, one reviewer offered: "**You were never the quarry. You were the key.**"
