# Week 5, The King's Stone: the storybook reading copy, first pass

*Not yet approved. Waiting on Fake Guru's choices in "Things I was unsure of" at the end.*

*A rewrite of the player-facing text of `docs/canon/episode-5-the-kings-stone.md`, Days 1 to 7, in the storybook voice of `docs/style/storybook-voice.md`, matched to the approved `docs/storybook/week-1-draft.md`, `week-2-draft.md` and `week-3-draft.md`, and to the Week 4 draft it follows on from. 16 Oct 2026. Nothing else in the repo has been changed.*

**How to read this.** Quote blocks are what MYTHIOS says. `[BUTTONS]` are what the player taps. Branch labels are in plain italics. Lines in italics outside the quote blocks are stage notes for the build, not player text. Cards, notice lines, fight cards and summary cards are kept as canon has them, except where a "What changed" note says otherwise. Mei, Dev and Jo stand in for the real friends. They act, and never speak.

**This pass, in short.** Week 5 is the best-written week of the weekdays, so this is a light refinement. The audits' real catches are fixed: the squared block on Day 2 that only Craft players had been told about, the "It" in Day 2's Pull, the "they" on Day 3 that could mean either army, the stiff Day 1 failure line, "Her hand drops" on Day 6 when the lookout may be a man, Gathering 5's sentence printed twice and its "every night of his life" said three times, the arrival's two hills that were hard to picture together, the flat "actually", the tally that reads as if it should drop to six, and the stage brackets in the Verse and on the watch-page. I also caught slips of my own finding. On Day 1 the card says "take a rubbing, as you did on the forest road", but on the forest road the party found Meira's rubbing; it did not take one. On Day 2 the block's lid lifts one line before the `[LIFT IT]` button. On Day 3 the grey thread of smoke is "that grey thread" to players who never saw the Aim line, and the same happens with the straight crack on Day 6. On Day 6 the lookout never crosses the wall, yet the map passes through every friend's hands. "This season" appears twice. Gathering 5's lamplight comes from nowhere, its dead "come up the street" while the watchmen come down, and its cliffhanger sees the moon "overhead" from inside a walled room. Each day now has a time of day and a close. Garrick has no new lines. He gets two small kind actions.

## Day 2. The old kingdom's road

*Heading: **The old kingdom's road.***
*Question: what is the heartbeat doing to the country?*

> You leave Reedham in the grey morning, and by noon the lake is gone behind you.
>
> The road was built to carry armies. It still runs straight as a spear across the empty country, but grass has grown up through its stones, and nobody has mended it in longer than anyone can say. Larks should be up over country like this. None are.
>
> Every mile has its stone, and every stone a number, one less than the last.

> Late in the afternoon the road passes between two long, low rows of grassy mounds, one on each side, running away across the fields in perfect lines.

> Drawn up at the head of the rows are the only people you have met all day. A family, come down from the north, with all they own on one cart, and a cow tied behind.

## Day 3. The herald's gate

*Heading: **The herald's gate.***
*Question: how do you get in, and where in a whole dead city do you start?*

## Week 5 daily recaps

*One per day, for a friend who missed the day or wants the story so far. Plain past-tense sentences, one idea to a sentence, about 100 to 125 words, at most five sentences, each ending on the day's Pull; the Gathering about 210. Each tells only what every branch shares, except where item 11 says otherwise. Friends are never named. Gates: "Highwall" is allowed from Day 1. "The Order", "king", "fifth", "moon-blood" and "heartbeat" are long past their gates. No "Malakor" anywhere. The Highwall tally: eight left on Day 5, seven at the end of Gathering 5.*

**Day 1. The grey afternoon**
> The dawn after the undercroft never finished, and the heartbeat from the north went on under a thin grey sky. You rowed Joss home, and his grandmother carried him up the shingle in her arms, so every child you were sent to find was home. Meira's records only ever counted four Stones, but the torn page spoke of a fifth. On the old waystone at the head of the north road, a line had been cut away with a patient knife, the same work as the king's face scraped off the carter's coin. “Every telling I know ends at Highwall,” Garrick said. What is left in that dead old capital, two days north up the old kingdom's road, where nothing grows and nobody goes?

**Day 2. The old kingdom's road**
> You walked north on the old kingdom's road, an army road gone to grass, with a milestone counting down every mile. A family fleeing south told you their beasts would not settle since the sky turned. Beside the road ran rows of soldiers' graves from the old war, and one new grave, dug badly this week. At dusk the heartbeat came, the grass on the graves shivered with it, and the farm dogs came out of the dark at you until the beat stopped and they wandered away. The heartbeat is nearer every dusk, and it is coming from inside Highwall, which you will reach by noon.

**Day 3. The herald's gate**
> Highwall rose out of the grey noon whole and pale, with nothing green between its stones, no birds, and a great hall shut on the hill at its top. Its gate was shut, so you climbed the very wall the Seekers held on the war's last night, and found a fragment set into their parapet. From above you saw that the gate was barred on the inside with new, pale timber. The iron brackets it lay in were worn bright by more bars than anyone could count, and far up the hill one thread of smoke rose from a fire below the hall. Who bars a dead city from the inside, year after year, and who waits down in its streets?

**Day 4. The dead streets**
> In the streets of Highwall you came on a dozen of the king's soldiers in armour gone green and black, standing mid-stride where they stopped on the night he fell. You got past them, and when you looked back from the end of their street, the nearest helm was turned a little further toward you than before. That night, in an empty house with no fire, Garrick poured one more cup than there were people and set it at the window facing the hall. On your watch he said, “My family's waited a long time for something. I'd like to live to see it.” Who keeps the smoke rising from the square below the hall?

**Day 5. Below the hall**
> At the top of the city you reached a square below the hall, and the heartbeat came from behind its shut doors. Across the square, hooded figures of the Order were camped facing those doors, and some of them had kept that camp all their lives. They were not trying to get in, and as the light went one of them crossed out one more stroke on a chalk board, leaving eight. A fragment in the dry fountain could be read in part, “…where we could watch it, and watch him.” They are counting down. To what? You hold five pieces of the map, and the trail began on the wall.

**Day 6. The wall again**
> You went the long way round the street of soldiers, back to the herald's gate, and posted a lookout on the gate-tower. While the Order's watch changed in the square, “Keep the dark” answered by “It keeps us”, you crossed forty paces of open wall-walk unheard. The sixth fragment lay in the walkway, and the map went from hand to hand without a word. It pointed into the wall itself, to a hidden room a stone's throw away with one slit of a window on the hall, where the Seekers kept the fifth and watched him all their lives. The Order has never found it. Into the wall, tonight.

**Gathering 5. The wall at Highwall**
> That night the Order's watchmen saw your lamps at the foot of the wall and came down the hill. The king's soldiers came with them in their green-black armour, walking at last. When it was over, one watchman lay dying with a page in his fist. It told how the Seekers laid the king down. They did not kill him. They turned his own gift back on him and laid on him the Undying Sleep, and the soldiers nearest him stood on to keep his watch. Of the fifth Stone, the older hand said only, “The fifth drinks deep of moon-blood, and of no other.” In the hidden room you found the fifth, the first Stone you have ever held. Your blood, which had woken the other four, did nothing to it. Garrick bound your cut palms in the same white linen you have seen on every child you saved. The Seekers' last fragment gave the reason, “Where he fell, under the dark moon, the five were joined.” Not here, and not yet. The watchman's second page counted the nights to that dark moon, and seven were left. Garrick looked at the half moon and said, “We're early.” Then a fresh watchman struck the board late, and looked straight down the hill at your window.
