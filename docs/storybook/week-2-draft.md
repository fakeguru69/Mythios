# Week 2, The Wind Stone: the storybook reading copy, first pass

*A rewrite of the player-facing text of `docs/canon/episode-2-the-wind-stone.md`, Days 1 to 7, in the storybook voice of `docs/style/storybook-voice.md`, matched to the approved second pass of `docs/storybook/week-1-draft.md`. 16 Oct 2026. Nothing in the repo has been changed.*

**How to read this.** Quote blocks are what MYTHIOS says. `[BUTTONS]` are what the player taps. Branch labels are in plain italics. Lines in italics outside the quote blocks are stage notes for the build, not player text. Cards, notice lines, fight cards and summary cards are kept as canon has them, except where a "What changed" note says otherwise. Mei, Dev and Jo stand in for the real friends. They act, and never speak.

**This pass, in short.** Week 2 was already lean and good, so this is refinement, not reinvention. Each day gets a sense of place where canon had a bare line: the empty country on the mill road, the turning wheel heard before it is seen, the river up and loud after the rain, the climb to the shaft, a second cold night on the hill. The 16 Oct audit's real catches are fixed: the passive carter, the menu-like "three things here", the repeated "She has been there for some time", the fragment "housings" that no earlier fragment came from, the "So many at the lamps" line that read like a missing number, and the friends on watch who need a reason to hear the map read aloud. I added one simile in the whole week. Garrick gets four small kind actions and no new lines. At the Gathering, the black book gets its own moment, the scripture is kept word for word with its bracket turned into narration, and "You're marked too. Every one of you." is now set up as the answer to Week 1's last question.

## Day 4. What the hillside hides

*Heading: **The end of the path.***
*Question: what is at the end of the hidden path?*

> The path climbs for an hour through bracken and scree. Up here the wind has an edge to it, and it brings you lamp-smoke before it brings you anything else.
>

>

**What you notice**
> **Might** · The ladder is new ash, pegged, and strong enough for men carrying a load. *They mean to use this place for a long time.*
> **Agility** · The third rung from the top is worn pale on the left. *Everyone who climbs this steps in the same place.*
> **Intellect** · Lamp-smoke, coming up steadily, and no cooking in it. *Nobody lives down there. They work there.*
> **Charm** · The voices are not arguing, and they are not laughing. *They are saying the same thing, together.*
> **Craft** · The shaft is old. The timbering is this year's. *Somebody found it, and opened it again.*
> **Aim** · Straight down: a lamp, a patch of floor, and the edge of something that is not floor. *A foot. Small, and bare.*

## Week 2 daily recaps

*One per day, for a friend who missed the day or wants the story so far. Each tells only what every branch shares, keeps the day's clue lines plainly, and ends on the Pull. Friends are never named. Gates are kept for the day each recap covers: no "king" before Day 5, and in the Gathering recap no "the Order", no "Highwall" and no "Malakor".*

**Day 1. The mill road**
> Tamsin goes home to Fenmark on a carter's wagon, but first she tells you what she heard while she was held: one of them said, "The mill boy next." The second name on the mine wall is Corin, and Meira's notes place a family at a working mill, two days south-east; Garrick, who agreed to come only as far as the Widow's Tooth, shoulders a pack and comes too. At the last farmhouse before the mill, an old man tells you a cart with no lamps went up to the mill three nights ago, came back before dawn and turned upriver: the same people who took Tamsin. The mill is an hour on. At first light: what did the family see?

**Day 2. The mill**
> The mill wheel is still turning, and the old stone under it carries the shape from the burned rubbing. The miller's wife says a big, quiet man took her boy Corin four nights ago and never hid his face. He asked for Corin by name, and he knew the night the boy was born better than she did; she gives you a horn button, with a twist of dark thread through it, torn from his sleeve. They are not taking children; they are collecting particular ones. The cart's ruts are four nights old and go upriver, and there is weather coming.

**Day 3. Upriver**
> After a night of rain, the ruts lead upriver until the bank turns to stone, and a hidden path climbs into the hillside. A great dog on a new chain guards the path where it narrows, and you get past it. Beyond it lies a stranger, dead a week, his lockpick still jammed in an old stone lock built into the hill, a lock that would not open for him and opens at once for you, with a cold fragment inside. Somebody on the slope above is watching. The path goes on past the dead man, and up.

**Day 4. The shaft**
> The path ends at a timbered shaft, with a new ladder going down into lamplight and voices below saying the same thing together. Corin is down there, alive, held in a chamber cut into the hill by hooded figures who are making something ready. Then a shout goes up, you are driven back from the shaft, and they haul the ladder away: they know you are here now. Is there another way into this hill before they finish? You have tonight to find it.

**Day 5. The hillside**
> There is no second door on the hillside, and Meira's margin says why the old fragments never name the man they speak of: every record of him was scraped clean, because someone wanted him forgotten. A hooded figure comes up over the hill alone, fights without a word, tears free and runs, leaving a strip of his coat with a small black circle sewn to it. At its cuff is a snapped dark thread, the same twist as the thread through the horn button from the mill. Before the dark takes him he opens a lamp twice toward the shaft. Now there is a clock: last week six cold slivers showed you a way nobody was watching, and you have five.

**Day 6. The other way in**
> On your second night at the lip of the shaft, the chanting starts again and the light below says they are nearly ready. The sixth fragment turns up among the roots, not hidden at all, and the six make a map again: an older room deeper in the same hill, with a shaft of its own. Your party is strung out along the hillside on watch, so the way is read aloud, twice, across the dark, so that everybody has it. Two hundred paces along, under a fall of old stone, is its mouth, real and unguarded; within the hour the hill wakes, and far down in the rock you hear picks. They are digging toward the old room: be in it first.

**Gathering 2. Under the mill hill**
> Down the old shaft and a stair worn soft with age, you reach the oldest room under the mill hill, where robed figures are chanting round a stone table with Corin on it. You win the fight, and Corin is alive, but his palm is already cut and bound in white linen: whatever they needed from him, they had already taken. Behind the table is another socket, cut with the same care and empty, the dust at its rim barely disturbed, and the burned rubbing fits it. Beside the table lies their scripture, a small black book with a black circle on its first page, the same circle as the scrap from the scout's coat. Its verse tells of a war that ate the world, and of one king on the last wall who would not kneel. On the night the moon went dark he gave his own blood to a thing his house had kept, and by morning the enemy was gone. The world he saved took back his name, and the war every child is taught ends in a fever and in luck, with no king in it at all. His faithful remember him and give the marked blood in his name, until the throne rise whole. In a niche by a lamp is a ledger: page after page of children's names, places and dates, and beside many of the old names one word, *given*. Its newest page holds the four names from the mine wall: Tamsin, Corin, and two children you have not found. The twist is that those names were never a list of victims: they are offerings, written down for years, and the marked are those born on a night the moon went dark, as Corin was. Meira wrote your own birth nights beside your names, and they are dark-moon nights too: you are marked, every one of you. Garrick reads the ledger and tucks it inside his coat, saying, "This hand's been keeping books like this for years. This isn't new, and it isn't finished. Two places. Two children." Then Corin tells you there was a girl held down here with him, taken away not long before you came. She said they told her she was going somewhere warm.
