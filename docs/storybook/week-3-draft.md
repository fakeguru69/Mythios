## Day 2. Ashcombe

*Heading: **Ashcombe.***
*Question: did Wren come home?*

> On the second morning the west road climbs, and the grass along it grows thin and brown. Somewhere on the way the wind begins to smell of old smoke, though nothing is burning anywhere you can see.
>
> Ashcombe sits where the green hills give out and the black ones begin: low stone houses, roofs weighted with slabs against the wind. The ground is warm underfoot, like a hearthstone the morning after. The springs run hot, and steam lifts off the trough by the first house.
>
> Every door in the village shuts as you come up the street. Strangers took a child from here two weeks ago. You are strangers.
>

**What you notice**
> **Might** · Every roof here is held down with stones a man could barely lift. *These are people used to holding on to things.*
> **Agility** · A curtain moves in every house but one. *In that one, somebody is standing right behind the door.*
> **Intellect** · Two weeks since a child was taken, and nobody is out searching. *They were told something that made searching seem useless.*
> **Charm** · The last door to shut, shut slowest. *She wanted a longer look at you.*
> **Craft** · The well-rope is new, and the bucket is old. *Somebody here still mends things. This is not a village that has given up.*

## Day 3. The Blackglass

*Heading: **The Blackglass.***
*Question: why the Blackglass? What is up there?*

> Half a day up the black road, where the shepherds take the left fork, the hill opens out and you see it.
>
> The Blackglass is a whole shoulder of hillside where the rock once ran like wax, and cooled: smooth, black, faintly rippled, as if a wave of stone had frozen halfway down the slope.
>
> Nothing grows on it. The wind slides off it without a sound. The shepherds do not walk their flocks across it, and do not say why.
>
> Meira's page points to its far edge.

**What you notice**
> **Might** · The glass is warm through the soles of your boots. *The heat is coming from underneath.*
> **Agility** · It is rippled, like sand under shallow water. *The ripples all run one way. There is a grain to it.*
> **Intellect** · No lichen. No moss. Not one seed. *Whatever burned this did more than burn it.*
> **Charm** · The shepherds never told you not to cross. *They only stopped talking when you asked.*
> **Craft** · Glass like this takes the heat of a furnace. *A whole hillside of it, all at once.*
> **Aim** · On all that smooth black there is one mark that is not a ripple. *On the far side, just above the crack.*

## Day 4. The black ridges

*Heading: **The black ridges.***
*Question: is she still alive?*

> The burned country is ridge after ridge of black rock, under a sky that looks paler for it. The soil between the ridges is ash-grey, and crunches like old snow. Dead trees stand bone-white, still upright after nobody knows how long.
>
> No bird sings out here. By noon your own footsteps are the loudest thing for miles.
>
> You are walking point when you stop, and crouch.
>
> In one of the hoof-scratches lies a chip of black glass, worn smooth by a thumb.

`[PICK IT UP]`

> You turn it over in your palm. It is no bigger than a fingernail: the kind of thing a child carries for luck.
>

>

**What you notice**
> **Might** · The dead trees are still standing. *Nothing here has rotted. Nothing has lived here to rot them.*

> **Charm** · The men who have her have not noticed a child's hand opening and closing. *She has made herself very easy to overlook.*

> **Aim** · A hundred yards off the riders' line there is a shadow that is too straight. *Nothing out here is straight unless somebody made it so.*

## Week 3 daily recaps

*One per day, for a friend who missed the day or wants the story so far, in the past tense of the Week 1 recaps in `src/data/dayRecaps.ts`. Each tells only what every branch shares, keeps the day's clue lines plainly, and ends on the Pull. Friends are never named. Gates are kept for the day each recap covers: "the Order" is allowed from Day 1; "five" first appears on Day 3, in Meira's count; no "heartbeat", "moon-blood", "Highwall" or "Malakor" anywhere.*

**Day 1. Somewhere warm**
> Corin remembered the girl who was held with him. Her name was Wren, her home was always warm, and they told her she was going home first. The ledger and Meira's list both named her village, Ashcombe, on the edge of the burned country, a long day west. Before dawn one of them came up out of the hill alone, fought you without a word and fled, and below the shaft a cart and a child's small prints turned west. You carried Corin home to the mill, and he told his mother every one of your names. Why would anyone take a stolen child back to where she was born?

**Day 2. Ashcombe**
> In Ashcombe, Wren's mother told you that two polite men in grey coats had taken her two weeks ago. They asked for her by name, and they knew the night she was born, a night the moon went out. One of them said they would only need her the once, and he looked her in the eye when he said it. The night before you came, a cart with no lamps carried Wren past her own door and up the black road. Garrick knew that road. “My family's been in these hills longer than the roads have.” Half a day up it lies the Blackglass, the place Meira marked. What does anyone need a child for, once?

**Day 3. The Blackglass**
> You crossed the Blackglass, a hillside where the rock once ran like wax, and split a boot on its far edge. Beside a shape you knew, an old stone housing opened at your touch. Tamsin, Corin and Wren were each taken from beside a place Meira marked, and you wrote it into her notebook: three children, three marks, one list. That evening you found her note in the margin beside the first fragment's “Five fell”: “Five, the fragment says. Every other record I can find says four. I cannot find a fifth anywhere, and I have looked.” By morning Garrick had mended your boot, and the riders had passed the mark without slowing. What are they riding to, out in the black ridges?

**Day 4. The black ridges**
> In the black ridges you found a chip of black glass in a hoof-scratch, and another fifty paces on. Wren is dropping them, and the men who have her have not noticed. A hundred yards off the riders' line, a buried doorway opened on a room cut from the ridge, where whoever wrote your fragments once slept. A bone die lay in one of its niches, and a fragment waited in a housing between them. At dusk you saw smoke from a hut to the north-east, and that night your own fire in the old doorway showed just as plainly. She is alive, and she is thinking. The hut, at first light.

**Day 5. The shepherd's hut**
> Five more of Wren's chips led you down to a shepherd's hut beside a spring. A grey coat left on watch came down at you, then fled over bare rock that takes no prints. The hut was empty. WREN was scratched fresh into the doorframe at a child's height, and cut long ago into the beam over her bed were the Order's words, *Until the throne rise whole.* They had been there within the hour and left at a run, because from that doorway they could see your fire. “They're always a step ahead of us,” Garrick said. Now it is a step and no more. The cairn on the highest ridge, before the light goes.

**Day 6. The cairn**
> You raced the light to the cairn on the highest ridge of the burned country, and found the sixth fragment wedged at its foot. The six made a map once more. It ended at a round black hill with a crack across its crown, which Garrick said the shepherds call the Kiln. The way was said aloud, twice, for the ones on the slope below: in through the crack, and down a way cut long ago to a room beneath the hill. Not an hour after full dark, a single lamp crossed the black ground, climbed the Kiln, and went in. Whatever happens under that hill has started. Into the Kiln, tonight.

**Gathering 3. Under the Kiln**
> You went down through the crack in the Kiln's crown into the warm dark, where a girl was crying, and glimpsed a robed figure climbing away up a vent with a small box held to his chest. You brought Wren down alive from a slope of loose black glass, her palm already cut and bound in white linen. Behind the stone table was the same swept, empty hollow, and the rubbing fitted it. The captive's sealed stone case sprang open at your touch. “Seeker blood,” he whispered. His scripture said the king never lied to his people, only kept silent about the price of his gift, and that the Seekers who wrote your fragments, his own soldiers, struck him down. He did not die. He sleeps where he fell, and the Seekers locked their ways with their own blood. “Your blood. Their blood. The same.” Their book says the faithful shall not lie, so his answers stood: the children are taken to wake the king, and the fourth child is already with them. The Order is not mourning a dead king. It is waking a sleeping one. “We're not finished with you,” the captive said, and every lamp went out. When one was lit again, his ropes lay cut clean.

## Things I was unsure of

1. **Timeline: when the party reaches Ashcombe.** Day 1 leaves the mill "by mid-morning", and Ashcombe is "a long day west". The boy saw the cart pass "last night", which fits only if the party arrives on the day after Day 1. The failure branch has you "lose the day's light waiting" and the mother come out "at dusk", so the party must arrive with daylight left. Walking a long day from late morning would put them at Ashcombe after dark on Day 1, so they must walk part of Day 1, camp, and arrive on Day 2 during the day. Nothing on the page says so, but nothing contradicts it either. I gave no arrival time and changed no canon time. If Fake Guru wants it explicit, one clause would do it at the top of Day 2, for example "On the second morning the west road climbs…".
2. **Timeline: the rest checks out.**
   - Gathering 2 ends at night. Day 1 runs from the grey before dawn at the shaft to mid-morning at the mill, and the cart's tracks are "two hours old at most".
   - Day 3: half a day up the black road, the crossing, the evening footnote and the morning boot.
   - Day 4: the ridges by day, smoke at dusk, the night in the Seekers' room.
   - Day 5: first light at the hut, which is why "within the hour" and "they saw your fire" work.
   - Day 6: the same day's afternoon and dusk, matching Day 5's Pull "before the light goes". The lamp goes in "not an hour after full dark".
   - Gathering 3 is that same night ("Into the Kiln, tonight"), and Week 4 Day 1 picks up in the dark, then brings Wren out "into a grey dawn". This all agrees.
   - The charcoal woman's nephew, taken "two winters back", and Wren, taken "two weeks ago", do not collide.
3. **Two wells.** Week 1 Day 4 had a boy behind the well at Fenmark who saw the night of the taking; Day 2 here has a boy at the well at Ashcombe who saw the cart. Both are canon.
