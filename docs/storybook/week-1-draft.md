## Day 1. The forest road

*Heading: **The old forest road.***
*Question: who is this man, and what does he want with me?*

> The old forest road has not been mended in a generation. >

>

>
> A man steps out onto the road with his blade already drawn. He does not ask for your purse. He does not say anything at all.

## Day 2. The Hollow Wake Inn

## Day 3. The Widow's Tooth

*Heading: **The Widow's Tooth.***
*Question: is the child still at the rock?*

> Two hours north the road runs straight at a rock taller than a house, split down the middle as if something struck it once, long ago. You see it a long way off, black against a white sky, and it does not get any friendlier as you come.
>
> It is the first time the [N] of you have walked anywhere together. Garrick agreed to come as far as the Widow's Tooth and not a step further, and in the two hours it takes to get there he has complained about the road, his boots and the weather, in that order, and seems to be enjoying himself.
>

> There is nobody at the rock. Close to, it is dark with old rain and furred with grey lichen, and the wind makes a low, hollow note in the split, like someone blowing across the neck of a jar.

## Day 4. Fenmark

*Heading: **Fenmark, where the cart went.***
*Question: what happened in this village, and why will nobody say?*

> Fenmark sits low at the edge of the fen, a huddle of reed thatch and peat smoke along a single lane of cobbles, slick with mud between. It goes quiet the moment you come down the lane. Doors do not quite shut. Nobody is standing in one, either.
>
> The lookout from the rock came through here ahead of you. You can tell by the gate at the far end of the street, left swinging, knocking softly on its post in the wind.
>
> Mei takes the left side of the lane, Dev the right. You take the well.

> Behind the well, on an upturned bucket: a bowl of barley, half eaten, the spoon still standing in it. You hold your hand over it. It is still warm.
>
> Whoever was eating it left in a hurry, and not long ago.

**What you notice**
> **Might** · The bucket was not knocked over. It was set down. *Whoever sat here is small, and careful.*
> **Agility** · One heel-mark in the mud, deep at the toe. They went at a run. *Toward the back lanes, not the road.*
> **Intellect** · Barley and nothing else, in a house bowl. This is somebody's ordinary dinner. *They live here, and they did not expect to be found.*
> **Charm** · Every shutter on the street is closed but one. *Somebody wants to be asked.*
> **Craft** · The spoon is whittled, not bought, and whittled badly. *A child's knife did that.*
> **Aim** · From this bucket you can see the whole lane and nobody in the lane can see you. *It was chosen for watching.*

If Fake Guru prefers canon's original choice, that the innkeeper stays unnamed at the inn because nobody at the table asks, the Day 2 text above changes in two places, and Day 3 opens with the name given as a beat of its own.

**Day 2, after `[ASK HIM ABOUT THE LIST]`**, in place of the name beat:
> He comes round the end of the bar and looks at the pages for a while, not touching them. Then he goes back, reaches under the counter, and brings out a satchel, worn soft at the corners.

Everywhere else on Day 2 he stays "the innkeeper" or "he". This fits the Charm notice even more sharply, but the party chat, which opens that same day, and the Day 2 Campfire already call him Garrick, so a player meets the name in the chat before the story gives it. That is the reason the main text names him on Day 2.

**Day 3, the opening**, in place of its second and third paragraphs:
> It is the first time the [N] of you have walked anywhere together. The innkeeper agreed to come as far as the Widow's Tooth and not a step further, and in the two hours it takes to get there he has complained about the road, his boots and the weather, in that order, and seems to be enjoying himself.
>
> A mile out, it occurs to somebody that none of you knows what to call him. He gives it without breaking stride, as if it were the least interesting thing about the morning.
>
> *"Garrick."*
>
> A little further on the other question is put to him: how did he know this place from one line of scribble? He answers that one to the air.
>
> *"People tell a man with a cloth in his hand everything,"* Garrick says. *"They forget he's got ears."*

The rest of Day 3 is as in the main text.

## Appendix B. New Garrick lines

1. **Day 2, after `[ASK HIM ABOUT THE LIST]`:** *"Garrick."* NEW. His name, given before the question is finished. It is true, it predicts and promises nothing, and it denies nothing. He offers his name and asks for none of theirs, which keeps the Charm notice true and fits the Deception Table's "he does not offer, he corrects": he gives only the smallest thing asked. In Appendix A's version the same single word moves to Day 3.

There are no other new Garrick lines. New actions for him, all small and kind: crossing the room like a man who has crossed it ten thousand times, with the cloth on his shoulder; coming round the bar to look at the pages without touching them; coaxing the charcoal woman's banked coals into flame on Day 5, which canon already gives him as "builds the fire up"; ordering at a crowded drovers' inn on Day 6.

## Appendix C. Things I was unsure of

1. **Morning or evening at the mine.** The Day 6 Pull says "Cross at first light. You will be there by morning." The Gathering opens "Evening finds you where the map said you would be." Both are canon and I kept both. A bridging line such as "You are across by morning, and it takes the rest of the day to find the way down to it" would invent a day's search, so I left it for Fake Guru: either the Pull's "by morning" or the arrival's "Evening" could move.
2. **Day 4 times.** The boy says the man ran through "at first light" and "he has half a day on you"; Day 5 says the camp is two hours east and the party arrives after dark. I did not change any time, and gave Fenmark "thin sun" and no hour.
3. **Garrick's look.** Canon gives him no description. I gave him only what canon already implies: the cloth, which his own Day 3 line names, the apron, which Day 2 names, and an unhurried way of moving. No age, hair or face.
4. **"A locked Seeker Fragment".** Kept on every day. The 16 Oct audit asks whether "locked" pulls against the glossary; that is Fake Guru's call and touches the generated scripts.
5. **The Day 4 summary card.** The worked example figures are now placeholders, `[gold]`, `[growth]` and `[what your gold now buys]`. The build already overwrites them; if Fake Guru wants the "You can now afford" line on every day's card, it belongs in the card template, not only on Day 4.
6. **"It is a roll, and a miss costs nothing."** The Gathering's first [LOOK CLOSER] line is a true rules statement, not comfort before a danger, so I kept it. Flagging it only because the rule against reassuring a player is close by.
7. **The other five Day 1 openings.** Only the Warrior's opening is in the episode file, so only that one is rewritten here. The other five classes' openings should be brought to the same voice, ending on the same axle and the same silent man.
8. **New detail added as texture**, not as fact, for checking against later weeks: the sign at the Hollow Wake worn soft like a face in an old coin; the drovers, dogs and ham at the Day 6 roadside inn; the lichen and the wind's note in the split of the Widow's Tooth; Fenmark's reed thatch and peat; frost on the charcoal road. None of them touches a clue, a gate or a later scene I know of. The roadside inn in particular should be checked against any later visit, in case a later week describes it differently.
9. **Gate check.** No narration here uses "the Order", "king", "Malakor", "five" or "fifth", "moon-blood", "Highwall" or "heartbeat". The only counts are canon's own: "four names" at the Gathering, "That is six", and the card field "Fragment: 5 found".
