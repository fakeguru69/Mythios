## How the game plays (the rules in brief)

- **The app and the party.** *Tales of the Dark Moon* is a story game in a chat app for a fixed party of 2 to 6 real friends, over 6 weeks. A storyteller called MYTHIOS tells it from stored text. Each friend plays one character, under their own name, with one of six classes (Warrior, Artificer, Sage, Trickster, Assassin, Archer).

- **A week** is six weekdays (Days 1 to 6), each played alone in about 8 to 12 minutes, then a live Gathering on Day 7, played together. A day opens at 5:00am party time.

- **The screen:** a one-screen cover, then four tabs, STORY · RECORD · BAG · STORE, under a top bar (series, party, day, gold). STORY is one thread from morning to night: the recap, "The Party, Yesterday", Garrick's morning line, the chapter (arriving message by message), the end-of-day card, then the fire (the party's chat) once your chapter is finished. RECORD: twelve numbers that only go up, twelve badges, the next step for each. BAG: the Seeker Chest, the Dark Moon Verses, Found Along the Road, the Journal (the fire's nights, your margin notes), Relics. Tapping the party name opens the party panel. On Day 7 the Gathering is played in the same thread, after a tap to join.

- **Attributes:** Might, Craft, Intellect, Charm, Agility, Aim, each in a tier: Novice 1–25 (+0), Apprentice 26–50 (+2), Adept 51–75 (+4), Expert 76–100 (+6), Master 101+ (+8).

- **A Challenge:** pick one of two Attributes the card offers, roll d20 + its tier bonus − the DC; 1 or more passes, a tie fails. Weekday DC by week: 6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 9. The card says "You succeed on X+". Pass: +500g and the Attribute grows. Fail: −150g, and a worse branch of the scene. Both branches reach the same plot. [PRESS ON], on some days after a pass: 4 harder, +200g and one extra detail.

- **Fights:** 100 HP each side, one tap a round, damage = the hit's margin × 5. Gear on two tracks (Weapon 14–18, Armour 27–31, Levels 1–5) bought in the Store. Nobody dies: at 0 HP, "DEFEATED.", and the story goes on.

- **Relics** (150g): an Attribute Relic gives +4 on one roll; a Combat Relic gives +1 Weapon and +1 Armour for one fight. Relics can be given to friends; gold cannot.

- **The Seeker Fragment:** each day a short old text with two missing words appears in every friend's Seeker Chest; each word is a jumble of letters to unscramble (+25g a word). Six fragments make the week's map, which helps at the Gathering.

- **Garrick**, the innkeeper who travels with the party, is met in Week 1 Day 2's chapter. From then on he answers in the party's chat when a message names him, from stored lines (AI small talk only about the road, food and weather, filtered, and never at the fire). He never lies, promises or predicts.

- **After the chapter:** the fire: one more look at the last light (one optional lead and a clue) or sit down, then the party's chat around tonight's question, the clues laid on the table, the clicks, and the fragment.

- **The Gathering:** the Approach (a group vote, one friend rolls), the Battle (everyone fights their own enemy at once), Notice and [LOOK CLOSER], the United Effort (one shared job, turns), the Lore Reveal with Match and Ask, the Twist, the Cliffhanger.

- **Each new day opens with "the road so far":** a short recap of yesterday (and of any day a friend missed).

> It is an hour before all of you are together again, and another before the hole is bridged with what timber is left.
>
> Behind the place where they held her, the tunnel ends in a slab of fallen roof as big as a barn door, with a room beyond. It takes all of you to lift it, and nobody is careful. You go in tired, wet and all at once, and the floor is a mess of your own bootprints before anyone thinks to look at it.
>
> Whatever the dust in there could have told you, it will not tell you now.

#### The six, read together

*⚙ Either way, the whole party reads this:*

> In the doorway of the cut room, by torchlight, somebody reads the week's six aloud, one after another, the way they were meant to be heard together.
>
> *Day 1, the boundary stone:* “Five fell. Five were turned against him. Four were scattered after. One stayed close.”
>
> *Day 2, the inn door-beam:* “They wept for him before they feared him.”
>
> *Day 3, the Widow's Tooth:* “Not every hand that tries will open it. Some locks already know their key.”
>
> *Day 4, the Fenmark well:* “Born when the dark has eaten the moon, marked before the first cry's through.”
>
> *Day 5, the hollow tree:* “The power that gives is also the power that takes.”
>
> *Day 6, the ford-stone:* “The key that locks is also the key that unlocks.”

#### The discovery

*⚙ If option 2 was picked:*

> Behind you, grit is still falling from the place the prop came out of.

*⚙ Either way, the whole party reads this:*

> The room is small, square, and cut by hands a very long time ago. It is dry: the first dry air since the river. Your torches burn straight up in it. Nothing drips. The river, which has been in your ears all night, cannot be heard in here at all.
>
> The map ended at a mark. Here is the mark.
>
> It is cut into the chamber wall at the height of your chest: the shape from Meira's rubbing, the shape at the end of the map. The burned scrap, held up to it, fits, as you knew it would.
>
> But the mark is not a carving. It is a hole. Something sat in it, something made to that shape, and it is gone.

*⚙ If the United Effort was won:*

> The dust on the rim has settled again. Whatever was here was lifted out. Not tonight. Not centuries ago either.

*⚙ If the United Effort was lost:*

> There was dust on the rim. Your own sleeves have wiped it. When this was emptied, nobody can now say.

*⚙ Either way, the whole party reads this:*

> Under the empty place, scratched into the stone itself: **four names.**
>
> None of them is yours. The first is Tamsin's.
>
> The scratches are rough and uneven, made with a knife point and no care. Somebody bends close with a torch and reads them through once, in silence. Nobody reads them aloud.

#### The Cliffhanger

> The chamber is very still.
>
> In the lamplight, Garrick counts the names twice, silent.
>
> *"Three more,"* he says finally.
>
> And from the mine mouth, far behind you: hooves. One horse, already at a full gallop, going away.
>
> By the time anyone reaches the mine mouth there is nothing to see but the dark, and the sound of it fading.
