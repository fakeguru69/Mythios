## The game

*Tales of the Dark Moon* is a story game played in a chat app. A fixed party of 2 to 6 real friends plays it over six weeks.

- An AI storyteller called MYTHIOS tells the story from stored, pre-written text. It does not improvise the plot.
- Each week is six weekdays and a Gathering:
  - On each weekday, each friend plays alone for about 8 to 12 minutes:
    - a chapter in their own thread, with one Challenge or fight that pays gold;
    - a short "last light" lead;
    - a Seeker Fragment word puzzle;
    - the Campfire, a shared evening screen meant to start talk among the friends.
  - Day 7 is the Gathering: a live session of 20 to 30 minutes, played together.
- An innkeeper called Garrick travels with the party. He speaks only in stored lines. A small amount of filtered AI small talk is allowed, and only about the road, food and weather.

## How to work

**Do one week at a time.** Do Week 1 in full, then stop and ask me: "Shall I go on to Week 2?" Wait for my answer before you start the next week. Do the same after each week. After Week 6, ask me for the file `after-the-finale.md`. Then do the hindsight check and the whole-season verdict.

**For each week, go day by day (Days 1 to 6, then the Gathering).** For each day, give:

1. **The day in one line:** what happens, and the question the day answers.
2. **Scores out of 10**, each with one or two sentences of why:
   - **Story:** is it interesting, does it move, does it pay off?
   - **Play:** do the choices, Challenge, fight, lead, fragment and fire matter?
   - **Prose:** is it a pleasure to read?
   - **Pull:** would I open the app tomorrow?
3. **What works:** the best moment, quoted briefly.
4. **What does not:** each problem, quoted briefly, with the file's heading (for example "Week 2, Day 3, Your thread"), why it is a problem, and a concrete fix. Mark each one **Big**, **Medium** or **Small**.

Then, for the week as a whole, answer every question below that applies to it, and end with the week's three biggest fixes, in order.

## What to audit (including, but not only, these)

**The daily game**
- Is each day interesting, and does it reach a strong payoff, or does it stop where a scene ends?
- Does each Challenge and each fight make sense in the story, or is it filler? Would the day be better without it? Does passing or failing feel different enough to matter?
- Do the choices change anything a player can feel, or are they only buttons?
- Does it feel like pressing buttons to move on, or is there a real sense of story and of the characters developing?
- Do I want to log in every day? What brings me back, and what might make me skip a day?

**The Gathering**
- Is the week's payoff at the Gathering strong? Does it answer the week's question and open the next?
- Is the Gathering day interesting to play together? Does every friend have something to do, or do some just watch?
- Do the devices, the Approach vote, the Battle, Notice and [LOOK CLOSER], the United Effort, Match, Ask and the question to the table, make sense? Do they make the night better, or are they machinery between the story beats?

**Social play and the Campfire**
- Is the Campfire too complicated? It holds the board of clues, tonight's question with its pins, the fragment's meaning question, the riddle, the case so far, an open question and a message in a bottle. What would you cut or merge?
- Does it do its job: to get the friends talking, guessing and working together after the day? Or does it replace the talk with forms to fill in?
- Compared with a live tabletop game like D&D, does it feel less like organic play, where players act freely and the story bends to them? Is that a problem for this kind of game, or the right trade? What could bring back some of that freedom without losing the written story?

**Garrick**
- Is Garrick likeable? Is he warm and dry with a light humour, and never curt, bossy or smug?
- Speaking only in stored lines, will he sound like a computer character: repeating, stiff, off the point? Where is that risk greatest, and how could it be lowered?
- Do his lines stay true to his rule of never lying, promising or predicting, while still feeling natural?

**The story**
- Is every story piece clear to a player, at least in hindsight? Name anything a player could miss, or misunderstand, that a later part depends on.
- Are there narrative loopholes: things that do not add up, characters acting against sense, questions raised and never answered, or anything that contradicts something earlier?
- Is every piece of prose a pleasure to read: the chapters, recaps, Garrick's lines, the fragments, the scripture and the notices? Point to anything rushed, flat, over-written, unclear or repetitive.
- Is the pacing right across the week and the season? Does each week escalate, and does the mystery build fairly?

**The whole**
- Is it a good game? Are the mechanics, numbers, choices, risk and reward sound and satisfying?
- Is it a good story? Are the characters, mystery, stakes, twist and ending strong?
- Do the two support each other, or pull against each other?

**Anything else worth looking out for**, for example:
- how the game teaches itself in Week 1;
- the length of a day on a phone;
- repetition across weeks;
- whether the gold, gear and Relics stay interesting;
- whether a party of two or of six would feel different;
- whether a friend who misses days can catch up;
- tone, above all toward the children;
- whether the hidden truth is fair once known.

Be honest and specific. Praise is useful only when it says exactly what works. I would rather hear a hard truth now than after launch.
