4. *After Week 6 it asks for `after-the-finale.md`. Upload it only then: it holds the secret, and the AI should first meet the story the way a player does.*

You are a senior game designer and story editor. You know:

- tabletop roleplaying (D&D and the like);
- narrative and social mobile games;
- interactive fiction;
- daily-habit game design.

Please audit a game I am designing. I am the designer. I do not write code, so write in plain words. Explain any design term the first time you use it, in one line.

## The game

*Tales of the Dark Moon* is a story game played in a chat app by a fixed party of 2 to 6 real friends, over six weeks.

- An AI storyteller called MYTHIOS tells it from stored, pre-written text. It does not improvise the plot.
- Each week has six weekdays and a Gathering.
  - On each weekday, each friend plays alone for about 8 to 12 minutes:
    - a chapter in their own thread, with one Challenge or fight that pays gold;
    - a short "last light" lead;
    - a Seeker Fragment word puzzle;
    - the Campfire, a shared evening screen meant to start the friends talking.
  - Day 7 is the Gathering: a live session of 20 to 30 minutes, played together.
- An innkeeper called Garrick travels with the party. He speaks in stored lines, with a little filtered AI small talk about the road, food and weather only.

## What you will be given

The season comes in three files, two weeks each, given to you in turn:

- `season-part-1-weeks-1-2.md`: you have this now;
- `season-part-2-weeks-3-4.md`: ask me for it before Week 3;
- `season-part-3-weeks-5-6.md`: ask me for it before Week 5.

Each file starts with the rules in brief: read them first. In the files:

- quoted text (`>`) is exactly what a player reads;
- lines marked *⚙* describe the gameplay: what the screen offers, the rules, the numbers;
- every story branch is given and labelled;
- the sample party is four friends (you, Mei, Dev and Arjun); the game fills in real names and the party's size.

The files do not show the screens' look, timing or sound, or Garrick's free AI small talk. Judge what is there, and say where something can only be judged by playing.

After Week 6 you will get a fourth file, `after-the-finale.md`. It holds the season's secret and what Garrick's lines really meant, for a hindsight check.

## How to work: one week at a time

**Give each week your full attention, alone.**

1. **Audit only the week you are on.** Do not read ahead into the next week, even when it is in the same file.
2. **Judge it as a player who has played only up to that point.** Some things are meant to be hidden or puzzling at first: say what a player believes at each point, and whether that is fair.
3. **Stop at the end of each week.** End by asking: "Shall I go on to Week N?" Then wait for my answer.
4. **Ask for the next file** before Week 3 and before Week 5.
5. **Ask for `after-the-finale.md`** after Week 6, then do Part 2 and Part 3 below.

Keep notes as you go. The final report will draw on every week.

## Part 1: each week, day by day

For each day (Days 1 to 6, then the Gathering on Day 7), give:

1. **The day in one line:** what happens, and the question the day answers.
2. **Scores out of 10, each with a sentence or two of why:**
   - **Story:** is it interesting, does it move, does it pay off?
   - **Play:** do the choices, the Challenge or fight, the lead, the fragment and the fire matter?
   - **Prose:** is it a pleasure to read?
   - **Pull:** would I open the app tomorrow?
3. **What works:** the best moment, quoted briefly.
4. **What does not:** each problem, with:
   - a brief quote and the file's heading (for example "Week 2, Day 3, Your thread");
   - why it is a problem;
   - a concrete fix;
   - a size: **Big**, **Medium** or **Small**.

Then, for the week as a whole:

- answer every question below that applies to it;
- give the week's score out of 10;
- list its three biggest fixes, in order.

### What to audit (including, but not only, these)

**The daily game**
- Is each day interesting, and does it reach a strong payoff, or does it just stop where a scene ends?
- Does each Challenge and each fight make sense in the story, or is it filler?
  - Would the day be better without it?
  - Does passing or failing feel different enough to matter?
- Do the choices change anything a player can feel, or are they only buttons?
- Does it feel like pressing buttons to move on, or is there a real sense of story and of the characters developing?
- Do I want to log in every day? What brings me back, and what might make me skip a day?

**The Gathering**
- Is the week's payoff at the Gathering strong? Does it answer the week's question and open the next?
- Is the Gathering day interesting to play together? Does every friend have something to do, or do some just watch?
- Do the devices make sense, and do they make the night better, or are they machinery between story beats? The devices are:
  - the Approach vote;
  - the Battle;
  - Notice and [LOOK CLOSER];
  - the United Effort;
  - Match;
  - Ask;
  - the question to the table.

**Social play and the Campfire**
- Is the Campfire too complicated? What would you cut or merge? It holds:
  - the board of clues;
  - tonight's question, with its pins;
  - the fragment's meaning question;
  - the riddle;
  - the case so far;
  - an open question;
  - a message in a bottle.
- Does it achieve its aim: to get the friends talking, guessing and working together after the day? Or does it replace that talk with forms to fill in?
- Compared with a live tabletop game like D&D, does it feel less like organic play, where players act freely and the story bends to them?
  - Is that a problem for this kind of game, or the right trade?
  - What could bring back some of that freedom without losing the written story?

**Garrick**
- Is Garrick likeable: warm and dry, with a light humour, never curt, bossy or smug?
- With stored lines, will he sound like a computer character: repeating, stiff, off the point? Where is that risk greatest, and how could it be lowered?
- Do his lines keep his rule of never lying, promising or predicting, and still sound natural?

**The story**
- Is every story piece clear, at least in hindsight? Name anything a player could miss or misunderstand that a later part depends on.
- Are there narrative loopholes? For example:
  - things that do not add up;
  - characters acting against sense;
  - questions raised and never answered;
  - anything that contradicts something earlier.
- Is every piece of prose a pleasure to read? This covers the chapters, recaps, Garrick's lines, fragments, scripture and notices. Point to anything rushed, flat, over-written, unclear or repetitive.
- Is the pacing right? Does the week build, and is the mystery fair?

**Anything else worth looking out for.** For example:
- how the game teaches itself in Week 1;
- the length of a day on a phone;
- repetition from week to week;
- whether gold, gear and Relics stay interesting;
- whether a party of two or of six would feel different;
- whether a friend who misses days can catch up;
- the tone, above all toward the children.

## Part 2: the hindsight check (after `after-the-finale.md`)

Re-read the season with the secret in mind.

1. **Clues and Garrick's lines:** which land well in hindsight, which feel like cheating, and which are too hidden ever to find?
2. **Loopholes:** list every loophole the secret opens or fails to close.
3. **Fairness:** is the hidden truth fair once known? Could an attentive party have suspected it?

## Part 3: the whole season, as one

Now step back and judge the season as a whole, not week by week:

- **The arc:**
  - Does the season build?
  - Does each week earn its place?
  - Is there a sagging middle?
  - Does the finale pay off six weeks of play?
- **The game:** are the mechanics, numbers, choices, risk and reward sound and satisfying across six weeks, or do they wear thin?
- **The story:** are the characters, mystery, stakes, twist and ending strong?
- **Game and story together:** do they support each other, or pull against each other?
- **Garrick across the season:** does he stay likeable and alive, or does he become a repeating computer character?
- **The friends together:** does the season make the friends play together, talk and care? Is it worth six weeks of their evenings?
- **Would I play it with my friends, and finish it?**

## The final report: one .md file

When Parts 1 to 3 are done, write the **final report as one Markdown file** named `dark-moon-audit-report.md`.

- If you can create files, give it to me as a file to download.
- If you cannot, put the whole report in one Markdown code block that I can save as a `.md` file.

The report contains, in this order:

1. **The verdict in five lines:** is it a good game, is it a good story, and the single most important change.
2. **Scores:** a table with a score out of 10 for each of the following, each with one line of why:
   - the game;
   - the story;
   - the prose;
   - the Gatherings;
   - the Campfire;
   - Garrick;
   - "would I log in every day";
   - "would I play it with my friends".
3. **The whole season:** your findings from Part 3, by theme.
4. **The hindsight check:** your findings from Part 2.
5. **Week by week:** for each week, its score, a short summary, its scores day by day as a table, and every Big and Medium problem with its fix.
6. **Every problem found, in one list:** each with week, day, heading, size, the problem and the fix, sorted Big first.
7. **The ten most important fixes for the whole season, in order:** each with what to change and why.
8. **What must not change:** the parts that work best and should be protected.

Be honest and specific. Praise is useful only when it says exactly what works. I would rather hear a hard truth now than after launch.
