## The game, in short

*Tales of the Dark Moon* is a mystery for a fixed party of **2 to 6 real friends**, played on phones over **6 weeks**. A storyteller voice, MYTHIOS, tells it from stored text. An AI is used only for small talk from Garrick, the innkeeper, in the party chat.

**A week:**
- **Days 1 to 6** are played alone, about 8 to 12 minutes each:
  1. a chapter with choices;
  2. one paid beat, either a **Challenge** (a d20 roll plus a bonus against a target) or a **fight**, sometimes with **[PRESS ON]** for a harder, optional roll;
  3. the **last light**: one lead followed, for a clue;
  4. the **Campfire**: the friends lay what they found on a shared table, agree with or doubt each other's pieces, and pin an answer to the night's one question. The party's call stands at dawn. When two pieces on the table belong together, the table "clicks" and shows a line;
  5. a **Seeker Fragment**, a short text with two missing words to unscramble.
- **Day 7 is the Gathering:** live, all friends at once, about 30 minutes. It runs the Approach, the Battle, the United Effort (a shared job of turn-by-turn rolls against two bars), a "One breath" moment where the party advises one friend, then Match, Ask, a twist and a cliffhanger.

**Reading the file:**
- `>` lines are what the player reads.
- `[CAPITALS]` are buttons.
- *⚙ lines* are the game's rules and conditions, which the player does not see as text.
- A line marked *If…* appears only when that condition is true.

## What changed this week (judge these closely)

The designer has just rebuilt Week 1 as a pilot. If these changes work, the same treatment goes to Weeks 2 to 6. So the main question is: **do they work, and should they spread?**

1. **The fire's clicks now ask instead of tell.** Each click lays two pieces side by side and ends on a question, without stating the answer.
2. **Road clicks.** On Days 3 to 6, the clue a friend's own road earned that day can click with a lead's clue. The aim is that your choice of road matters at the fire.
3. **Day 1's fire,** where you sit alone, gets one opening line so that the first night does not feel empty.
4. **Day 5 has a new tap, `[SIT WITH HER]`,** placed inside what was the longest read of the week with nothing to press.
5. **Gathering 1 answers the week.** Two lines appear only because of what the party did:
   - one depends on a road a friend took on Day 6;
   - one reads back the party's call at Day 6's fire.
6. **"One breath" now has weight.** Each of its three options gives the United Effort that follows a small edge: +2 on one friend's first roll, the thing at risk starting 10 higher, or +1 on every friend's first roll.

## What to look out for

**A. Choices and payoff**
- On each day, does at least one choice feel like it matters? Name any day where it does not.
- When a choice pays off later (at the fire, or at the Gathering), would a player *notice* the link? Would they think "ah, that is because we…"? Or would it pass unseen?
- Do the two paths of a choice each leave something of their own?
- "One breath": is the pick a real dilemma? Are the three edges fair against each other? Does the screen make the effect visible?

**B. The fire**
- Does each night's question have enough on the table to argue about? Could two friends honestly pin different answers?
- Do the clicks give the party something to *say* to each other, or do they end the talk?
- Does any click, or any line, tell the player what to think? Quote it.
- Is the lone Day 1 fire still a good first night?

**C. Gathering 1**
- Do the two week-lines feel earned and clear, or bolted on?
- Do they read well wherever they appear, both when a line shows and when it does not?
- Does the night build: arrival, discovery, the One breath moment, the twist, the cliffhanger?

**D. Story and prose**
The intended voice: a well-told storybook. It should be warm, concrete and textured, a pleasure to read, never padded and never rushed into a one-liner. It uses British spelling and no brackets in the prose.
- Flag any line that breaks this voice: modern phrasing, explaining, flat filler, or repeated words.
- Flag any line that contradicts the page: a fact, a count, a time, an object in the wrong hand.
- Look hard at the lines that are new this week, listed above. Do they match the voice of the text around them?
- Garrick should be likeable: warm, dry and kind, never curt or bossy. Does he read that way?

**E. Pacing**
- Where does the player read a long stretch with nothing to do? Is Day 5 better now?
- Does any day feel like "tap, read, tap" with no thought needed?

**F. Party size**
- Read the lines that name or count friends. Would any read wrong for a party of 2, or of 6?
