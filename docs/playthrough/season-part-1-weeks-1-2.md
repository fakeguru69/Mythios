## How the game plays (the rules in brief)

- **The app and the party.** *Tales of the Dark Moon* is a story game in a chat app for a fixed party of 2 to 6 real friends, over 6 weeks. A storyteller called MYTHIOS tells it from stored text. Each friend plays one character, under their own name, with one of six classes (Warrior, Artificer, Sage, Trickster, Assassin, Archer).

- **A week** is six weekdays (Days 1 to 6), each played alone in about 8 to 12 minutes, then a live Gathering on Day 7, played together. A day opens at 5:00am party time.

- **Places:** your own thread (the day's chapter, private questions to Garrick), the party chat (one result line per friend, Garrick's morning line, talk, the Gathering), and the tabs: Record (your numbers), Bag (Relics, Seeker Chest, Dark Moon Verses), Store (gear and Relics).

- **Attributes:** Might, Craft, Intellect, Charm, Agility, Aim, each in a tier: Novice 1–25 (+0), Apprentice 26–50 (+2), Adept 51–75 (+4), Expert 76–100 (+6), Master 101+ (+8).

- **A Challenge:** pick one of two Attributes the card offers, roll d20 + its tier bonus − the DC; 1 or more passes, a tie fails. Weekday DC by week: 6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 9. The card says "You succeed on X+". Pass: +500g and the Attribute grows. Fail: −150g, and a worse branch of the scene. Both branches reach the same plot. [PRESS ON], on some days after a pass: 4 harder, +200g and one extra detail.

- **Fights:** 100 HP each side, one tap a round, damage = the hit's margin × 5. Gear on two tracks (Weapon 14–18, Armor 27–31, Levels 1–5) bought in the Store. Nobody dies: at 0 HP, "DEFEATED.", and the story goes on.

- **Relics** (150g): an Attribute Relic gives +4 on one roll; a Combat Relic gives +1 Weapon and +1 Armor for one fight. Relics can be given to friends; gold cannot.

- **The Seeker Fragment:** each day a short old text with two missing words appears in every friend's Seeker Chest; each word is a jumble of letters to unscramble (+25g a word). Six fragments make the week's map, which helps at the Gathering.

- **Garrick**, the innkeeper who travels with the party, is in the party chat: he answers when a message names him, from stored lines (AI small talk only about the road, food and weather, filtered). He never lies, promises or predicts. He can also be asked privately in your own thread and at the fire.

- **After the chapter:** the last light (one optional lead and a clue), then the Campfire (shared notes, tonight's question, the riddle), then bed.

- **The Gathering:** the Approach (a group vote, one friend rolls), the Battle (everyone fights their own enemy at once), Notice and [LOOK CLOSER], the United Effort (one shared job, turns), the Lore Reveal with Match and Ask, the Twist, the Cliffhanger.

- **Each new day opens with "the road so far":** a short recap of yesterday (and of any day a friend missed).

> The map was right. Deeper in the hill than the working chamber where they drove you back, down a shaft of its own, there is a stair. It was cut so long ago that its edges have worn soft as soap. The air on it is cold, and has not been breathed in a long time.
>
> At its foot is a second room, older than the one above it, older than the mill, older than anyone's memory of this valley.
>
> You hear the chanting before you see the light. Low, even, patient: the same few words, over and over, too muffled to make out. It is not the sound of people in a hurry. It is the sound of people who have done this many times before.

#### The Battle

> You come off the last step into lamplight.
>
> Four robed figures stand in a ring around a stone table, lamps at their feet, their shadows thrown huge and wavering up the walls. On the table lies the miller's boy. He is awake, with his wrists bound, and his eyes find yours over the shoulder of the one holding the knife.
>
> Nobody shouts. The robed figures simply turn, all at once, as if they had been expecting you. The chant does not even break.

#### The black circle

> Somebody lifts the book out from under the fallen lamp and beats the sparks out of its corner. It is small, bound in black cloth, and still warm from the flame. The cloth is worn shiny in one place, where a thumb has held it a great many times.

> This belongs to *them*: their scripture, the kind of thing a man carries against his chest. On its first page, under a black circle painted like a moon with no light in it, is a title: *The First Book of the Order of the Dark Moon.*
>
> The scrap of cloth torn from the scout's coat, two nights ago on the hillside, is laid down beside it. The same circle.
>
> For a moment nobody says anything. The spilled lamps tick as they cool.
>
> The kidnappers have a name. And somewhere in these pages is the one they pray to.

#### The United Effort

> You have the black book in your hands when the far wall begins to sound.
>
> It comes from the hole they dug: boots, a great many, and lamplight growing in it like water rising in a pipe. They left with what they came for. Now they are coming back for what they left behind: their dead, and their book.
>
> There are more lamps in that tunnel than you can count. This is not a fight.
>
> But they cut that tunnel in a night and a day, through ground nobody had disturbed in a thousand years, and it is standing on green timber and hope. Take out the right prop, and the hill will shut it for you. Take out the wrong one, and the fall comes down on your side of it.
>
> Every fall changes what is holding what. So one of you looks, and calls it, and the nearest boot does the rest. Then the next of you looks again.

**THE UNITED EFFORT: THE BREACH**

*⚙ Two bars at 100 (60 for a friend alone): "The Tunnel" (the thing in the way) and "The Room" (the thing at risk). Friends take turns, nobody twice until all have gone. - **On a pass:**

  - *(early)* The cross-piece. It was carrying nothing until the first fall; now it is carrying everything. Out it comes. The lamplight in the hole narrows to a slot.
  - *(middle)* Left-hand post, low down. The roof behind it comes down in a long soft rush, and the boots on the far side stop.
  - *(middle)* You call it, and you are right. Somewhere in the dust, on their side, a man is coughing.
  - *(late)* One frame is holding all of it now. You can see which leg.
  - *(late)* The slot is a hand wide. A lamp is pushed up to it from the other side, and an eye.
  - *(the end)* The last leg goes. The hill shuts like a book. The dust takes a long time to settle, and when it has there is no hole, no light, and no sound from the other side at all.
- **On a fail:**
  - *(early)* The big prop, the obvious one. It was holding up your side. A slab of the old wall leans out and comes down across the floor, and one of the lamps is under it.
  - *(early)* Wrong. The roof over the hole stays where it is. The roof over your heads lets go a barrow-load of stone.
  - *(middle)* The fall comes your way. When the dust thins, the far end of the room is rubble, and the stone table is the edge of what is left.
  - *(middle)* A crack runs across the ceiling from the hole to the stair, as quick as a lizard.
  - *(late)* There is room to stand, and not much more. Everything you have not got in your hands is under stone.
  - *(late)* The foot of the stair is going.
  - *(the end)* The room comes in.
- **Between turns:** Corin has put both hands over his ears. / On the stone table, the lamps the fight kicked over are guttering. / Whoever is coming is not hurrying. They have never needed to.

*⚙ If the United Effort was won:*

*⚙ If the United Effort was lost:*

And Corin is holding something out to you with both hands: a book bound in plain oiled leather. He took it from the niche by the lamp as you carried him past.

#### The ledger

> It is Corin who shows you. Still shaking, he points past the table to a niche in the wall, where a lamp still burns beside another book. It is nothing like the Order's scripture. This one is bound in plain oiled leather, the kind a merchant keeps his accounts in.
>
> It is a ledger.
>
> Page after page of children's names. Beside each name, a place. Beside each place, a date, and the miles to the next. The newest pages are in one small, careful hand. The oldest are in other hands, faded almost to nothing. And beside many of the old names, a single word, written the same way every time: *given.*

> *given.*

> The newest page has four names on it. They are the four from the wall in the mine. Two are the children you've saved: Tamsin, the girl from Fenmark, and Corin, the boy beside you now. The other two you haven't found yet.

> Garrick reads the ledger over someone's shoulder, and for the first time since you have known him he goes quiet in a different way: slower, more careful, like a man choosing his words.
