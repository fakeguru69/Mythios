# The storybook voice (agreed with Fake Guru, 16 Oct 2026)

The standard for every word a player reads: the story, the cult's scripture, the lore, Garrick, the Campfire, the fragments, the notices. Fake Guru: "Make this a pleasurable story to read. Dun rush it." "I can see, hear, smell, taste, feel the scene." "A river that runs a colour it should never have; a book older than age itself."

## What it is

**A good storybook, not a game script.** Someone should enjoy reading it even if they never pressed a button.

1. **A world that feels old and lived in.** Places have weather, light, smell, sound and a past. Roads have not been mended, inns have regulars, villages have habits. Said in passing, through the thing in front of you, never as a history lesson.
2. **All five senses.** The reader can see, hear, smell, taste and feel the scene: woodsmoke in the rafters, cold that gets into the teeth, the taste of iron in the air.
3. **The telling detail over the general word.** Not "it was cold" but frost on the cart ruts, thin as paper. One sharp detail beats three adjectives.
4. **Language with a little strangeness and music in it.** A river that runs a colour it should never have. A book older than age itself. Fresh images, never purple or clever for its own sake.
5. **Moments get room to breathe.** An arrival, a beat, a turn, a close. A big moment has a pause before it and a line that lingers after. Short punchy lines only where they hit, never as the default.
6. **Rhythm.** Long sentences and short ones mixed. Read aloud, it flows.
7. **People are shown by what they do.** Garrick by gesture, timing and dry warmth; likeable, pleasant, lightly funny, until the reveal.
8. **Always clear.** Every passage leaves the player sure where they are, what just happened, and what matters. A load-bearing clue is said plainly (canon's own rule). Beautiful, never foggy.
9. **Clean prose.** No brackets or parentheses, no asides, no stage notes, no game words inside the story.
10. **The right length.** A passage, not a page: usually three to six sentences for a scene. Nothing padded, nothing rushed.
11. **Judge the whole day.** Read a day start to end: does it have a sense of place, a shape, a mood, a close? Rework a scene where needed rather than swap a word.
12. **One voice.** Second person, present tense, British spelling, quietly wry, a little old-fashioned; warm on top, something cold underneath.

## What never moves

The story's facts, clues, names, numbers and outcomes; every [PLANT] line and double meaning word for word; every Garrick line in the Deception Table word for word (the narration around it may grow); the first-use gates; placeholders such as [N] and friends' names; buttons and cards. Garrick never lies, promises or predicts.

## Examples

**The first lines of the game (Week 1 Day 1).** Now: "The wagon axle snaps on the old forest road, and something is already moving in the treeline. A man steps out with a blade drawn. He does not ask for your purse. He does not say anything at all."

> The old forest road has not been mended in a generation. Roots have shouldered up through it, the ruts are full of last year's leaves, and the trees lean so close overhead that the afternoon comes down through them green and thin, like light through bottle glass.
>
> Your wagon has been complaining about it for miles. Then, with a crack like a branch in frost, the axle gives, and the whole cart lurches down onto one corner and is still.
>
> In the quiet afterwards, something moves in the treeline.
>
> A man steps out onto the road with his blade already drawn. He does not ask for your purse. He does not say anything at all.

**A rushed pass line.** Now: "It gives at the first try."

> The lock fights you for a moment. Then something inside it gives with a small, dull click, and the lid comes up in your hands while there is still light enough to see by.

**A character's name, as a proper beat**, not an aside in brackets: he gives it before the question is finished, and does not ask for yours.
