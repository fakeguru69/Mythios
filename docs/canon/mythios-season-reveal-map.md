# Tales of the Dark Moon — Season Reveal Map (Step 2 + Step 3)

**Status: DRAFT, written 2026-09-23.** **Updated 2026-09-28:** Episode 2's Gathering Version 2 and the full Seeker Fragment relook are approved and applied — the king's victory (the heirloom, his blood, the enemy walking away) and the Order's offerings of marked blood now land in Ep2, and every fragment table below is final. Episodes 2 and 3 are locked; Episode 3's daily beats are drafted in `episode-3-the-fire-stone.md` (pending review).

**TVMen's season spine (stated 2026-09-28) — everything else works backwards from this:** the player is ambushed, finds their name on a list of [N] with a clue to the inn; at the inn they meet the others and are shown Meira's encrypted notes, which point to a kidnapped child; they ask Garrick to join; they rescue the first child and find a list: four children, probably a kidnapping syndicate (Ep1). Then it's a cult worshipping an ancient king, with blood offerings (Ep2). Then it's more than offerings — blood and Stones and the king's awakening — and the party has led the cult to the Stones; a fifth Stone, they're told, will stop it (Ep4). They find the fifth, but their Seeker blood doesn't work, and the clues say it must be done at the king's tomb (Ep5). At the tomb they fight the undead, try their blood again, fail — and Garrick's blood works. He is the cult's leader, who tricked them into finding all five Stones, which were always what was needed to wake the king (Ep6). *The Gathering reveals, the Order's scripture, the Seeker Fragments, Meira's notes and Garrick's true-but-misleading lines all serve this spine, across 6 episodes × 7 days × 6 fragments. **Episode 3's step (TVMen, 2026-09-28 — "use my split"):** the awakening itself lands at Ep3 — he isn't dead, he sleeps; the Seekers who struck him down were the party's ancestors; the faithful can't lie; and the Order is bleeding children *to wake him*. Ep4 then narrows to the mechanism: not blood alone — the Stones, and the party found them all. "The Six" is retired: the ancient heroes are only ever "the Seekers", their number never stated, since party size varies.*

**What this document is:** the delivery plan for the whole season's lore, in the order TVMen set:
1. **The overall lore** — lives in `mythios-mythology-canon.md` (Step 1, locked). This document never changes the lore; it only decides *when and how* each piece reaches the party.
2. **Step 2 — each Gathering's Lore Reveal and Twist**, mostly delivered through the Cult Bible.
3. **Step 3 — each episode's 6 Seeker Fragments**, mostly setting up that episode's own reveal, with a few deliberately seeding later ones.

**The three in-world documents, unchanged:** Seeker Fragments (the Seekers' own writing — never quote the ancient text), Meira's notes (her commentary on the Seeker Fragments, plus her own fieldwork: find-sites, the marked children, past takings, the party's birth-nights), the Cult Bible (the only place the ancient text is ever quoted — via the Royal Fragment, Layer 2 — surrounded by the Order's own theology, Layer 3). See `mythios-cult-bible.md` for the layer model and presentation convention.

**Where each trail starts (confirmed 2026-09-26):** each of the four trails begins at its shard's *find-site* — where it fell when the moon broke and where the Seekers first found it. Meira's notes are those four find-sites (the Widow's Tooth for Earth, the mill for Wind). The fifth has no find-site; its trail begins on the Seekers' own stretch of the capital's wall, and Garrick sends the party to the old capital, truthfully. The cult picks a marked child near each find-site in advance (from Meira's own worried list), and its tracks always lead the right way because its watchers overhear the party working out each map (season audit H1, 2026-09-28). See `mythios-mythology-canon.md`.

**How the cult always gets there first (clarified 2026-09-23):** the Seekers' blood-lock guards their *ways* — the fragments and the maps they assemble into — not the Stones' sockets. A socket is an open cut in stone; anyone who knows where it is can lift the Stone out. The Stones' only protection was that nobody knew where they were. So the cult doesn't need Seeker blood at the site — it needs the party to *solve the map*. **Rewritten 2026-09-28 (season audit H1, approved by TVMen):** no single fragment shows anything; only all six together show the place. So the Order's watchers shadow the party (its own scripture: *"closer to them than their own shadows"*); on each map night the party works the place out aloud, a watcher hears, and before dawn the Order moves the held child there and wakes the Stone. Garrick tells no one where — his signals only ever say *tonight* — so "Did you tell anyone where we were going?" → *"No."* is true. First shown on-page: the courier at the Ep1 Day 6 inn. (Garrick's Ep1 "he's known it since he was a boy" means the old mine workings, not the Seekers' room inside them — the map is what showed that room.)

**A structural fact worth stating once:** Seeker Fragments were written by the Seekers just after the battle. They cannot know about a present-day kidnapping ring. So fragments can only set up the **lore** half of a Gathering (the Lore Reveal). The **present-day** half (the Twist — Layer A (scale of the crime)'s "it's not X, it's Y" ladder) is always set up by in-scene evidence: sockets, tracks, witnesses, ledgers, captured cultists.

**TVMen's fragment rule (2026-09-27, GM Principles 2.10):** every Seeker Fragment is either important setup or a dramatic moment from the lore, and must read plainly when it's quoted back at its payoff; colour that is neither doesn't earn a fragment. The full relook of all 36 (`review-fragments.md`) was approved and applied 2026-09-28; the fragment tables below are final.

**TVMen's rule for every Gathering (stated 2026-09-26):** reveal enough for *this* episode — often by adding a twist to what the previous episode seemed to settle — and make it clear. The lore is complicated, so clarity comes first. Be cryptic, double-edged and clever, but never so much that a reveal turns vague. The Seeker Fragments do the setting-up beforehand, and make the reveal click in hindsight.

## The three threads that make the finale land

GM Principles 2.4: any clue supporting a major twist needs 5+ occurrences. These are the three big ones, with every occurrence counted.

### Thread 1 — "moon-blood" (supports Ep6 Twist 1: only Garrick's blood works)

**The whole mechanism in one line: the Royal Fragment uses two different words.** The four drink **marked** blood. The fifth drinks **moon-blood**. The party reads them as the same word because Meira — honestly, using the drifted modern sense of the term — glosses "moon-blood" as "the old songs' word for the marked," and nobody ever corrects her. **Why the word drifted that way (confirmed 2026-09-26):** after his fall the king was erased from every record, so his title outlived him and slid onto the only people still tied to the moon — the children born in its dark. Only his household's own copy kept the true meaning.

This answers TVMen's standing concern that the misreading "isn't obvious even on reveal." At the Ep6 reveal, the two lines are put side by side and the difference is plain on the page — not an assertion, a thing the party can see they misread.
