## What it is

The Order of the Dark Moon's own scripture — their telling and understanding of the lore, not a neutral historical record. Found in fragments at Gathering sites, starting from **Episode 2 onward** (not Episode 1 — confirmed: the party has no reason to find cult writings before they know a cult exists).

**In-world, there are exactly three found documents — confirmed 2026-09-23, locked:**

| Device | What it is | Cadence | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seeker Fragments** | Ancient objects containing text the original Seekers wrote themselves (their number is never stated to players), immediately post-battle. **Original writing — never a quotation of the ancient text**, even though the Seekers knew it in full. | Daily, all 6 episodes, 6 per episode | Primary-source history, poetic and ancient-voiced — mostly sets up that episode's own Lore Reveal, with a few season-long seeds (Layer F (false hope) and the finale threads) |
| **Meira's notes** | A modern scholar's actual research: **her commentary on the Seeker Fragments, plus her own fieldwork** — the four find-sites, the marked children near them, past takings (widened 2026-09-26). No access to the Order's scripture or the ancient text; she never writes mechanism content. | Found early (Ep1 Day 2), referenced/added to throughout | Practical investigation direction plus her own translation/commentary bridging old fragment-language to modern terms (see `mythios-meira-notes.md`) |
| **Cult Bible** | The Order's own scripture. **The only document that ever quotes the true ancient text — and even then, only the surviving Royal Fragment of it (see Layers, below), embedded inside the Order's own added theology.** | Found only at Gatherings (Day 7), Episode 2 onward | The actual weekly Lore Reveal — the vehicle for each episode's big reveal beat |

## The three layers behind the Cult Bible (locked 2026-09-23)

This is the one document with real stakes in "what's actually true vs. what's someone's telling," so it needs a clear internal structure:

1. **The Ancient Text (true original)** — never seen whole by anyone, ever. Pre-Malakor, written by whoever first learned to work the shards. Background only. **Never quoted directly anywhere in the game** — nobody in the story possesses it.
2. **The Royal Fragment** — a real, surviving *piece* of that ancient text, passed down through Malakor's family for generations alongside the blood shard itself. This is the one place in the whole story with a genuine, verbatim-as-it-survives line back to real instructional content. **The load-bearing mechanical lines live here.** These have to be literally true, because the season's payoff depends on them being exactly what they say.
3. **The Order's own theology** — centuries of the cult's own composed commentary, doctrine, and mythologizing, built up *around* the Royal Fragment, in their own voice. Not instruction. Not necessarily accurate. *(Added 2026-09-23, not a change to the lock: it's written by a faith whose first tenet is "the faithful shall not lie," so it is never a deliberate falsehood — where it misleads, it's through sincere belief or through what it leaves out, the same way Garrick works.)*

**Every Cult Bible page mixes 2 and 3, on purpose — that's the actual mechanism of the misdirection**, not a gap to fix. A hostile party's spin (Layer 3) reads exactly as authoritative as real mechanical instruction (Layer 2), because nothing marks the difference for the party in the moment.

**Presentation convention (apply to every entry):** the genuine Royal Fragment quote (Layer 2) is always set apart within the page — shorter, harder, older-voiced, explicitly framed as older than the Order's own founding ("set apart on the page, in an older hand — words older than the Order's own name"). The Order's commentary (Layer 3) surrounds it in the book's own, more discursive voice. A careful reread *can* separate the two — the fair-play contract holds — even though a first read takes the whole page as one voice. **First used at Ep2**, so the party already knows how to read an older-hand inset before the Ep4 torn page depends on it.

## The Order's liturgical close — "Until the throne rise whole."

**Every Cult Bible passage from Ep2 to Ep5 ends with this line** — the Order's prayer, repeated the way a congregation repeats an amen. The captured cultist in Ep3 says it too, as a blessing. By Ep4 the party has read it enough times to stop noticing it — which is exactly when the torn page stops one breath before it ("What the four begin, the fifth shall end—"). In Ep6, Garrick's own untorn copy finishes the sentence: "...and the throne rise whole." The party recognises the words at once. **This is the supporting clue for Ep6 Twist 2** (GM Principles 2.4 — see the count in `mythios-season-reveal-map.md`, Thread 2). Keep the wording exactly the same every time (GM Principles 6.4).

## Why it exists as a device

Before the Cult Bible was designed, there was no answer to "how does the Lore Reveal actually get delivered to players — a voice-of-god GM summary?" That was correctly flagged as unsatisfying. The Cult Bible solves it: the party is *reading a real, discoverable, in-world document*, not being told a summary.

## Tone and format — confirmed rules

- **Not a summary. A telling — lore-style, like reading a good book.** Confirmed after multiple drafting passes: a "summary" version (analytical, plain) was explicitly rejected in favor of prose that builds world and character the way genuine lore-text does.
- **Cinematic, not contemporary.** Lines that read as modern/analytical were flagged and rewritten toward mythic, verse-like phrasing.
- **No fixed paragraph count — length follows what the content needs.** Don't rush or compress to hit an artificial length. **Confirmed again 2026-09-23:** TVMen read the first Ep2 draft and asked "so short?" — the Verse of the Answering has been expanded to a full telling, and every later passage is written at that weight.
- **Can be split into multiple discoverable pieces** — Ep4 and Ep5 each have two pages (a Lore Reveal page and a Twist page).
- **Framed as excerpts from an actual text**, titled and structured like scripture. **Every verse opens liturgically with "Hear…"** (series audit, 2026-09-29) — the Answering and the Fall always did; the Four, the Binding, the Watch and the Name now do too.
- **TVMen's rule for every Gathering (stated 2026-09-26):** reveal enough for *this* episode — often by adding a twist to what the previous episode seemed to settle — and make it clear. The lore is complicated, so clarity comes first. Be cryptic, double-edged and clever, but never so much that a reveal turns vague. The Seeker Fragments do the setting-up beforehand, and make the reveal click in hindsight.
- **A joy to read (TVMen, 2026-09-27):** paint the scene and the world — cinematic, with a real story shape (a hook, rising stakes, a turn, an ending) — but never convoluted. Clarity wins. See GM Principles 6.5.
- **Every passage is followed by the Connection** (GM Principles 1.9, point 3a): the strongest matching fragments — about three from the week, since the series audit of 2026-09-29 — laid beside the lines they match and quoted whole. Let the characters say what they now mean; the narrator doesn't explain it for them. In narration it is always "the Order's scripture" — never "Cult Bible" (6.6).
- **Can't be too cryptic.** This is the primary vehicle for explaining the lore, so the writing needs to land the information. The one deliberate exception: the Ep4 torn page is *supposed* to be incomplete — what's missing is information the party fills in themselves, not information withheld from the reader.
- **Never ahead of its episode.** Each passage must be audited against every *later* misreading it has to survive (see the two fixes logged below — both were passages that accidentally gave away a later twist).

## Cult motivation — locked 2026-09-22, deliberately not noble

The Order of the Dark Moon is what remains of Malakor's own royal household — cast out and hunted once his corruption became visible to the kingdom, centuries of hiding and decline. This is **not** a "restore order" or "save the world" belief. Garrick's plan is simple and selfish: wake the king, and the family stops hiding. A king with the undead power at his command — under family control this time, not one man's unmediated grasp — means the family commands the world instead of cowering from it. Whether the bloodline itself is actually free of Malakor's corruption is a belief Garrick holds, not a fact the season confirms; it stays unstated in-fiction, a possible Season 2 lever. See `mythios-mythology-canon.md`'s Present section for the full mechanical logic this content has to support.

## Per-episode content plan (locked 2026-09-22; reconciled with TVMen's escalation ladder 2026-09-23)

- **Ep1 — not used, and no Lore Reveal beat at all.** The Gathering is only 3 acts (Battle → Twist → Cliffhanger). The real payload is the Twist: three more children's names beneath the empty socket — "this was never about you." A syndicate, not (yet) a cult. Meira's notes carry the baseline legend instead (see `mythios-meira-notes.md`).
- **Ep2 — the whole story of the war (locked 2026-09-28, Version 2).** *The Verse of the Answering* — the war, the last wall, the heirloom, his own blood, the dark moon, "it answered," the enemy walking away into the dark, the morning the world didn't end, and the world taking back his name afterward. First Layer 2 inset (the older-hand line, moved up from Ep3). Ties the Order's name to nothing present-day yet.
- **Ep3 — who struck him down, and that he didn't die (rewritten 2026-09-28 — TVMen's split).** *The Verse of the Fall* — his "gift" (the corruption, told as a blessing) and the price he never told anyone; the Seekers, who had wept for him, turning against him; "he did not die... he sleeps"; the Seekers' blood-locks (Layer C (the party's own nature) proven); the doctrine ("the faithful shall not lie"). No heirloom or answering here — Ep2's scripture already told that. The present-day twist, delivered by the prisoner: the Order gives the marked blood *to wake him*.
- **Ep4 — not blood alone: the Stones, then the torn page.** *The Verse of the Four* — moon-origin; blood on bare ground is only blood (the bridge from Ep3's belief); why marked blood wakes a shard, and "where the Seekers' children walk, the faithful follow... carried home to him": it's the Stones, and the party found them all. Then the torn page (the Twist) — the fifth exists, "the fifth shall end—". **Must be delivered as a physically damaged document.**
- **Ep5 — the binding, "moon-blood," then the Tomb.** *The Verse of the Binding* — the binding, told as a scene (his own people at his doors, his own gift turned against him as a blow, his hand opening), the Undying Sleep, the soldiers who still stand his watch, and the Royal Fragment's "The fifth drinks deep of moon-blood, and of no other." Then *The Watch* (the Twist) — where he fell, the dark moon, seven nights.
- **Ep6 — Garrick's own copy.** *The Verse of the Name* (moon-blood was a title; the Order is his house) and the untorn page: "...end, and the throne rise whole."

## The passages (all drafted 2026-09-23)

### Ep2 — The Verse of the Answering *(Version 2, locked 2026-09-28; Layer 3 + first Layer 2 inset)*

> *From the First Book of the Order of the Dark Moon — The Verse of the Answering.*
>
> *Hear how the world nearly ended, and who would not let it.*
>
> *There was a war before this order had a name. It came out of the east like weather, and like weather it hated no one. It only arrived, and kept arriving.*
>
> *Where it passed, the towns burned, and the rivers below them ran grey with ash, and then red. Kings were raised against it and spent, like coin no one expected to see again. Cities older than their own songs went quiet in a single season. Every throne that stood against it fell. Every throne that knelt fell too, only more slowly.*
>
> *At the last there was one kingdom left, and one wall, and one king upon it. He had opened his gates to every people the war had driven from their homes, until his city held what was left of the world inside it. His armies were a handful of tired men who still answered to their names — and he knew the name of every man on his wall. His allies were names cut into stone. His own household had packed what little they could carry, for a flight that had nowhere left to go.*
>
> *It is told that he walked that wall every night of the siege; that he did not sleep; that he did not weep where anyone could see. It is told that the enemy's fires outnumbered the stars. And it is told that on the last evening their herald rode up to the gate and named the hour of the city's ending — dawn — and that the king heard him out, and thanked him, and sent him home with bread.*
>
> *He had been asked to kneel. He would not.*
>
> *And that night, while the city waited for its last dawn, the moon went dark.*
>
> *His house had kept a thing since before his house had a name. No one living could say what it was. It was old; it was not to be sold, or lost, or given away; that was all the keeping had ever passed down. That, and a few pages in a hand no one could read whole — of which, that night, by the light of a single candle, he could read one line:*
>
> > **[Set apart on the page, in an older hand — words older than the Order's own name, and nothing like the plain, careful hand the Seekers left in every fragment]**
> > **"Blood beneath the dark moon — and it answers."**
>
> *So the king went down alone into the oldest room of his house, with the moon gone out above him and the enemy's fires waiting for morning. He set the thing on the stone before him. He opened his palm with his own knife. He had nothing left to lose, and he said so aloud, and he gave it his blood.*
>
> *It answered.*
>
> *And what answered him went out of that room, over the wall, and into the dark where the enemy's camp lay waiting for dawn.*
>
> *No one who stood on the wall has left us their telling. Ours comes from his own house — from those who watched from the high windows while the city slept, and told it after, and never told it any other way. In the dark, the enemy's camp rose: every man of it, all at once, the way a field of birds lifts at a single sound. They did not arm. They did not cry out. They walked out past their own fires into the night, and they did not stop. By morning the fires were cold, the tents stood empty, and the horses wandered loose in the fields. No one ever found where the men had gone.*
>
> *The city that had been told to expect its ending climbed onto its walls and looked out on nothing, and wept. It is told that they wept for him that morning, every one of them, for joy. That was the morning of the answering. The war that had eaten the world was broken, and the world, which had been ending, did not end.*
>
> *And the world he saved took back his name. It scraped it from its stones and burned it from its books, and taught its children the story of that war with him cut out of it.*
>
> *So the faithful remember him, and give the marked blood in his name.*
>
> *Until the throne rise whole.*

**Version 2, locked 2026-09-28 (`review-ep2-gathering-v2.md`, approved by TVMen).** Replaces the Version 1 split between Episodes 2 and 3: the whole war, the heirloom, the answering and the morning after now land in one telling here, read in two parts with a page-turn between *"the moon went dark"* and *"His house had kept a thing."* **Since 2026-09-29 the reader also pauses after *"with him cut out of it"*:** a player-character recognises the schoolbook version of the war every child learns — *the Grey Host broke at Highwall of a fever in its camp, and Highwall was spared by luck* — before the last two lines are read. Episode 3 no longer repeats any of it — see *The Verse of the Fall*, below.

**Changed 2026-09-29 (series audit):** *"This order has no telling from anyone who stood on the wall that night. It has one from those who watched from his own house…"* became *"No one who stood on the wall has left us their telling. Ours comes from his own house…"* — plainer, and *"Ours comes from his own house"* is now the Layer G seed the Ep6 Gathering re-quotes. Added the one human detail about the king: *"he knew the name of every man on his wall"* (it echoes Corin, who says each rescuer's name back, and the Verse of the Binding, where he speaks each Seeker's name). The Answering names no place: "Highwall" and "the Grey Host" come from the players' own schoolbook memory, never from the Order's page.

**Never named in this passage:** shard, stone, awakening. The "thing his house had kept" is the blood shard — the party learns that only at Ep6.

### Ep3 — The Verse of the Fall *(rewritten 2026-09-28 — TVMen's Ep3/Ep4 split; Layer 3 only)*

> *From the First Book of the Order of the Dark Moon — The Verse of the Fall.*
>
> *Hear what became of the king who would not kneel.*
>
> *After the war, he kept his people as a shepherd keeps a flock through a hard winter. The power that had answered him on the night of the dark moon did not leave him, and he shared it. Those who stood nearest him were given the first of his gifts. They no longer felt the cold, nor the blade, nor the long fear of dying. They did not tire. They did not hunger. They stood their watches without end, and never once asked to be relieved. Where his armies camped, nothing grew — and nothing needed to. In time the gift would have reached every one of his people. In time, the whole world he had saved.*
>
> *There is a price for every gift, and he never spoke of this one. He never spoke a false word to his people — not one. He only kept silent about the price. A kept silence is not a lie. But it is a seam. And through that seam, in time, the Seekers came in.*
>
> *They had held his wall. They had wept for him on the morning of the answering. They were his own, and he loved them. But they looked at his gift and called it rot, and would not be told otherwise. So they went out into the far places of the world and gathered what they needed, and they came for him in the night, when he had never once thought to be afraid. And there, where he stood to meet them, they struck him down.*
>
> *He did not die.*
>
> *Hear this, faithful, and never let it be forgotten: he did not die. He fell where he stood, and he sleeps where he fell — the long sleep, death's likeness and not death — while the world outside grows old and forgets his name.*
>
> *And the Seekers hid their work, and set their own blood as the lock upon every way they left behind, so that no hand but their children's could ever open it.*
>
> *If so small a seam as a silence let them in, what would a lie do? It would throw the door wide. Therefore the faithful shall not lie. Speak true, always. Keep your counsel where you must. Hide inside the truth.*
>
> *So the faithful remember him, and give the marked blood in his name, and wait.*
>
> *Until the throne rise whole.*

**What it carries, and what it leaves to the scene:** the heirloom, the blood and the enemy's fate are told at Ep2 (*The Verse of the Answering*), so this passage carries only what Episode 3 needs — the gift and its price (the corruption, told as a blessing — made plain at Ep3 by TVMen's direction, 2026-09-28), the Seekers' betrayal, that he did not die, the Seekers' blood-lock, the doctrine. It ends on *"give the marked blood in his name, and wait"*, deliberately open: the prisoner, who cannot lie, supplies the twist in-scene — the blood is given *to wake him* (see `mythios-gathering-scripts.md`). Renamed from *The Verse of the Six* when "the Six" was retired (2026-09-28). The corruption is in on purpose: players must know what waking him would mean the moment the twist lands.

**Never named in this passage:** shard, stone, the mechanism of waking; his hall, or where he fell (Ep5's twist — "where he fell" is as far as it goes).

### Ep4 — The Verse of the Four *(Layer 3; the Lore Reveal page)*

> *From the Second Book of the Order of the Dark Moon — The Verse of the Four.*
>
> *Hear what the moon left behind.*
>
> *Before there were kings, the moon broke. No telling survives of why. What fell from it fell everywhere — into the hills, into the deep water, into the dark beneath the roots of the world — and where each piece came to rest, it kept a little of the moon inside it, the way a coal keeps a little of the fire.*
>
> *So too the children born in the hour when the moon goes dark. The same light marks them. Their blood remembers what the shards remember. Set that blood upon a shard, and the shard remembers too — and wakes.*
>
> *For longer than any of us can count, the faithful gave the marked blood in his name, and he slept on. Blood poured out upon the ground is only blood. It must be given to a shard.*
>
> *When the Seekers had struck down their king, they took the shards that had served them and carried them to the far places of the land, and hid them, and set their own blood as the lock upon every way that led to them. Nothing of theirs, they said, would ever be found by any hand but their children's.*
>
> *They were right.*
>
> *Therefore the faithful have not waited for the ways to open. We have waited for those who can open them. Where the Seekers' children walk, the faithful follow, closer to them than their own shadows. Where a shard is uncovered, a marked child is brought to it, and the shard drinks, and wakes, and is carried home to him.*
>
> *One shard that has drunk is a coal in the dark, and no more. But when four have drunk, the sleep that holds him wears thin as ice in spring — and as each is carried home and set about him, it thins the faster, until the sky itself remembers the night he answered.*
>
> *Until the throne rise whole.*

**Updated 2026-09-26; rewritten 2026-09-29 (series audit):** the last paragraph states the rule — one shard alone does nothing ("a coal in the dark, and no more"); once four have drunk, the sleep "wears thin as ice in spring", and faster as each is carried home (canon: "What the four actually do"). That's why the dawn stops halfway at the Ep4 Gathering, while the fourth Stone is still on the water. The 2026-09-29 version says the same rule as scripture rather than as a rulebook; the old wording was *"One alone is only a shard that has drunk. But when the four have drunk, the long sleep begins to fail — and as each is carried home and set around him, it fails the faster. The sky itself will remember the night he answered."* **Knock-on:** Garrick's Ep4 line quoting this page is now *"Everything on this page says the sleep wears thin. Thin."* (was "…begins to fail. Begins.") — every copy of that line, and any daily beat quoting "the long sleep begins to fail", must follow.

### Ep4 — The torn page *(Layer 3 frame + Layer 2 inset; the Twist page — deliver as physically damaged. Not a verse, so it has no "Hear…" opening — its frame line is left exactly as it was, 2026-09-29)*

> *[The Order's own hand]* From the Second Book of the Order of the Dark Moon. Even in the old telling, before we had our name, it was known—
>
> > **[Set apart, in the older hand]**
> > **"Four were given to the wind, the water, the fire, and the earth, and drink deep whichever marked blood finds them first. The fifth heeds no element at all, but blood alone, and remembers whose. What the four begin, the fifth shall end—"**
>
> *(The page is torn here, the edge burned black. Whatever came next is gone.)*

**Fixed 2026-09-23 — this page used to give away the finale.** The previous draft read *"...but to blood alone — the king's, and no other's."* That told the party, in Ep4, that the fifth needs the king's blood — which would have made Ep5's moon-blood misreading, and the party's own blood attempt, impossible to believe. Replaced with *"and remembers whose"*: literally true (it's bonded to the first blood that claimed it), once a direct echo of the Ep2 Day 2 Seeker Fragment ("Not every shard remembers the hand that first held it. One did." — **cut 2026-09-28 by TVMen**: beside this page it pointed at royal blood too early; "remembers whose" now stands on its own), and read on first pass as "it knows marked blood when it tastes it."

**Revised again 2026-09-26 (independent review of the Gathering scripts); updated 2026-09-28 to reflect the heirloom's move to Ep2:** "The fifth *answered* to no element" became "The fifth *heeds* no element." "Answered" was the same verb the Ep2 Verse (then Ep3's, before the merge) uses for the king's heirloom ("It answered"), a link a sharp party could follow straight to royal blood. The same review changed two other lines. In what is now the Ep2 Verse, "As he gave his blood beneath the dark moon, so we give the marked blood" became "give the marked blood in his name" — the old parallel pointed at the Stones a week early. In Ep4, the Verse of the Four gained "closer to them than their own shadows": it explains how the Order always got there first, and on a reread it points at Garrick.

### Ep5 — The Verse of the Binding *(Layer 3 + Layer 2 inset; the Lore Reveal page)*

> *From the Third Book of the Order of the Dark Moon — The Verse of the Binding.*
>
> *Hear how they laid him down.*
>

>

>
> > **[Set apart, in the older hand]**

**Rewritten as a scene 2026-09-29 (series audit):** the doors, the names, the blow, his hand opening — a story rather than a summary.
