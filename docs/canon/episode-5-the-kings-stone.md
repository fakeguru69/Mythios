# Episode 5 — The King's Stone

**Investigation:** The four Stones have drunk, the sky has gone wrong, and something in the north has a heartbeat. The torn page says a fifth Stone can end it. Meira never found a fifth — but every telling Garrick knows ends at Highwall, the old capital.
**The debunk (revealed at the Gathering):** The fifth is found — and the party's blood does nothing to it. Not here, and not yet: the old words say where he fell, under the dark moon. Seven nights.

**New places this week:** **The old kingdom's road**, grass-grown, running north. **Highwall**, the old capital — the dead city at the road's end, where nothing grows. **The herald's gate**, the city gate in the stretch of wall the Seekers held on the war's last night. **His hall**, on its hill at the city's heart, doors sealed.

## Day 1. The grey afternoon

*Heading: **The grey afternoon.***
*Question: Meira never found a fifth. Where do you look for a Stone no record names?*

> A third beat. Then a fourth. You feel each one in your teeth and the soles of your feet more than you hear it: slow as a sleeper's heart, from somewhere far to the north.
>
> The dawn never finishes. The morning stays grey and thin, as if the sun had been pushed a long way off, and along the whole shore of the Stillwater the birds have stopped singing. Under that light the lake has gone the colour of old pewter.

> You row Joss home. He sits in the bow with his bandaged hand in his lap, and the little boat in the other.
>
> His grandmother is already down on the shingle, in the strange light, with her hands over her mouth. Garrick holds the bow steady against the stones. She gets him out of the boat by herself.
>
> She does not put him down. She carries him up the shingle the way she must have carried him when he was two, with his feet knocking against her knees, and he lets her.
>
> At the top of the lane he twists round in her arms and holds the little boat up over her shoulder, high, so that all of you can see he still has it.

**What you notice**

## Day 2. The old kingdom's road

*Heading: **The old kingdom's road.***
*Question: what is the heartbeat doing to the country?*

> You leave Reedham in the grey morning, and by noon the lake is gone behind you.
>
> The road was built to carry armies. It still runs straight as a spear across the empty country, but grass has grown up through its stones, and nobody has mended it in longer than anyone can say. Larks should be up over country like this. None are.
>
> Every mile has its stone, and every stone a number, one less than the last.

> Late in the afternoon the road passes between two long, low rows of grassy mounds, one on each side, running away across the fields in perfect lines.

> Drawn up at the head of the rows are the only people you have met all day. A family, come down from the north, with all they own on one cart, and a cow tied behind.

`[WHAT DID YOU SEE?]`

> The man answers without turning round. *"The beasts won't settle. Not since the sky turned."*
>
> It is not an army they are running from. It is their own yard. The dogs first, and then the pigs. He has a bandage on his forearm, and the cow is the only animal they have left that will still be led.
>
> A child on the tailboard counts you, with one finger.

`[WALK IN AMONG THEM]`

> Every mound is the same length. A man's length.
>
> They are graves. Hundreds, dug in straight rows, the way soldiers bury soldiers: the ones who fell in the war, before the last night on the wall.
>
> Among them, one is new. The turf was cut this week and laid back badly, by people with no time to do better.

**What you notice**
> **Might** · The new grave is shallow. *Whoever dug it could not stay to dig deeper.*
> **Agility** · The family's cow has not stood still once since the cart stopped. *She keeps her head to the south.*
> **Intellect** · Hundreds of graves, and not one of them marked with a name. *They were buried by people who knew every one of them.*
> **Charm** · The family walks wide of the new grave, and the child is turned to face the other way. *They know whose it is.*
> **Craft** · At the end of the rows stands one block that is no grave. *It is cut like the waystone at Reedham.*
> **Aim** · The rows run dead straight, and they all point one way. *North. At the city.*

> At the far end of the rows stands one squared block of stone that is no grave. Its top is a separate slab, fitted like a lid.

`[LIFT IT]`

> It comes up with a long grate of stone on stone. The air inside is colder than the evening, and smells of old earth.
>

> The light is going. The family will stay where you stay.

`[CAMP AT THE HEAD OF THE ROWS]` `[CAMP ON THE OPEN ROAD]`

> At dusk the heartbeat comes.
>
> The grass on the graves shivers with it, row after row, like the hide of a horse. The cow screams and throws herself against her rope.
>
> And out of the dusk come the dogs.

## Day 3. The herald's gate

*Heading: **The herald's gate.***
*Question: how do you get in, and where in a whole dead city do you start?*

>

> The road runs straight to a gate. You know the words. You heard them read under the mill hill. *Their herald rode up to the gate and named the hour of the city's ending — dawn.*

## Day 5. Below the hall

*Question: who keeps a fire below the hall?*

>
> The last terrace ends in a square, and the hall fills the sky above it: a great pale building on the city's highest ground, its doors taller than houses, and shut. The heartbeat comes from behind them.
>
> And in the shell of a house across the square, a fire is burning.

> **THE FIRE IN THE SQUARE**

> **Agility** · get close enough to hear them · `[ROLL]`
> **Aim** · stay where you are, and read the whole camp from here · `[ROLL]`
> REWARD: +500g
> IF YOU FAIL: you see the fire and little else, and a lantern swings your way

> You see everything, and they see nothing.
>
> Near dusk the watch changes. In the dead silence of the city, every word carries. A watchman walks out to the one at the wall, and touches his shoulder.
