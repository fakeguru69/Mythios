# Part 1. Brief for the reviewer

You are reviewing the daily story spine of a narrative game. Act as a senior narrative designer who has shipped serial mystery games and has written for investigative television. Be direct. Praise is not needed; findings are.

**Read Parts 2 to 5 first, then do the following.**

1. **Score each of the six weeks** against the fifteen criteria below, 1 to 5 (1 = fails, 3 = works, 5 = excellent), with one sentence of reason for every score of 3 or lower.
2. **Name the weak days.** List every day whose answer does not really answer its question, whose answer is not a payoff a player would feel, or whose pull would not bring a player back tomorrow (criterion 15). Rank the ten weakest of the 36.
3. **Rewrite those ten** in the same six-line format the ladder uses (Question, Player does, Answer, Twist, Pull, Change). Keep to the constraints in Part 2. Say what new story fact each rewrite needs, if any.
4. **Check the ladder as a ladder.** For each week, say whether each day's answer builds on the day before, whether the week climbs to its Gathering, and whether any day could be deleted without loss. A day that could be deleted is a filler day and must be flagged.
5. **Check the Gatherings are protected.** Flag any weekday answer that gives away, or makes obvious, what that week's Gathering reveals. Fair foreshadowing is wanted; a spoiled twist is not. Say which side of the line each flagged case falls on.
6. **Find repetition.** List devices used too often across the season (the witness interview, "they were here an hour ago", the map on Day 6, a child's sign left behind) and propose a different device for the days where it has gone stale.
7. **Find errors.** Continuity, time and distance, anything a character could not know, anything that contradicts the hidden truth in Part 2, anything that would make Garrick say something false.
8. **Answer the questions for TV** in Part 4, each with a recommendation and a reason.
9. **Finish with the three changes that would most improve the season,** in order.

**Rules for your suggestions.**
- Do not change what any Gathering reveals, or the order of the six reveals.
- Do not write dialogue for Garrick. If a day needs him to speak, say what the line must do, and mark it "needs a Garrick line"; the designer writes and approves those. Every existing Garrick line quoted in this file is approved and may be reused.
- Do not change the text of any Seeker Fragment (Part 5). You may move where one is found.
- Do not make a fragment the payoff of a day. See "Fragments" in Part 2.
- A weekday enemy from the Order must get away and must never be questioned. See "The faithful cannot lie" in Part 2.
- Keep each day playable in about five minutes on a phone.
- Where you disagree with a constraint, follow it in your rewrites, and say separately why you would change it.

## The fifteen criteria

| # | Criterion | The test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A clear question** | Thirty seconds in, could the player say in one sentence what today is for? |
| 2 | **A content payoff** | Does the day end with a fact about the people, the place or the plot that the player understands today, without solving a fragment and without waiting for Day 7? |
| 3 | **Earned, not handed over** | Is the answer produced by something the player does (a choice, a question, a roll, a fight)? Does failure cost something the player can see, without ever blocking the story? |
| 4 | **The pull** | Is the closing question sharper than the opening one? Does it name tomorrow's place, and a reason it cannot wait? |
| 5 | **The ladder** | Does each answer stand on yesterday's? Does the week climb? Could any day be removed? |
| 6 | **Twist quality** | Where there is a twist, is it surprising and also fair (set up earlier, obvious in hindsight)? Are twists rationed, and no two alike? |
| 7 | **Protects the Gathering** | Does Day 7 still land as the week's biggest moment? |
| 8 | **Variety** | Do adjacent days feel different to play (talk, search, chase, stealth, fight, a held breath)? Is any device overused across the season? |
| 9 | **A team adventure** | Is the party visibly there, doing things, in a game each friend plays alone? |
| 10 | **Organic** | Does each day read like a scene from a story, with its own shape, and not like a template being filled in? |
| 11 | **Fits about five minutes** | Is the day one focused scene (two at most)? Is it overloaded, or thin? |
| 12 | **Canon and logic** | Consistent with the hidden truth, with what the party knows on that day, with travel time, and with Garrick never lying? |
| 13 | **A win each day** | The party is "a step behind" for five weeks by design. Does each day still feel like progress and not futility? |
| 14 | **Someone to care about** | Is there a person, or a human detail, in the day that a player would repeat to a friend? |
| 15 | **Back tomorrow** | Put yourself in a player who has just finished this day, is tired, and could stop here. Does today's payoff feel satisfying enough that they feel the day was worth it? Does the closing question make them want tomorrow's chapter, as against feeling they have to? For every day, say which pull is strong, which is weak, and why. Check that cliffhangers are not all the same kind. |

# Part 2. What the reviewer needs to know

## The game

Tales of the Dark Moon is a story game played inside a chat app by a fixed party of two to six friends. A game master AI ("MYTHIOS") runs it. A season is six episodes. An episode is one real week:

- **Days 1 to 6 (weekdays): one chapter a day, played alone, whenever each friend has time.** Each friend plays the same chapter privately with the game master. Friends see each other's results in a shared party chat and a ticker, but they do not play at the same moment.
- **Day 7 (the Gathering): played live, together.** A battle, then a Lore Reveal (a page of the enemy's scripture), a Twist that overturns what the party believed, and a Cliffhanger that opens next week.

A weekday chapter should take **about five minutes** (five to nine; never more than ten). It is one focused scene, with a clear end.

**What a player can do in a chapter:**
- **Look / Choice:** pick what to examine or which lead to take. No roll.
- **Talk:** ask a character a question. No roll.
- **Challenge:** a dice roll against a difficulty, offered through two of the six Attributes (Might, Agility, Intellect, Charm, Craft, Aim), so there are two ways to do it. The stake is stated before the roll. **Failure never blocks the story;** it costs time, a bruise, a worse position or a lost detail.
- **Fight:** an automatic, round-by-round combat that plays out one beat at a time. About two weekday fights a week, where the story allows.
- **Seeker Fragment:** one is found each day, by an act and never by a roll. See below.

Every chapter ends with **the Pull** (one line: where the party goes tomorrow and why it cannot wait) and a short summary card.

## What TV (the designer) has ruled

1. **No filler days.** Every day has a mission and a payoff, and a twist where one can be earned.
2. **The spine of every day is three lines:** today's question; today's answer (the payoff); the new question that pulls the party to tomorrow's place. It should work like a good investigative drama: small payoffs, reveals and turns on the way to the bigger mystery.
3. **The payoff is content, not the fragment.** A fragment is too cryptic to pay off a day.
4. **It must read as a team adventure,** though each friend plays alone. The party is in every scene. Friends are named for what they do, never for what they say (the game cannot put words in a real friend's mouth). At each played beat the act is the player's own.
5. **It must feel organic.** The beats are a checklist for the writer, never a visible sequence. Days differ in which beat leads and how long they run.
6. **Garrick never lies.** No new line for him is written without TV's approval.
7. **Never reassure a player before a risk.**

## Fragments

Each day the party finds one **Seeker Fragment**: a cold sliver carrying one sentence written centuries ago by the Seekers. It arrives locked; a player solves a small puzzle to read it, perhaps hours later, perhaps not until the weekend. The six fragments of a week fit together on Day 6 into a **map** to the Gathering's location. Fragments are slow clues: their meaning lands at the Gathering or later, and several are written to be misread. They are listed in Part 5. **A fragment must never be the answer to a day's question.**

| 4 The Water Stone | The Order wants children's blood, and wants something from us. | Where is the fourth child, and why are we still alive? | It was never the children. ## The hidden truth (writer-facing; the party must not learn it early)

- **Garrick,** the innkeeper who guides the party, leads the Order. He is the heir of the king's household. He is warm, dry and likeable, and he never says a false sentence. He misleads by saying true things that the party hears wrongly.
- **Meira** was a scholar of the Seekers who drank at his inn for two years. She found the four places where the Seekers once found the Stones, and listed the party (Seeker descendants) and the marked children living near those places, out of worry. Garrick's people killed her and used her notes. Her shorthand can be read only by Garrick, so the trail runs through him.
- **Why the party is alive:** only Seeker blood can open the Seekers' fragments and maps. The Order cannot find a Stone until the party's six fragments make a map. The Week 1 ambush was staged to be lost: it put the list in each player's hands so that they would come to the inn believing they chose to.
- **How the Order "always gets there first":** its watchers shadow the party. In Weeks 1 to 4 the map is read aloud on Day 6; a watcher hears; the Order moves the held child to the Stone and wakes it before the party arrives. Garrick never tells anyone where. The party is hunting people who are waiting for them.
- **The Order holds each child near that week's site ahead of time.** It needs each child "only the once", and it does not kill.
- **The faithful cannot lie.** So no member of the Order may be questioned on a weekday: a truthful answer would break the season. Weekday enemies from the Order fight, and get away. The first one questioned is at the Week 3 Gathering.
- **"Moon-blood"** is read by the party (and by Meira, honestly and wrongly) as "the marked". It is the title of the king's house. Garrick is moon-blood.

## The six beats a writer checks (not a visible order)

Opening (where, what for, what clock) · Look (a Choice or a Talk) · Challenge (stake first, two Attributes as two ways, a visible cost on failure) · Action (a fight on about two days a week; otherwise a chase, a standoff, a crossing, a watch) · Payoff (today's answer) · Hook (the Pull).

# Part 3. The ladder (version 3)

**How to read an entry.** *Question* is what the player wants to know as the day opens. *Player does* lists the played beats; "fight" is marked where there is one. *Answer* is the content payoff. *Twist* appears only where there is one. *Pull* is the new question and tomorrow's place. *Takes* is what the party walks away holding that the Order cannot take back (an object, a deduction, an ally, an advantage). *Change* says how the day differs from the script as it stands today, and what version 2 changed after the outside reviews.

**Three rules added in version 2.**
1. **The Order takes the person; the party takes the evidence; the evidence adds up.** Every day ends with something in the party's hands. On some days the party makes the Order react.
2. **One paid beat a day.** A day has a Challenge or a fight, and only rarely both. This keeps days to one focused scene.
3. **The weekday shows; the Gathering names.** A weekday may show what the party sees. It does not state the conclusion that the Gathering exists to deliver.
4. **More game per minute (TV, 4 Oct 2026).** Each day carries a "notice" line that depends on the player's highest Attribute; a passed roll changes the player's position in the next beat; fights run at about two a week where the story allows; the summary card shows growth and what gold can buy. See Core Rules §1.

Every Garrick line quoted below already exists in the approved scripts. **This ladder proposes no new Garrick lines.**

## Week 1. The List

*The week's climb: who attacked me → who wrote the list → whose rubbing → who took Tamsin → it has happened before → where she is.*

**Day 1. The forest road** (played alone; the tutorial) · fight
- **Question:** Who is this man, and what does he want with me?
- **Player does:** Fights a lone attacker who never says a word and runs. Chooses what to look at first: where he stood, or the box he was at. Challenge on the jammed lock (the class's own Attribute, or a second); the stake is daylight on a road he may come back down.
- **Answer:** He was not robbing you. Nothing is missing. Something has been put in: a list of [N] names in a neat hand, yours among them, and under them the name of an inn.
- **Twist:** The lock was forced to leave something, not to take something.
- **Pull:** Who are the other names, and who wants you at that inn? → The Hollow Wake Inn, a day north.
- **Takes:** The list, and a half-burned charcoal rubbing he dropped.
- **Change:** Sharpened. Unchanged in version 2. The one day with both a fight and a Challenge, because it teaches both.

**Day 2. The Hollow Wake Inn**
- **Question:** Who are the others on the list, and who wrote it?
- **Player does:** Walks into a taproom where every stranger is holding the same page. Asks the innkeeper (a Talk, answered by Garrick's existing lines about Meira). Challenge (Intellect or Craft): fit your own page to the torn stubs at the back of her notebook; on a pass you also catch the one line nobody at the table can read. The table then asks Garrick to guide them; he agrees *"As far as the Widow's Tooth. That's all I'm agreeing to."*
- **Answer:** The list is Meira's. She was a scholar who sat in this room most nights for two years and has not been back in three weeks. She wrote all your names. Every one of you was set upon on the road within the same few days, and every one of you came away holding a page from this notebook.
- **Twist:** The last thing she wrote that anyone can read names none of you. It is about a child, taken, and held near a rock called the Widow's Tooth.
- **Pull:** Is the child still there? → The Widow's Tooth, two hours north. Only the innkeeper can read the way.
- **Takes:** Meira's satchel and notebook; a company of [N]; a guide.
- **Change:** Sharpened. **Version 2 fixes a mistake:** the first version said the pages were "delivered at knifepoint", which nobody in the room could know. It now says only what each of them lived through.

**Day 3. The Widow's Tooth** · fight
- **Question:** Is the child still at the rock?
- **Player does:** Chooses a lead: the worn marking on the rock, or the cart-ruts (friends take the other). A man left to watch the rock breaks cover; a fight; he runs for the village. Then looks under the overhang, out of the wind.
- **Answer:** Nobody is here now. But a child was kept here once: on a dry shelf under the overhang lies a small carved horse, grey with a winter or two of weather. And this is where the rubbing came from: the scrap your attacker dropped fits the marking exactly, with charcoal still ground into its edges. Meira stood at this rock. A cart's ruts turn off toward a village whose smoke shows past the trees.
- **Twist:** The man who came at you on the forest road was carrying her work.
- **Pull:** Whose toy is this, and how did her rubbing come into his hands? The lookout is ahead of you now, running to shut every door. → Fenmark.
- **Takes:** The rubbing matched to its rock. A child's carved horse, to ask about.
- **Change:** Reworked. Version 3: three second-round reviewers still found the payoff thin ("nobody is here"). The carved horse gives the day a find, and it pays off on Day 5. *New facts 2 and 21.*

**Day 4. Fenmark**
- **Question:** What happened in this village, and why will nobody say?
- **Player does:** Finds a meal left warm behind the well. Challenge (Agility or Charm) to find who left it: a boy of fourteen, hiding. Wins his trust (a Talk; whether he was cornered or came out to you decides how the next beat opens). Then a standoff at the well with the elder's men. No roll; nobody draws.
- **Answer:** Three nights ago, men came in the dark for a girl called Tamsin. No torches, not a word wasted, "like they'd done it before". One carried a curved, black-handled knife. The one who saw the boy watching told him, calmly, that they would know if he talked.
- **Twist:** The elder who blocks you is not one of them. He is frightened for the village, and it is he who gives you the lead, under his breath: a woman two hours east has seen that blade before.
- **Pull:** The man who ran through here this morning took the east path. He is heading for the only other person who has seen that knife. → The old charcoal camp, tonight.
- **Takes:** A name, Tamsin. A description of the taker. A witness to protect.
- **Change:** Sharpened. Version 2: the trust beat is a Talk, not a second roll (the day was too long). Tamsin is named (*new fact 3*; all six reviewers agreed). The pull now has a clock, built from the Day 3 lookout.

**Day 5. The old charcoal camp** · fight
- **Question:** Who has seen the curved knife, and what happened that time?
- **Player does:** Chooses what to examine in a camp that is supposed to be abandoned (the swept step, the cold fire, the path worn too clean), and learns someone lives here and is watching. The man who ran through Fenmark is here before the party, moving round the shed in the dark; a fight; he runs. Then she steps into the firelight with an axe, having watched all of it: *"Which of you carries the curved knife?"* (a Talk: what the player says first decides how fast the axe comes down).
- **Answer:** She sees the little carved horse from the Widow's Tooth in someone's hand, and has to sit down. It was her sister's boy's. The same man took him two winters ago: same knife, same stretch of country, same time of year. He was never found.
- **Twist:** This has a calendar. And the child in Meira's notes cannot be Tamsin: Meira was gone before Tamsin was taken. She was writing about the last one.
- **Pull:** If they come at the same time of year, the time is now. Where do they take them? She walks the party to the old charcoal road and points north. → The ford.
- **Takes:** An ally who will swear to the knife and the season. The name of the child who was held at the rock. The road north.
- **Change:** Sharpened. The fight replaces the roll (TV, 4 Oct: twelve weekday fights); it also pays off Day 4's pull, which had promised a man heading for this witness and then never showed him. *New fact 26.* "North" is inside the chapter (an audit mistake, fixed). *New fact 4.*

**Day 6. The ford**
- **Question:** Where did they take her?
- **Player does:** Challenge (Aim or Intellect) to read the prints at the waterline before the current takes them. The stake is said first: on a pass you learn how she came to the water; on a failure the river takes the prints and you never will. Then lays the week's six cold scraps side by side (an act, no roll).
- **Answer:** On a pass: small prints walk to the water's edge on their own feet. She was alive and unhurt here. Either way: she was carried into the water, and the cart went on without her weight. No tracker can follow from here. But the six scraps move under your hands and settle into one thing: a map, old and exact. The place it marks bears the same mark as Meira's rubbing, and it lies the way the cart was heading. The trail you lost and the map you hold point the same way.
- **Twist:** At supper the way is read aloud twice. Across the room a courier pays for a room he does not take, and is gone within the hour.
- **Pull:** What is at the place the map marks, and is she alive when you get there? → Across the ford at first light: the Gathering.
- **Takes:** A destination the party chose. On a pass, the knowledge that she walked.
- **Change:** Reworked. Version 3 gives the roll a stake (one reviewer saw that pass and failure ended the same). *New fact 5.*

## Week 2. The Wind Stone

*The week's climb: which child is next → he was chosen → someone else tried these locks → he is alive → they know we are outside → the way in.*

**Day 1. The second name, and the mill road**
- **Question:** Three more names on the wall. Which child is next, and can we get there first?
- **Player does:** Sees Tamsin onto a carter's wagon home, still humming; she tells you what the men said when they thought she was asleep (a Talk). Garrick places it: *"I can tell you where the next name on that wall lives. That's where I'd start."* Two days on, at the last farmhouse before the mill, an old man does not put down his axe: Challenge (Charm or Intellect).
- **Answer:** The men spoke of "the mill boy". That is Corin, the second name on the wall. The party is too late to stop it: a cart with no lamps went up to the mill three nights ago and came back before dawn. But the old man saw which way it turned. Upriver.
- **Twist:** "Moving like they'd done it before": the Fenmark boy's words, from a different county. The same people.
- **Pull:** What did the family see? → The mill, an hour on at first light.
- **Takes:** A direction, from a child the party saved.
- **Change:** Reworked. Merges today's Day 1 (no play) and Day 2. Version 2: four reviewers called the first version's payoff ("you are late") a restatement. The lead now comes from Tamsin, and the answer is a direction. *New fact 6.* Garrick staying on keeps his existing line: *"Worst guests I've ever had. Good tippers, though."*

**Day 2. The mill**
- **Question:** What happened at the mill?
- **Player does:** Chooses what to examine first: the latch, the lane, or the old stone footing of the wheel (friends take the others). Challenge (Charm or Intellect) with the miller's wife, who will not talk until she is sure you are not them.
- **Answer:** Corin was taken four nights ago by a big, quiet man who did not trouble to hide his face.
- **Twist:** *"He asked for my boy by name. And he knew the night he was born — knew it better than I did."* They are not taking children. They are collecting particular ones. And cut into the wheel's footing is the shape from the rubbing.
- **Pull:** Why this boy, and why does his birth night matter? The cart tracks upriver are fresh enough to follow today. → Upriver.
- **Takes:** The first sign that the children are chosen. The mark, a second time.
- **Change:** Sharpened. Unchanged in version 2.

**Day 3. Upriver** · fight
- **Question:** Where did the cart go?
- **Player does:** Challenge (Craft or Might) where the tracks stop dead on stone; a pass finds the path from above, a failure walks straight up it. A big dog is chained across the path where it narrows; a fight (from above, the player strikes first). Later, sets a hand to a small stone lock (the player's own act).
- **Answer:** Onto a hidden path into the hillside, kept clear, kept out of sight, and kept guarded.
- **Twist:** On the path lies a stranger, a week dead, a lockpick still jammed in a carved stone housing. It is the same work as the housings your fragments come from. It would not open for him. Under your hand it opens at once. Then a pebble skitters down the slope above, and a branch is swinging with no wind.
- **Pull:** Where was he trying to get to? The path goes on past him. → Further up, to wherever it ends.
- **Takes:** A way in that the cart took. Proof that these locks open for the party and not for others.
- **Change:** Kept. **Version 2 fixes a mistake:** the first pull asked "who else is hunting these?", a question the season never answers. It now asks a question Day 4 answers.

**Day 4. What the hillside hides**
- **Question:** What is at the end of the hidden path?
- **Player does:** Challenge (Agility or Charm) at the head of a shaft. A pass gets you halfway down before anyone looks up, and you see everything: how many, where the boy is, that his hands are not yet cut. A failure meets a watchman's eyes at the top, and you see only lamplight and hooded shapes. Either way the party is driven back up one ladder, one at a time.
- **Answer:** Corin is alive: bound, in a chamber cut into the hill, in a ring of lamps, with hooded figures making something ready around him.
- **Twist:** They know you are here. The ladder is hauled away and a second lamp is lit at the foot of the shaft. Garrick, last up: *"That went about as well as it looked."*
- **Pull:** Is there another way into this hill before they finish? → The hillside, tonight.
- **Takes:** He is alive. On a pass: their number, and that there is still time.
- **Change:** Sharpened. **Version 2 fixes a mistake:** the first version's twist said outright "this is not a gang", which is what this week's Gathering reveals. The day now shows what the party sees and draws no conclusion.

**Day 5. The hillside by night** · fight
- **Question:** Who are these people, and what are they making ready?
- **Player does:** Chooses how to spend the night: walk the hill for another entrance, or go through Meira's pages by the last light (friends take the other). A lone hooded figure comes up to check the hillside. A fight; he says nothing and breaks away. The party is left holding what tore loose from his coat.
- **Answer:** A small black circle, sewn on cloth. He fought without a word, and ran. In Meira's margin, pressed hard into the paper: *"Not one of these fragments names him… every record of him scraped clean. Whoever he was, someone wanted him forgotten."* No fragment has ever given him a name. Where they speak of him at all, they say only "him".
- **Twist:** Before he is gone he lifts a shuttered lamp and flashes twice, down toward the shaft. The hill knows the party is still outside. Whatever is being made ready around Corin will not wait.
- **Pull:** There is no second door on this hillside, and now there is a clock. Last week six cold slivers found a way nobody was watching. Five are in hand. → The lip of the shaft.
- **Takes:** The black circle. The party's first piece of the enemy.
- **Change:** Reworked. A new day built from the second half of today's Day 5 and its optional extra. Version 2: the game no longer states that the forest-road attacker was one of these; it gives the evidence and leaves the player to say it. The pull was a procedure; it now has a clock. *New fact 7.*

**Day 6. The other way in**
- **Question:** Is there another way in, and how long is left?
- **Player does:** Keeps the watch: Challenge (Aim or Intellect) to read the sounds in the hill; a pass counts those moving and places them, a failure hears only that there are many. Lays the six fragments at the lip of the shaft and traces the way aloud for the friends still on watch. Then goes to the place the map marks and puts a hand to it (an act).
- **Answer:** Yes, and it is real: where the map says, under a fall of old stone, is the mouth of a second shaft, breathing cold air. It leads to an older room deeper in the same hill. Nobody is guarding it.
- **Twist:** Within the hour the hill wakes: lamps, feet, a child's voice cut short, all moving deeper. Then, far down in the rock, picks.
- **Pull:** They are digging. Reach the old room before they break through. → Into the hill by the old shaft, now: the Gathering.
- **Takes:** A door the enemy does not hold, found and tested. On a pass, how many are below.
- **Change:** Sharpened. Today there is no play at all. This week's Day 6 is a race. Version 3: the party goes and finds the shaft the map shows, so the day's answer is a place and not a drawing.

## Week 3. The Fire Stone

*The week's climb: who is the girl → she passed her own door → the mark again → she is leaving a trail they cannot see → for the first time, they ran → the hill.*

**Day 1. Somewhere warm** · fight
