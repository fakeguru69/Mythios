**TVMen's brief for these (2026-09-26):** the Gathering carries the heavy lifting of explaining *this episode's* part of the lore, clearly. It has to read like a great story, in structure and in tone — it's the payoff for the week. Reveal enough for this episode, often as a twist on what the previous one seemed to settle. Be cryptic, double-edged and clever, but never so much that a reveal turns vague. The Seeker Fragments have done the setting-up all week; the Gathering makes them click.

**How each one is built — the same story shape, five times:**
1. **Arrival** — the place, and the dread of it.
2. **The Battle** — the tangible win, and the cost hidden inside it.
3. **The quiet after** — the discovery.
4. **The reading** — the Order's scripture, read aloud like a story inside the story (in parts, if it's long).
5. **The connection** — after each part, the strongest matching fragments (about three from the week, plus any earlier line that closes here) are laid beside it, each quoted whole. Let the players and the characters make the link; the narrator doesn't do their thinking for them (GM Principles 1.9, point 3a; series audit, 2026-09-29).
6. **The turn** — the twist, clear at least in hindsight, with its key sentence in a character's mouth wherever possible.
7. **The hook** — the cliffhanger.

**Conventions:** narration in quote blocks, second person, present tense. The Cult Bible is set apart as scripture; its Layer 2 lines (the Royal Fragment) sit in bold, framed as "the older hand." `[PLANT]` marks lines a recap must keep. Challenges are in parentheses and never block the story — success and failure both move it forward. Garrick's lines are scripted: deliver them word for word (every one is audited as literally true in `garrick-deception-table.md`). The full Cult Bible passages are also kept in `mythios-cult-bible.md`; the two copies must match. **In any recap:** the party finds each Stone's hiding place, never the Stone itself; say "the socket was empty", never "they found the Stone". **If every friend falls in a Battle:** the enemy steps over you and leaves you alive; nobody is paid; the scene carries on as written, told as something you see from the floor (Core Rules §4). **[N] is the party size;** every fight is one opponent per player, so enemy counts are always [N] (GM Principles 3.8). **Every Gathering opens with the Approach and has one United Effort after the Battle (added 2026-10-03).** The Approach is a group Choice, then a Challenge taken by one volunteer in the party chat. The United Effort is one Challenge too hard for a single player, where up to two friends may each give any Attribute Relic. Its Attribute is named in that day's "Tonight" message: Might (Ep1), Intellect (Ep2), Agility (Ep3), Craft (Ep4), Charm (Ep5), Aim (Ep6). In the Approach, the group's Choice decides which Attribute the Challenge uses. In a Lore Reveal, each paragraph of a found document is its own message, and a friend taps [READ ON] for the next. One roll per friend per Gathering. **Failed Challenges cost gold only** (−150g, Core Rules §3). The earlier "carried costs" (an HP wound; the Order striking first) were retired 2026-10-03, so no failed Challenge changes how a Gathering Battle starts. **Every found document is named every time** — "the ledger", "the Order's scripture", "Meira's notes" — never a bare "it" or "the book" when two are in the scene, and never "Cult Bible" in narration (6.6). **The writing standard:** a joy to read — paint the place and the world — but never convoluted; clarity wins (6.5).

# EPISODE 2 — THE WIND STONE

*(Version 2, locked 2026-09-28 — replaces the Version 1 draft. Series audit applied 2026-09-29.)*

**What this Gathering has to land:** the king's story, told by the people who worship him. A war that ate the world, and one king left on one wall who would not kneel. The night the moon went dark, the thing his house had kept, his own blood — and by morning the enemy was gone and the world was saved. The world then cut him out of its story: every player-character grew up on the schoolbook version, where the Grey Host broke at Highwall of a fever. The people who wrote the Seeker Fragments were his soldiers on that wall. And the kidnappers are his faithful: they give the blood of *marked* children in his name, and have kept count of it for years.

**The twist on Episode 1:** Episode 1 found children's names scratched under an empty socket. Episode 2 finds out what they were: offerings, written in an account book, to a king the world tried to forget.
