# Tales of the Dark Moon: the United Effort as a story

**Status: APPROVED by TV as the pattern, 4 Oct 2026** (after five outside reviews, Part 10). The rule is now in Core Rules §5, and each job's scene and endings are in its Gathering script; the full line tables stay here. All six jobs written in full. TV approved the water-gate as the pattern ("the water lock story is good") and left two calls to me, recorded in Part 9. Not yet in Core Rules or the approved scripts; the numbers wait for the simulation.

The idea is TV's: the United Effort is told as a story in the party chat. Each beat hands the job to one friend by name. That friend rolls. The numbers and a line of story follow, and the story hands on to the next friend.

---

# Part 1. The rule, in the words the game already uses

Nothing here is a new kind of roll. It is a **Challenge** roll followed by a damage roll, written the way a fight writes them.

- **Two things each have 100,** exactly as both sides of a fight have 100 HP: the thing in the way, and the thing the party stands to lose.
- MYTHIOS names one friend. That friend gets an ordinary **Challenge card**: the night's Attribute against a **Difficulty Class**, with `[ROLL]` and, if they hold the matching Relic, `[USE]` for +4.
- **A SUCCESS** rolls damage against the thing in the way. **A FAIL** rolls damage against the thing the party stands to lose. **Damage is 10 + a d20** (11 to 30). A fight's damage is a d20 doubled; here that swung too wildly, and one roll could end a third of the job.
- **The Difficulty Class is the episode's weekday DC** (6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 9), not the old United Effort's (+12). I first wrote "weekday + 2"; the simulation showed that was too hard, because on any night most friends are Novices in that night's Attribute. See the numbers in Part 9.
- It ends when either reaches 0.
- **Who goes next is the party's choice.** The friend who has just rolled hands it on: `[TO JO]` `[TO DEV]`. Nobody goes twice until everyone has gone once. If no choice is made in ten seconds, MYTHIOS names the next friend. MYTHIOS names the first.
- **Any friend may give the roller an Attribute Relic** for +4, as in the old United Effort: the roller's own must match the Attribute, a friend's may be any. One Relic to a roll.
- A friend who fell in the Battle still takes their turn.
- **A friend alone** takes every turn, and both things start at 60, so the job is four or five rolls and not eight.
- A failed roll costs no gold. The reward is paid to every friend alike, and only if the job is done.

**The card, on a friend's turn** *(the Challenge card, with two lines added)*

```
THE WATER-GATE · United Effort · Difficulty Class 8
The Pin 78 · His Breath 100

Craft +2 (Apprentice)
You Succeed On 7+ (Roll + 2 − DC 8)                    [ROLL]

Craft Relic · you hold 1
With +4, You Succeed On 3+ (used once, on a Craft roll)  [USE]

A SUCCESS damages The Pin. A FAIL damages His Breath.
REWARD: +300g to every friend, if The Pin reaches 0
IF HIS BREATH REACHES 0: no reward, and nobody left to ask
```

**The result** *(the Challenge result line, then the fight's damage line)*

```
Mei Rolls 14 + 2 (Craft) − DC 8 = 8. SUCCESS
Damage = 10 + Roll 12. The Pin 100 → 78
```

```
Jo Rolls 4 + 0 (Craft) − DC 8 = −4. FAIL
Damage = 10 + Roll 6. His Breath 100 → 84
```

**Then the story.** After every result MYTHIOS posts one line of story and names the next friend.

- The line is chosen by **where the damaged thing now stands** (above two-thirds, the middle third, the last third, or 0), not by which turn it is. So the telling makes sense in whatever order the rolls fall.
- **A line speaks only of its own thing.** A SUCCESS line never says how the endangered thing is doing, and a FAIL line never undoes progress, so no two lines can contradict each other.
- **In a FAIL line the place does the harm,** not the friend: the board goes, the glass turns, the gate settles. The friend who rolled low is not written as the one who hurt the child.
- **Each band has two lines,** used in order. No line is ever said twice. If a band's two lines are both used, MYTHIOS posts only the numbers and the hand-on.
- Three short lines come once each **between** turns (after the second, fourth and sixth), whatever the rolls.

# Part 2. Gathering 1 · Might · THE DROP

*Replaces "lift the slab". Sits after Notice.*

## The scene

> The way to the slab is back across the gallery. Dev is three steps ahead of you when the floor gives.
>
> There is no warning. A timber that has waited fifty years goes all at once, and Dev drops through it to the chest and stops there, jammed, arms flung wide over boards that are already splitting. Below Dev's boots there is nothing but the sound of the river, a long way down in the dark.
>
> Nobody can walk out there. The boards that are holding Dev will not hold a second pair of feet.
>
> A rope is no use. Dev's two arms are all that is holding Dev up, and neither can let go to catch one.
>
> One of you can go out flat, on your belly, with the rest standing on the stone at the gallery's edge and holding your ankles, and haul. An arm has one haul like that in it. Then the next of you.
>
> And Dev, jammed in the boards, can do more than hang there. Every elbow got onto sounder wood, every hand's breadth won from below, counts as much as a haul from above.

*Dev is the friend who fell in the Battle; if nobody fell, the friend who finished it with the least HP.*

*Alone:*
> It is you. You are jammed to the chest over the dark, and nobody is coming. You get an elbow onto sounder wood, a hand's breadth at a time.

**The two things:** The Drop 100 · Dev HP 100

> **Mei. You are lightest on your feet tonight. Flat on your belly. Go.**

## Between turns

- *After the second:* Somewhere behind you, out in the air, a counting song is still going.
- *After the fourth:* Below Dev's boots, the river goes on saying the same thing.

## How it ends

**The Drop reaches 0**
> You go round by the wall after that, one at a time, where the stone carries the boards.
>
> Behind the place where they held her, the tunnel ends in a slab of fallen roof as big as a barn door. Cold air breathes round its edges from a room beyond. It takes all of you, and it goes up in one heave. The room beyond has not been walked in: the dust lies as it fell.

**Dev's HP reaches 0**
> You find Dev where the river brings out what the mine drops: at the drowned mouth, grey, coughing, wrapped round a pit-prop, alive.
>
> It is an hour before all of you are together again, and another before the hole is bridged with what timber is left. By the time the slab at the tunnel's end is up, every one of you has been in and out of that room a dozen times, and the floor is a mess of your own bootprints.
>
> Whatever the dust in there could have told you, it will not tell you now.

## Why it holds up

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why the floor, and not the roof? | The Approach already says the gallery is "floored with timbers nobody has trusted in fifty years". The roof props are new; a notice line says so. |
| Why not all pull at once? | The boards round the hole will not take a second body. One goes out flat; the rest stand on stone and are the anchor. |
| Why not throw a rope? | Dev's arms are what is holding Dev up. Neither can let go. |
| Why does progress stay? | Each gain puts more of Dev's weight on sounder wood. |
| Why does a fail hurt Dev? | Every movement out there breaks more of the boards Dev is jammed in, and the broken ends are what do the damage. A fail never undoes a gain; it hurts Dev where Dev hangs. |
| What is zero? | Dev falls to the river and is washed out at the drowned mouth: hurt, alive. The clue in the dust (when the thing was taken) is lost for good. |

# Part 3. Gathering 2 · Intellect · THE BREACH

*Replaces "get the scorched pages apart". Sits after the black book is found, before the Verse.*

## The scene

> You have the black book in your hands when the far wall begins to sound.
>
> It comes from the hole they dug: boots, a great many, and lamplight growing in it like water rising in a pipe. They left with what they came for. Now they are coming back for what they left behind: their dead, and their book.
>
> There are more lamps in that tunnel than you can count. This is not a fight.
>
> But they cut that tunnel in two nights, through ground nobody had disturbed in a thousand years, and it is standing on green timber and hope. Take out the right prop, and the hill will shut it for you. Take out the wrong one, and the fall comes down on your side of it.
>
> Every fall changes what is holding what. So one of you looks, and calls it, and the nearest boot does the rest. Then the next of you looks again.

**The two things:** The Tunnel 100 · The Room 100

> **Mei. Take the lamp. Which one is holding it up?**

## The story, on a SUCCESS

| The Tunnel | Story |
|---|---|
| 67 to 99 | You see it: not the big prop, the little wedge under it. It goes with one kick, and a cart-load of hill sits down in the tunnel's mouth. |
| | The cross-piece. It was carrying nothing until the first fall; now it is carrying everything. Out it comes. The lamplight in the hole narrows to a slot. |
| 34 to 66 | Left-hand post, low down. The roof behind it comes down in a long soft rush, and the boots on the far side stop. |
| | You call it, and you are right. Somewhere in the dust, on their side, a man is coughing. |
| 1 to 33 | One frame is holding all of it now. You can see which leg. |
| | The slot is a hand wide. A lamp is pushed up to it from the other side, and an eye. |
| 0 | The last leg goes. The hill shuts like a book. The dust takes a long time to settle, and when it has there is no hole, no light, and no sound from the other side at all. |

## The story, on a FAIL

| The Room | Story |
|---|---|
| 67 to 99 | The big prop, the obvious one. It was holding up your side. A slab of the old wall leans out and comes down across the floor, and one of the lamps is under it. |
| | The roof over the hole stays where it is. The roof over your heads lets go a barrow-load of stone. |
| 34 to 66 | The fall comes your way. When the dust thins, the far end of the room is rubble, and the stone table is the edge of what is left. |
| | A crack runs across the ceiling from the hole to the stair, as quick as a lizard. |
| 1 to 33 | There is room to stand, and not much more. Everything you have not got in your hands is under stone. |
| | The foot of the stair is going. |
| 0 | The room comes in. |

## The hand-on

> Mei steps back and hands on the lamp. **Jo. Look again. It has all changed.**

> Dust, and less room than there was. The lamp is passed. **Jo.**

*Both in the last third:*
> One more fall will shut their tunnel. One more wrong call will shut this room. **Jo.**

## Between turns

- *After the second:* Corin has put both hands over his ears.
- *After the fourth:* On the stone table, the lamps the fight kicked over are guttering.
- *After the sixth:* Whoever is coming is not hurrying. They have never needed to.

## How it ends

**The Tunnel reaches 0**
> The room is yours.
>
> The scorched corner of the book has fused a dozen leaves into one brittle black wafer. There is time, now, to get them apart whole.

*The Verse is read in order, in the old room, as written.*

**The Room reaches 0**
> You are on the soft stair with the boy before you have decided to go, and what was the room is filling up behind you. The last thing you see in it is lamplight coming through the hole, and patient hands beginning to clear the stone.
>
> At the lip of the old shaft, in the night air, you count what you came out with. The black book is in three pieces inside somebody's coat. And Corin is holding something out to you with both hands: a book bound in plain oiled leather. He took it from the niche by the lamp as you carried him past.

*The Verse comes out as three loose clumps, which the table puts in order, and is read on the hillside. The ledger scene follows there.*

## Why it holds up

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why not fight them? | There are more than the party can count. The fight system gives each friend one enemy; this is a tide. They do not want the party dead, only out. |
| Why not just leave? | The party can, and at zero it has to. Staying is how it keeps the room, the book whole, and the reward. |
| Why one at a time? | Each fall moves the load. The next friend has to read it afresh. |
| Why does progress stay? | A fallen tunnel stays fallen. |
| Why does a fail cost? | The same timbering holds up both sides. A wrong prop brings the fall down on the party's side, and the room gets smaller. (The second draft had "Your Lead", an abstraction that nothing could plausibly damage. Two reviewers caught it.) |
| What is zero? | The party is driven out, with the book torn and the room lost. **This is the mildest of the six**, and I have not found an honest way to make it bite harder: nothing in this room can be lost without breaking Week 3. |

*Replaces "take him alive". The last of the faithful has set his blade down; he lets himself be bound, and there is no roll in it. Sits where the party first looks for Wren.*

## The scene

> The girl is not by the table.

## Why it holds up

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why is she up there? | A frightened child runs from a fight toward daylight. The scree is the Approach's own: "only waiting for a reason to move". |
| Why not wait, or call her down? | The slope is already moving under her. |
| Why one at a time? | The glass will not bear two. |
| Why does progress stay? | The rope is made fast at each stretch. |
| Why does a fail hurt her? | The slope is one sheet, and it is already going. Any weight on it that does not find a firm place moves all of it, and she is on it. The lines make the glass the one that does it, not the friend. |
| What is zero? | She comes down in the slide: alive, cut in a dozen places. In Week 4 Day 1 she is carried home, not walked. Nothing later changes; the party will remember it. |
| What it costs the approved night | "Two more questions if the captive is unhurt" is gone. The board is one question each. |

# Part 5. Gathering 4 · Craft · THE WATER-GATE

*Replaces "the lock on Joss's chain". Sits after Joss, the socket and the oars; before Notice and the board.*

## The scene before it

> The boy on the table is alive. Joss. The cut on his palm is still bleeding through its linen. In his good hand he holds a little wooden boat, no longer than a finger, and he doesn't let go of it even while you lift him down.
>
> A chain runs from his ankle to a ring in the drowned floor. The lock on it is older than he is, and mostly rust. It breaks under the flat of a blade.
>
> Behind him, the socket.
>
> Empty — and this time the dust at its rim hasn't settled. It's still falling, a faint grey haze drifting down through the lamplight.

## The scene

> From the far door comes a sound like a bell dropped down a well.
>
> It is the old water-gate: an iron grille as wide as the doorway, hung up out of the way on a rope for longer than anyone has been alive. The last of them cut that rope as he went under it, so that nobody could follow.
>
> He was not quite fast enough.
>
> He is on his back in the doorway with the gate across his legs, in water that would cover him if he lay flat. He is holding himself up on his elbows. His chin is just clear.
>
> Beyond the bars, on the black water: oars, pulling hard, going away. A boat, with a black circle painted on its prow. Then only the sound of it.
>

> You have known since the Kiln what his kind are. *The faithful shall not lie.* He is the only one left who can answer you.

> The gate cannot be lifted. It came down crooked, and one corner has jammed in the groove it runs in. Heave at it, and the other corner grinds down on him.
>
> What holds it is a single iron pin, low in the groove, under the water. Work the pin out, and the gate will come up like a lid.
>
> There is room at it for one pair of hands, and for as long as one breath lasts.

**The two things:** The Pin 100 · His Breath 100

> Two of you take his shoulders. Somebody finds a knife with a good point.
>
> **Mei. You are nearest. One breath.**

*Alone:*
> You get his shoulders across your knees. One hand for him, one for the knife. One breath.

| The Pin | Story |

| 67 to 99 | Your fingers find it in the dark: a pin of iron as thick as a thumb, furred with rust. You get the knife's point under its head and lean. Something gives, a grain at a time. Then your chest is burning, and you are up. |

> Mei has nothing left. She holds the knife out, hilt first. **Jo. One breath.**

> Mei comes up shaking her head, and holds out the knife. **Jo.**

*Both in the last third:*

## Between turns

- *After the second:* Out on the lake the oars are fainter. Whatever they carried out of here, it is not coming back.
- *After the fourth:* From the table, Joss is watching. He has the little boat held so tight that his knuckles are white.
- *After the sixth:* The man under the gate has not once asked you to hurry.

## How it ends

**The Pin reaches 0**
> For a moment nothing moves.
>
> Then every one of you has a hand on the bars, and the gate comes up out of its groove with a groan, and he is dragged clear by the collar, coughing black water.
>
> He does not thank you.
>

*He is the captive, and he answers all night.*

**His Breath reaches 0**
> You get the pin out in the end. It takes all of you, and it takes too long.
>
> When the gate comes up, he comes with it like a sack.
>

>
> Tied at his belt, sealed in a waxed pouch: pages. More of the Order's scripture — from a book you haven't seen before.

*There is no board tonight. The pen is still found in the pouch, and the pen says enough.*

## Why it holds up

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why not just lift the gate? | It is jammed crooked. Lifting drives the far corner down on him. The story says so before the first roll. |
| Why one at a time? | The pin is under water, with room for one pair of hands. A breath lasts as long as it lasts. |
| Why does progress stay? | A pin worked part-way out of its hole stays part-way out. |
| Why does a fail hurt him? | A slip puts weight on the gate; it settles; it drags him under. |
| What is everyone else doing? | Two hold his shoulders. The rest hold the lamp low, and wait for the knife. |
| Why save him at all? | He cannot lie, and he is the last one left to ask. |
| What is zero? | He is alive and senseless. Every answer he had is lost, including what his people did to Meira. Nothing later depends on those answers. |
| What about the Stone? | The boat leaves as it always did. The gate is why nobody can follow. |

# Part 10. What five outside reviews changed (4 Oct)

All five say the shared job is a clear improvement on the old United Effort and should be kept. All five rank the water-gate first or second, and the scree and the drop with it. All five rank the breach and the hawser last. None found a leak of the hidden truth.

**Real mistakes, fixed**
1. **Lines could contradict each other** (two reviewers). In Gathering 1 a first-roll FAIL said Dev "drops the hand's breadth you had won" when nothing had been won, and "is lower than before". Rule added: a line speaks only of its own thing. Gathering 1's FAIL lines are rewritten to hurt Dev without moving Dev.
2. **A low roller was written as the one who cut the child** (all five). "What you kicked loose goes past her like a thrown handful of knives." Rule added: in a FAIL line the place does the harm. Gathering 3's FAIL lines are rewritten.
3. **"Your Lead" in Gathering 2 was an abstraction nothing could damage** (two reviewers). It is now The Room, and a wrong prop brings the fall down on the party's side.
4. **Damage swung too wildly** (four reviewers). A d20 doubled could end a third of the job in one roll. Now 10 + a d20. In the simulation that halves the number of jobs that end in five rolls or fewer (19% to 10%) and leaves the win rate the same.
5. **The DC on my example card did not match Core Rules** (one reviewer). The old United Effort is the weekday DC + 12. This job uses the Gathering Challenge's + 2. Now stated.
6. **Gathering 1: why not throw a rope, and are the ankle-holders on the boards too?** (three reviewers). Both answered in the scene.
7. **Alone, the scenes did not work** (two reviewers): nobody to hold the ankles, the shoulders or the line. Each has a line for a friend alone, and alone the job is shorter (60).
8. **Gathering 6:** "Your Ground" renamed "Your Line"; "They are on you" no longer contradicts the ending.
9. **Two lines too long or too faint for a phone:** the water-gate's middle FAIL line is cut to two sentences; the roll-call's first FAIL line now has a lantern in it.

**Taken**
- **The party chooses who goes next** (from two reviewers' worry that the game forces the weakest roller forward). The friend who has just rolled hands it on. It is the hand-on made into a decision, and it lets the table save its best hands for the end.

- **Gathering 5's zero leaves a mark:** Week 6 Day 1 is harder.
