# Meira's Notes — Registry

**What this document is:** the tracking record for Meira's notes, the third of the three in-world found documents (alongside `mythios-fragment-registry.md` for Seeker Fragments and `mythios-cult-bible.md` for the Cult Bible). Created 2026-09-23 as part of the same pass that clarified what each document actually is and isn't.

**What Meira's notes are (rule of 2026-09-23, widened 2026-09-26 by TVMen's direction on her research):** her commentary on the Seeker Fragments, plus her own fieldwork (below). No independent research tradition beyond that, no access to the Cult Bible or any scrap of the true ancient text (she couldn't have — the map-fragment puzzles are blood-locked to the Seeker bloodline, and she isn't one). Her own fieldwork is the other half of what's in her satchel, but the *lore content* specifically is always her reading of a specific fragment. **What her fieldwork is (confirmed 2026-09-26):** the four find-sites — where the Seekers first found each elemental shard, the part of their story that survived in old records and place-names — written in her private shorthand; her rubbing of the shard outline the Seekers carved at the Earth find-site (the Ep1 Day 1 rubbing); the marked children living near those places, listed out of worry; the undated takings she came across there; and **the Seekers' living descendants — the party — each with the night they were born, every one a night the moon went dark** (added 2026-09-28, season audit H4: only the Seekers' descendants born marked can open the Seekers' ways, which is why she chose them). See `mythios-mythology-canon.md`, "What Meira's research was."

**Why her theory undercounts, restated plainly (rewritten 2026-09-26 to match the find-site logic):** she read every fragment she had access to, including Fragment 1's "Five fell... four scattered after, one stayed close." She wasn't kept in the dark. But her research was the Seekers' *search* — where they found each shard — and she located four find-sites, no more. The Seekers never *found* the fifth; they took it from the king's hand, so no record anywhere gives it a find-site. For most of its life it was an heirloom nobody knew about, and after the sealing it sat in the dead ruins of the old capital, where no one lived to remember anything. She has the word "five," with nothing to chase — so she honestly dismissed it as a poet's count (her Ep3 footnote). She could also never follow any trail past its start: the fragment at each find-site opens only for Seeker blood. Her notes say where each trail *begins*; only the party can walk it.

**A structural limit worth remembering:** the 36 Seeker Fragments the party finds this season are being recovered fresh, via the party's own Seeker-blood-locked puzzle-solving. Meira could theorize about a handful of older, previously-surfaced fragments or fragment-echoes in older scholarship, but she never held most of what the party will find. Her notes predate and are largely independent of the season's own discoveries — which is also why she can be dead before Episode 1 even starts, and her notes still matter: they're a snapshot of partial, honest, pre-season scholarship, not a real-time commentary track.

**Her second job, added 2026-09-23 — she is the honest source of the season's biggest misreading.** Meira writes in the modern, drifted sense of the old words. So when an old line says "moon-blood," she reads it the way everyone now does — as a fancier word for a moonchild. She isn't wrong about how the word is used today; she's wrong about what the Seekers meant by it. That one honest gloss is what lets the party read the Royal Fragment's two different words ("marked" for the four, "moon-blood" for the fifth) as one — see `mythios-season-reveal-map.md`, Thread 1. Garrick never corrects her, and never has to: nobody asks him to.
