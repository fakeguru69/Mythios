## The Refrains

Two pieces of in-world folklore, established, not invented by any character — they recur throughout fragments and dialogue:

- **"The power that gives is also the power that takes."** — about the *activation* itself.
- **"The key that locks is also the key that unlocks."** — about the *concealment/reversal* mechanism.

**Both first appear in Episode 1 (TVMen, 2026-09-27):** Refrain 1 is the Day 5 fragment — and the woman at the old charcoal camp says it aloud first, as a saying she's always known; Refrain 2 is the Day 6 fragment. From there they recur, and both pay off at the finale.

**Where they recur (placed 2026-09-23; day slots updated 2026-09-28 per the full fragment relook):** Refrain 1 quoted whole at the Ep3 Gathering, where the corruption is revealed (moved there 2026-09-28), and echoed (trimmed) in the Seekers' Ep5 Day 1 fragment ("He gave us the war. Then it began to take."); Refrain 2 echoed (also trimmed) in the Seekers' Ep4 Day 5 fragment ("We have built a lock. Pray no one ever learns it is also a door."). Both Refrains are quoted in full again at the Ep6 Gathering, Refrain 2 alongside Garrick's "Same key. Turned the other way." *(An Ep6 Day 5 use was cut on review — too loud a completion warning in the finale week.)* They're folk sayings the Seekers use, not ancient-text quotations — so Seeker Fragments may carry them.

**The Order's own liturgical close (not a Refrain — the Order's prayer):** *"Until the throne rise whole."* Ends every Cult Bible passage Ep2-5; the Ep4 torn page stops one breath before it; Ep6's untorn page completes the sentence with it. See `mythios-cult-bible.md`.

## The Origin (deep past)

1. Legend: the blood of a **moonchild** (born on a full-moon eclipse night) can unleash great power.
2. **The shards are older than any kingdom.** They are fragments of the moon itself, broken free in a cataclysm lost even to the oldest histories, scattered across the world when it happened. Moonchild blood — blood marked by the moon at its darkest, most consumed hour — resonates with them because it carries the same mark the shards do.
3. **Four of the five shards landed unclaimed** — the elemental shards (Earth/Wind/Fire/Water). Any moonchild's blood wakes them, every time, with no memory of who came before. **The fifth — the blood shard — fell into what would become Malakor's family line, generations before him**, kept as an heirloom whose true nature nobody living still remembered. It is why the fifth "stayed close" while the other four scattered to the corners of the world — it never needed finding, because it was never lost.
4. **The concrete incident:** Malakor's kingdom was in the last days of a losing war — the enemy had broken his last defenses, and the following dawn was set to be the end, his capital's walls falling with no army left to stop it. That night happened to fall on a full-moon eclipse. Cornered, with nothing left to lose, he turned to the one strange, unremarkable heirloom his family had kept for generations without ever knowing its purpose — old, never to be sold or lost, that was all anyone had passed down about it — alongside a handful of his family's own half-surviving pages describing it, just enough for him to guess it might do *something* if given blood. He cut his palm that night, certain he had nothing left to lose either way. It answered. **What went out from him that night did to the enemy camp, all at once, what it would later do to his own people slowly: it emptied them.** Every man in the camp rose in the dark — no fear, no will, nothing of himself left — walked out past the fires into the night, and did not stop. By morning the fires were cold, the tents stood empty and the horses wandered loose; no one ever found where the men went. *(Confirmed 2026-09-26. This is the same power as the corruption in item 5 — the victory and the rot were one thing. It is also Refrain 1 made literal: the power gave him the war by taking the enemy, then kept on taking.)* The shard, merely kept until then, **bonded** to him at that moment — and to his line alone, permanently. He never possessed the four elemental shards; he never needed to.
5. With that power he won his war — but the power, drawn through a single unmediated channel, corrupted him. **Concretely, not vaguely:** everyone who served closest to him began to change. Soldiers stopped feeling wounds, stopped fearing death, stopped needing sleep or food — and grew quietly, visibly wrong, less and less themselves the longer they stood near him. The ground under his standing armies stopped growing anything living. It wasn't tyranny in the ordinary sense — his own people were becoming a slow, spreading extension of the same undeath he'd unlocked in himself, with no sign it would stop at his court. **This is what the Seekers actually moved against — not a distant threat, but people they'd fought beside, hollowing out in front of them.**
6. **A band of heroes, also moonchildren, known as the Seekers** *(their number is never stated to players — TVMen, 2026-09-28: "the Six" is retired, since party size varies)*, searched the kingdom and found the **four elemental shards** — the blood shard was never lost to find; it was still with Malakor. Each elemental shard lay where it had fallen when the moon broke — its **find-site** — sometimes still marked by the fall: the Earth shard lay in a rock it had split clean down the middle (the Widow's Tooth, Ep1); the Wind shard in the ground where the mill now stands (Ep2); the Fire shard on **the Blackglass**, a shoulder of hillside above the village of Ashcombe where the rock ran like wax and cooled into black glass (Ep3). *(Find-sites confirmed 2026-09-26; Fire set 2026-09-28 with the Ep3 daily beats — its hiding place is the Seekers' room beneath the Kiln, a black hill nearby. Water set the same day with the Ep4 daily beats: **the drowned tower**, Old Reedham's bell tower standing out of the Stillwater, a lake in the north; its hiding place is the flooded undercroft beneath Old Reedham, reached through the drowned streets that stand in the shallows in a dry year.)*
6a. **The frozen soldiers (added 2026-09-28, from the new Ep5 Day 4 fragment):** when Malakor fell in the battle with the Seekers, the soldiers nearest him at that instant stopped where they were, mid-stride, and never moved again — not dead, not alive, simply stopped. The Seekers couldn't bring themselves to break them, so they left them standing where they froze. This is why "the dead near him stop standing still" as the four elemental shards wake matters: the awakening doesn't just stir Malakor, it starts to unfreeze what his fall left frozen. *(Replaces the shelved line "He sleeps. It is not death..." at this slot — see `review-fragments.md`.)*
7. The Seekers possessed the ancient text **in full** — not the partial reading Malakor had worked from. It held out a second, deliberate instruction: the 5 shards, assembled and fed blood together — the 4 elemental shards each answering to a different moonchild's blood, the bonded blood shard answering only to the King's own — would not feed the power further. They would **bind** it. In the battle with Malakor, the Seekers wounded him and took his blood from the wound. Four Seekers gave their blood to the four gathered elemental shards; Malakor's own blood — the only blood the bonded shard would ever answer — went to the blood shard he still carried. The power turned against him. *(Corrected 2026-09-26: this line used to say his blood was "**taken by force**," in bold — a leftover from the retired willing-vs-forced mechanic. How the blood is given plays no part in the ritual. What matters is only whose blood it is and what state Malakor is in — see "The mechanic behind both Refrains," below.)*
8. Malakor was placed under **the Spell of the Undying Sleep** — a state that resembles death but is never true death.
9. Only now, for the first time, were all 5 shards together in one place — and only now did the Seekers hide the blood shard away from the royal line at all. They hid all 5 in 5 separate locations — the four elemental shards scattered far apart, the blood shard kept close, in the wall room at Highwall, where they could watch it and him (the Verse of the Binding: "The fifth they did not scatter with the others"; clarified 2026-10-03 after Audit C) — each recorded on a map broken into 6 fragments, locked by a magical puzzle whose answers are encrypted in blood and passed only through the Seeker bloodline. **How each trail is laid (confirmed 2026-09-26):** each of the four trails begins at its shard's find-site — the first fragment is left there, the rest lead on from it, and all six together show the hiding place. The party can pick fragments up in a different order from the map's own (in Ep1 they pick up two — on the forest road and at the inn — before reaching the Widow's Tooth, where the first one waits). At each find-site the Seekers also carved the outline of the shard that had lain there — the marking worn into the Widow's Tooth (Ep1 Day 3) is one, and the Ep1 Day 1 rubbing is Meira's copy of it. **The fifth has no find-site** — the Seekers never found it; they took it from the king's hand — so its trail begins at their own place: the stretch of the capital's wall they held on the war's last night. It ends at a Seekers' watch-post in the ruins, within sight of the sealed hall.
10. Centuries pass. "Shard" — the Seekers' own word, and the word of the ancient text — wears down over the centuries into "Stone," the word everyone uses now.

**What Meira's research was, and why it stopped at four (rewritten 2026-09-26, following TVMen's direction):** Meira was a historian of the Seekers — their search as much as their sealing. Where the Seekers *found* the four shards is the part of their story that survived, in old records and place-names, and she located all four find-sites. That is what her notes are: four places, written in the private shorthand only Garrick can read (he listened to her talk them over at his bar for two years — she took the innkeeper for furniture, which is why his "I doubt she thought I was listening" is true). She could never go further. The fragment waiting at each find-site opens only for Seeker blood, and only all six of a trail together show where the shard was hidden. So she honestly concluded that four shards exist — four find-sites — and treated Fragment 1's "five" as a poet's count: the Seekers never *found* the fifth, they took it from the king's hand, so no find-site for it exists in any record. The blood shard's own history hid it too: for most of its life it was an heirloom nobody knew about, and after the sealing the Seekers kept it in the dead ruins of the old capital, where no one lived to remember anything. Garrick's family always knew it existed and wanted it back; what they lost was its location. This is a structural blind spot, not an oversight of hers.

**Her readable pages carry two more things.** First, the marked children living near those four places, listed out of worry — she knew marked children were being taken in that country. Second, the takings she came across there, undated — such as "a child, taken, held somewhere near that rock" (Ep1 Day 2). The charcoal woman's nephew, taken two winters back (Ep1 Day 5), is one of these. The Order has drawn marked children for its offerings for years; the Ep2 ledger shows it. *(This settles the Ep1 timeline question: her note records a past taking, and the party reads it as current.)*

**What happened to the children the Order gave over the years, before this season (decided 2026-09-28 — season audit clarity item 7):** for generations the practice was to take a marked child, give their blood in the king's name at one of the Order's old holy places, and send the child back — cut, bound the same way this season's four were, and rarely spoken of afterward. Nothing ever answered: no one knew where any shard lay, and blood on bare ground is only blood. The faithful gave anyway, believing the giving itself would wake him. *(Corrected 2026-09-28: this used to say each child's blood was tested "against whichever shard lay nearest their home" — which contradicted the Stones' only protection being that nobody knew where they were, and Ep4's "For years they gave marked blood in his name, and nothing ever answered.")* All of them went home — except the charcoal woman's nephew, whom the Order lost. Asked, a captured faithful says only: *"Most went home."* — and if pressed about the nephew, *"Him we lost."* *(Simplified 2026-09-28 under GM Principles 2.11: the earlier backstory — a witness who followed the takers home and never came back — was one exception too many.)* See the Deception Table, Episode 3/4 knowledge-limit table, for the scripted exchange.

**Why Garrick needed both her notes and the party:** her find-sites say where each trail starts; only the party's blood can follow it. Neither alone finds a Stone. He had her killed once she had placed all four.

**The mechanic behind both Refrains — retired the willing-vs-forced-blood version (2026-09-23), replaced with something simpler:** the same 5-shard ritual does opposite things because Malakor is in a different *state* each time it's performed on him, not because of how the blood is given. The Seekers performed it while he was wounded and actively corrupting — the power had nowhere to go but down into forced dormancy. Garrick performs it on an already-sealed, dormant Malakor in Ep6 — the same act instead releases what was being held down. No extra rule needed: this is just what *"the key that locks is also the key that unlocks"* literally means. One lock, one key, opposite result depending only on which way it was already turned.

## The Present (the season)

**Garrick** is Malakor's last living descendant — the blood shard has been bonded to his bloodline since the day Malakor first claimed it. He holds the Order's own scripture (the Cult Bible) in its best-preserved, most complete form — rooted in the one genuine surviving fragment of the true ancient text that came down through his family alongside the blood shard itself (see `mythios-cult-bible.md`'s three-layer model) — and knows exactly what will wake Malakor and what won't. He has spent years seeking Seeker descendants who can locate the shards. He found Meira's research, had her killed, and kept her notes. *(Clarified 2026-09-23: she kept her satchel at his inn, where she lodged and worked; his people killed her away from it — he never leaves the inn — and he simply kept what she'd left behind. That is why his Ep1 lines — "Left this" / "She left her papers here. Never came back for them" — are literally true.)*

**What the Seekers' blood-lock actually guards (clarified 2026-09-23):** their *ways* — the fragments and the maps they assemble into — not the Stones' sockets. A socket is an open cut in stone; anyone who knows where one is can lift the Stone out. The Stones' only protection was that nobody knew where they were. That's how the cult always reaches a Stone first: the party solves the map, one of the Order's watchers overhears the place, and the cult simply walks in (see "How the Order learns where to go", below). Garrick's Ep1 "he's known it since he was a boy" is the old mine workings, not the Seekers' room inside them — the map is what revealed that room.

**Garrick cannot lie — full stop, no exceptions.** **Where the party learns this, concretely:** the Order's doctrine, "the faithful shall not lie," is Episode 3's doctrine reveal — the season's largest fair-play clue, already locked in `mythios-narrative-todo.md` Section 2. It's delivered two ways in the same scene: a captured cultist states it under interrogation, and a Cult Bible fragment (Layer 3 — the Order's own stated tenet about itself, not ancient-text instruction) confirms it in writing. The party learns this as *cult doctrine*, not yet as something they connect to Garrick specifically — that connection is theirs to make, later, on their own. Every deceptive beat in this season works entirely through true statements, withheld context, and the party's own inferences. He never fabricates evidence himself (see Deception Table).

**The 4 kids, Ep1-4:** the Seeker descendants (the party) are individually drawn in, clues leading to Garrick's inn. He hands them Meira's satchel when asked, and her own readable pages tell them a child was taken near the first of her places — he never frames the mission himself (Ep1 Day 2; Deception Table). As the cult captures each of the 4 real kidnapped children in turn, **that child's blood is used to activate the corresponding elemental Stone (Earth/Wind/Fire/Water). The children survive — blood is drawn, not fatal — and are rescued by the party.** This is not neutral: it is genuinely, correctly, the first half of the true awakening ritual. Solving the map-fragment puzzles (the party's own Seeker ability) is what actually reveals each stone's location — unknown to the party, they are leading Garrick's cult straight to them. **This is also the season's core dramatic irony, stated plainly for design reference: rescuing the child never stops the ritual, because the child was never the target — the location reveal was. The party succeeds and fails in the same motion, every time, which is why they stay structurally one step behind despite doing everything right.**

**Where the children come from, and how the Order learns where to go (rewritten 2026-09-28 — season audit H1, approved by TVMen):** Garrick holds Meira's notes, so the Order holds her list of the marked children living near each find-site — her attempt to protect them became its list of targets. It takes one child from the country around each find-site ahead of time and holds it there. It can't know where the Stone is hidden until the party's six fragments form a map — no single fragment shows anything. So the Order's watchers shadow the party, exactly as its own scripture says (*"Where the Seekers' children walk, the faithful follow, closer to them than their own shadows"*). On each map night the party works the place out aloud; a watcher hears it; before dawn the Order moves the child there and wakes the Stone. **Garrick tells no one where.** His passive signals (a lamp in a window, a horse tied outside) only ever say *tonight* — so if the party asks him "Did you tell anyone where we were going?", his *"No."* is true. The courier at the camp inn on Ep1 Day 6 is one of these watchers. The party is hunting people who are waiting for them.

**What ordinary faithful know (added 2026-09-28 — H2):** field faithful never see their leader's face or learn his name; orders come by riders. They know the king sleeps in his hall, that the marked are given in his name *to wake him*, that the blood is given onto a holy thing set in the stone, and that the faithful follow the Seekers' children. **One simple rule covers the rest: the faithful never speak of the holy thing to outsiders.** Asked, they keep silent — the doctrine allows silence, never a lie. So the Ep3 prisoner answers *"To wake him"* (true) and goes silent on the socket and on how blood wakes him; Ep4 opens that silence (the Stones). *(Streamlined 2026-09-28 at TVMen's request: an earlier draft split the faithful into Stone-carriers who knew and field faithful who didn't — one complication too many.)* No copy of the scripture outside Garrick's household is whole, so none of them has ever seen the torn line finished. So a captured cultist — who cannot lie — clears Garrick, truthfully: *"One whose face I've never seen."* / *"No."* (The scripted answers are in the Deception Table, Episodes 3 and 4.)

**The faithful never kill a Seeker's child (added 2026-09-28 — from the season audit's canon list, now on the page at Ep4 Day 1):** they need the party alive to walk the Seekers' ways. They fight to delay, never to kill — which is why Garrick can say, truthfully, *"Every time they've had knives out at you. Not one of you has died."* Ep4's scripture ("we have waited for those who can open them") explains it.

**Why the party is marked, and why the Order takes children (added 2026-09-28 — H4):** only the Seekers' descendants who were *born marked* can open the Seekers' ways — the Seekers locked them "with what we are": their own blood, born under the dark moon. Meira traced the Seekers' living lines from the same old records she used for marked births, and noted each descendant's birth-night; the [N] on her list are exactly those born marked. So the party is marked — the Ep2 Gathering shows it (beside each of their names in her notes, a date: every one a night the moon went dark) — and the kidnapped children, marked but not Seeker blood, could never open a fragment. The Order takes children rather than its own marked because each child is the bait that sends the party to that find-site, and because giving the marked young is its oldest practice.

**The fifth answers only beneath the dark moon — and why Garrick waits (added 2026-09-28 — H5):** the fifth answers its line's blood only beneath the dark moon; the old text says so (*"beneath the dark moon, it answers"*). At Ep5, even Garrick's blood would do nothing — so if the party asks him to try, he can bleed on it and nothing happens, which only strengthens their place-and-night reading. Why he doesn't simply take it after Ep5: taking it early means a fight he could lose, or a theft that sends them after him as enemies who can walk the Seekers' ways into the hall. Waiting seven nights beside them is safest — and the only moment he can ask for it with nothing but true words is right after their blood fails in the hall.

**The Episode 1 ambush (resolved 2026-09-28 — H6, approved by TVMen):** Meira wrote [N] pages at her table in his inn — all [N] names, and under them *The Hollow Wake Inn, on the drove road* — tearing each from the back of her notebook and pairing it with one of her rubbings of the Widow's Tooth marking, meaning to send them. The night she left the inn to send them, his people killed her on the road and took the pages; the rubbings came out of the fire half-burned. Three weeks later, his people put one page into each descendant's hands by staging an attack meant to lose, not to kill: the cargo lock forced the night before with the page left inside, the axle weakened, the rubbing dropped in the fight. The attacker never speaks, and runs — so no one who cannot lie is ever questioned. The party arrives believing they chose to come. At the inn, her notebook — [N] pages torn from the back, in the same neat hand as the list — ties the ambush to Meira and her missing child (Ep1 Day 2).

**What the party believes, stage by stage — and why Garrick can be sure of it (rewritten 2026-09-26).** This replaces an earlier "4 completes it" framing, which was wrong: the party never believes four Stones *complete* the awakening. They believe four *begin* it and the fifth *ends* it.

- **Ep1–3 — "there are four."** Four children (the wall's names, then the Ep2 ledger), Meira's four places, and her footnote dismissing "five" as a poet's count (surfaced Ep3). The party doesn't yet know the Stones matter at all.
- **Ep3 — "he isn't dead, and they're waking him" (rewritten 2026-09-28 — TVMen's split).** His power hollowed out his own people and was spreading (the corruption, made plain here so the stakes are obvious); the Seekers who struck him down were the party's own ancestors; he only sleeps; and a prisoner who cannot lie says the children are bled *"To wake him"* — and that the Order is *"not finished with you."* He keeps silent on the socket. Ep4 opens that silence: the blood only wakes the Stones, and the party was the key.
- **Ep4 — "not blood alone: the four are waking him."** Not inferred — stated and shown. The Order's own scripture says it outright (*The Verse of the Four*: blood on bare ground is only blood; set the four around him, each fed, and the sleep that holds him wears thin as ice in spring); the sky darkens that same hour; Garrick: *"It's the Stones that wake him."*
- **Ep4 — "the fifth will stop it."** Four true sources point that way: the torn page ("What the four begin, the fifth shall end—"); the Seekers ("We did not trust four locks to hold what needed five" — the fifth is what locked him last time); the Seekers again ("The fifth had no element at all, and we feared it more than the other four together" — it's different in kind, and they feared it most); and Garrick ("There's one more. A fifth Stone — she wrote about it"). *(A fifth source, Ep1 Day 6's "Only what is not yet taken can still be saved", was removed 2026-09-27 when TVMen replaced that fragment as too convoluted.)* Since Ep3, the party has also trusted that the Order's writing never lies.
- **Ep5 — "our blood, where he fell, under the dark moon, will seal him."** See the Ep5 twist below.

**Why it isn't chance — Garrick chooses what they find (confirmed 2026-09-26):** the Order's copies of its scripture are old and damaged; only his household's copy is whole. He sends a cultist carrying a genuinely torn copy of that one page to the Water Stone site — it's on whichever of them the party takes or finds fallen, so nothing depends on a prisoner — knowing the tear falls one breath before "and the throne rise whole." He forges nothing and says nothing false. He puts the right true page in their hands at their most desperate moment, with the sky going dark overhead. (A deliberate steer — GM Principles 4.4 — not luck.)

**What the four actually do (confirmed 2026-09-26):** one woken shard does nothing on its own — it's only a shard that has drunk. Once all four have drunk, the four together begin to wear the Undying Sleep thin, however far from him they are: he stirs, the sky darkens by day, the dead near him stop standing still. As each is carried home and set around him, the sleep fails faster. That is why nothing visible happens until the fourth has drunk — and why the sky goes wrong within the hour at the Ep4 Gathering, while the fourth is still on the road north. But the four can never finish it. Only the fifth, drinking moon-blood beside him under the dark moon, completes the waking. So at Ep4 the party is right that it has started and is getting worse; their one mistake is what the fifth does. By Episode 4 the cult has woken all four and carried them home to him — the party has rescued every child, and every Stone is live regardless.

**Ep4 twist — the torn page:** off a captured cultist — one Garrick sent there carrying it (see above) — the party recovers a page from the Order's own scripture, damaged, ending mid-sentence: *"What the four begin, the fifth shall end—"* Nothing after it survives. Combined with Garrick's true reveal that a 5th, uncatalogued stone exists — genuinely true, he needs their Seeker skill to find it — the party fills the gap with the ending they need: **the fifth will end the threat.** Nobody invented an ambiguous phrase; the party is finishing an incomplete one, under pressure, the only way hope allows. Garrick never claims the 5th stone will stop anything. He simply doesn't correct their reading of a sentence he never finished for them.

**Where the 4 elemental stones actually go, confirmed 2026-09-23:** the cult doesn't scatter or hide them after activation — each one is taken straight to the King's Tomb, stockpiled there in preparation for the final ritual. **The party never possesses any of the 4 elemental stones across Ep1-4** — every socket they find is already empty, the object gone minutes or hours before they arrive (reinforcing the "always one step behind" irony above). Episode 4's reveal ("it's not the kids, it's the stones") lands as a real betrayal for exactly this reason: every rescue, every solved puzzle, has been the party unknowingly delivering a stone into Garrick's hands.

**Ep5 twist — moon-blood, then the Tomb:** the recovered **Cult Bible** — a Layer 2 inset within it, genuine Royal Fragment text — says the 5th stone "drinks deep of moon-blood." Every stone so far has answered to any moonchild — so the party assumes this one will too, and tries their own blood on it. It fails. The second clue points them to the King's Tomb, on the next eclipse night — urgent, time-boxed. **Drafted 2026-09-23 as two genuine sources, never Meira (she never writes mechanism content):** the Seekers' Ep5 Day 6 fragment "Where he fell, under the dark moon, the five were joined"; and the Order's watch-page, *The Watch* (seven nights left). They travel there believing they're completing the sealing conditions. See `mythios-season-reveal-map.md`. **Where Ep5's search begins (confirmed 2026-09-26):** Meira's notes hold no fifth place, so Garrick supplies it, truthfully — his family's scripture says the Seekers kept the fifth near the king: *"The fifth isn't out in the hills with the others. Every telling I know ends at Highwall."* *(Corrected 2026-09-28: the line used to open "If there's a fifth" — fake doubt, since he said "There's one more" himself in Ep4.)* In the ruins, the trail's first fragment waits on the Seekers' own stretch of wall (Origin item 9).

**Why "moon-blood" being exclusive to Garrick's line isn't sprung on the party cold — it's already seeded** by the torn page's *"remembers whose"*, the Seekers' Ep4 Day 4 *"Moon-blood woke it once"*, and Ep5 Day 3's *"None of us gave to the fifth."* *(Cut 2026-09-28 by TVMen, on the season audit: the Ep2 Day 2 fragment "Not every shard remembers the hand that first held it. One did." — beside the Ep2 heirloom story and the torn page it pointed at royal blood too early. The text below describes its old role, kept for history.)* Old seed ("Not every shard remembers the hand that first held it. One did."): the Seekers themselves, centuries ago, plainly stating the blood shard's bonded nature — the party has this line long before Ep5, they just don't yet have a reason to connect it to "moon-blood" specifically. Ep5's failed blood attempt quietly supports it, but the fragment is **not re-quoted at Ep5** — quoting it right after the failure would point straight at royal blood. It closes at Ep6, re-quoted word for word when the fifth answers Garrick. *(Aligned 2026-09-26 with the scripted Gatherings; previously described as a two-part closure.)*

**How the faithful get into the sealed hall (added 2026-09-29, the season audit's Ep6 ruling):** by the stair his household fled down the night the Seekers came — the Seekers let the household go and never knew to seal it. The party's way is the Seekers' own blood-locked door in the crack behind the hall, marked to open on the dark moon. Set up on-page: Ep6 Day 1's fragment ("His household ran the night we came to the hall") and Garrick's Ep6 Day 4 line ("The Seekers sealed the doors they knew about").

**Ep6 is a convergence, not a race, confirmed 2026-09-23:** the party arrives at the Tomb believing they're about to perform a sealing ritual — and finds the cult already there, with all 4 elemental stones stockpiled and waiting, exactly as planned all along. Both sides are about to do the same physical ritual, with opposite beliefs about what it does. Garrick has been embedded with the party this entire season specifically to guarantee both groups end up in the same chamber, on the same night, together.

**Ep6 — the finale, two twists.** *(Delivery order at the Gathering, per TVMen: Twist 1 = only Garrick's blood works; Twist 2 = the fifth completes, not stops. The two paragraphs below describe the mechanics, not the scene order — see `mythios-season-reveal-map.md` Episode 6.)* At the Tomb, they try their blood again. Fails again. The full, untorn text — the same Royal Fragment, Garrick's better-preserved copy — completes the sentence at last: *"...end, and the throne rise whole."* Not stop. Complete.

**And "moon-blood" gets its own reveal, not just an assertion (confirmed 2026-09-23):** it was never a category. It was coined as a specific epithet for Malakor himself, the instant the blood shard bonded to him — not "a moonchild" (one of many), but "the moon's blood": the one person whose blood hadn't just carried the moon's mark like every Seeker's did, but had been taken *into* an actual piece of the moon and answered. A title, unique to him and whoever inherited the bond after. Over the centuries, the line between "moon-marked" and "one with the moon" blurred in common memory, and the term drifted from a specific identity into what now just sounds like a fancier synonym for "moonchild" — the same kind of erosion that turned "marked" into "moonchild" and "shard" into "stone," just running the other direction (a name narrowing to one person, then widening back out through forgetting, rather than a broad word settling on one meaning). **Why it drifted toward "moonchild" and not toward royalty (confirmed 2026-09-26):** after he fell, the king was erased — his name and titles scraped from every record, because nobody wanted the undead king remembered. "Moon-blood" survived only in old songs, cut loose from the man, and slid onto the only people still tied to the moon: the children born in its dark. Who misread it, in order: the world (over centuries), then Meira (who honestly wrote down the modern meaning, Ep4), then the party (reading through her). Only his own household kept the true meaning, in the one copy of its scripture it never let out — Garrick's — which the party sees only at the Ep6 reveal. Seeded before then by *The Verse of the Answering* ("the world he saved took back his name"), by the Seekers never once naming him in any fragment, and by Meira's note that every record of him has been scraped clean (Ep2). The shard→stone drift (Ep1, Meira's note) only shows that old words drift; the erasure is *why this one drifted the way it did*. It also makes Garrick knowing the name "Malakor" (given plainly the first time anyone asks, Ep2) a quiet plant. Garrick offers his blood, framed all season as nothing more than a loyal ally's true, incidental color about his family's "old ties" to this history — and it works, because it's the one blood the bonded shard was ever going to answer to. Malakor wakes. Season 2 hook.

**Cult motivation (confirmed, deliberately not noble):** the Order of the Dark Moon is what remains of Malakor's royal household — cast out and hunted once his corruption became visible to the kingdom, centuries of hiding and decline. Garrick's plan is not restoration-as-justice or a sincere belief in saving anyone. It is simple: wake the king, and the family stops hiding. A king with the undead power at his command, this time under family control rather than one man's unmediated grasp, means the family commands the world instead of cowering from it. Whether the bloodline itself is actually free of the corruption that twisted Malakor is a belief Garrick holds, not a fact the season confirms — left open, unstated in-fiction, as a possible Season 2 lever rather than exposition this season needs to carry.

## World details *(added 2026-09-29, series audit — depth without complexity)*

- **Highwall** is the name of both the old kingdom and its capital, the city on the last wall. The name survived; only the king's was scraped away. Players first hear it at the Ep2 Gathering; Ep5–6 take place there.
- **The Grey Host** is the name the world remembers for the enemy out of the east.
- **The official version**, the one every player-character learned as a child: *the Grey Host broke at Highwall of a fever in its camp, and Highwall was spared by luck.* This is the story "with him cut out of it" (the Verse of the Answering).
- **The king knew the name of every man on his wall** (the Verse of the Answering). In the Verse of the Binding he speaks each of their names at his doors, and none of them answers.
- **Dark-moon customs:** at Ashcombe, a baby born under a dark moon is given a chip of black glass "to keep the dark off" (Wren has kept every chip she's found since). At Reedham, a lamp is lit in every window on a dark-moon night (Joss was born with the whole village lit round him).
- **The Order's greeting:** *"Keep the dark."* — answered *"It keeps us."* First overheard in Ep5 as the watch changes; in the Ep6 Gathering the faithful greet Garrick with it, and he answers.
- **The four children's traits:** Tamsin hums a counting song; Corin asks each rescuer's name and says it back; Wren keeps chips of black glass and gives one to a player; Joss carries a little boat he carved himself. All four are named, one trait each, in the Ep6 finale.
- **Meira's death:** the Order killed her on the road, the night she left with the pages she'd torn from her notebook. Her pen, with her initials, is found in a cultist's pouch at the Ep4 Gathering, and Garrick confirms it in Ep6: *"My people. On the road, the night she left with those pages."* (The faithful never kill a Seeker's child. Meira was not one.)
- **The Ep3 prisoner's escape:** at the end of the Ep3 Gathering every lamp goes out, and when they're relit he's gone, his ropes cut. The Order's watchers came for him in the dark. The faithful never abandon their own, and never kill a Seeker's child: nobody in the party is touched.
- **Garrick's extra cup:** a family habit, poured every night for "whoever can't be with us". It has always been for the sleeping king. In the Ep6 Gathering he sets it down beside him.

**The old king's coin (added 2026-10-03, Audit D, Ep4 Day 2):** coins of the old kingdom still turn up. The world scraped his face off them, as it scraped his name out of its books. The faithful carry them still, and keep the other side polished. The Order's riders pay a carter with one on the north road.
