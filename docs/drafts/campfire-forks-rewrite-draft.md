# Campfire forks: rewrite draft (15 Oct 2026)

**Status: DRAFT for Fake Guru. Nothing here is in the game yet.** Each NEW fork needs his yes.

The fork is tonight's question at the fire: two readings, A and B, that the party argues about (a friend may also write their own). The audit (`docs/audits/point-6-campfire-discussions.md`, §A2 and Part B) found 10 weak forks and one awkward line. This draft proposes a fix for each.

**Sources used:** the current forks (`src/data/campfire-days-v23.json`); each day's chapter (`docs/canon/episode-N-*.md`); that day's three approved Fan Out leads (`docs/drafts/week-N-fan-out-and-fire-draft.md`); `docs/canon/garrick-deception-table.md` and `docs/canon/mythios-season-reveal-map.md` for the truth.

**What a good fork needs:** a real disagreement the evidence can move. That day's chapter must not settle it. Ideally each reading predicts something different ("if A, what should we find next?"). Both readings short and plain. No invented lore. First-use gates kept. Nothing about Garrick, whose blood, or the royal household. Nothing that warns the fifth Stone could finish the waking (the reveal map, Thread 2, keeps Week 6 free of that warning on purpose).

**Leads below** are numbered as in the week drafts (1, 2, 3). "Now supports" means which reading each lead backs under the NEW fork. The leads themselves are not changed.

## Week 2

### 2-1 · The mine, then the mill road

- **Now:** A "Corin is next because the cart went there." / B "Corin is next because the wall says so."
- **Why it's weak:** both are true, and the chapter already says the cart came three nights ago ("You are too late to stop it").
- **Proposed:** A "They're keeping Corin close, upriver." / B "Upriver is only the road away."
- **Why it works:** the day ends on "it turned upriver", and nothing yet says how far it went. If A, the tracks should end within a day; if B, they run on for days. Lead 1 (the steady horse that knows the lane in full dark) backs A: these people live near. B rests on the chapter: the farmer's "moving like they'd done it before" echoes the Fenmark boy "a whole county away". Lead 3 (ruts deeper coming down) backs both: they carried something away, but not where to. Lead 2 (ruts run past his gate) speaks to neither. Days 3 and 4 settle it.

### 2-4 · The shaft in the hillside

- **Now:** A "We were heard." / B "We weren't, and they know anyway."
- **Why it's weak:** B ends the argument; either way they know.
- **Proposed:** A "Now they'll hurry." / B "They won't change a thing for us."
- **Why it works:** the chapter's own question is "before they finish?", so the pace is what matters tonight. If A, expect them to move fast or move Corin; if B, there is still time. Lead 1 (the second lamp, filled and trimmed, nobody grabbed it in a hurry) and lead 3 (the chant never stopped under the shout) back B. Lead 2 (the pale scar, a stone that fell all the way down) backs A: we caught them off guard. The chapter backs both: they haul the ladder away (A), and the new ladder shows "they mean to use this place for a long time" (B).
- *Qwen's idea ("the second lamp was for the next shift / for us") is good but narrower. It rests on one clue only.*

## Week 3

### 3-1 · Somewhere warm

- **Now:** A "Wren's in Ashcombe." / B "'Somewhere warm' means somewhere else."
- **Why it's weak:** the chapter already says it: "'Somewhere warm' is home: Ashcombe."
- **Proposed:** A "They need her at home." / B "Home is only the first stop."
- **Why it works:** it argues over the day's two open lines: "they told her she was going home first" and "Why would anyone take a stolen child back to where she was born?" If A, she'll be in or near Ashcombe; if B, the trail goes past it. Lead 1 (Corin's grey mitten, smelling of sheep) backs A: she really is going back to sheep country. Lead 2 (a cask topped up to the brim) backs B: a long road ahead. Lead 3 (Corin stood facing west, then went the cart's way) backs both. Day 2 moves it both ways: she passes within a mile of her door, then goes on up the black road.

### 3-2 · Ashcombe (only a small reword)

- **Now:** A "They took her willingly with manners." / B "The manners are the trick."
- **Why it's weak:** A reads awkwardly. The fork itself is strong.
- **Proposed:** A "The manners were real." / B "The manners are the trick." (B unchanged)
- **Why it works:** same meaning, plain words, and it now mirrors B. It rests on the mother's "I've been lied to by better men than him. That wasn't what this was." The leads keep their leans: lead 2 (the made bed in the cart) A, lead 3 (sacking to quiet the hooves) B, lead 1 (the boy at the well) both.

## Week 4

### 4-5 · The reed island

- **Now:** A "Joss is safe while he carves." / B "They let him carve to keep him calm."
- **Why it's weak:** both can be true at once, so there is nothing to argue.
- **Proposed:** A "They still move Joss by boat." / B "Joss is held in one place now."
- **Why it works:** it is the chapter's own question: "where did the boat take him, and what has it been doing since?" If A, follow the boat to find him; if B, the ferry is for something else, and he is in a fixed place. Lead 1 (long, unhurried curls in the boat) and lead 2 (a dry blanket folded under the stern seat) back A: he spends long nights in this boat. Lead 3 (three willow shoots cut days apart) backs B: someone fetches him wood, so he can't fetch his own. The chapter gives both: a ferry "night after night" (A); "kept eight nights… waiting for something" from Day 3 (B). Day 6 settles it.

## Week 5

### 5-2 · The old kingdom's road

- **Now:** A "The heartbeat is turning the beasts." / B "The beasts are afraid, not turned."
- **Why it's weak:** the day's summary states A as fact.
- **Proposed:** A "The dogs were sent for us." / B "We were only in their way."
- **Why it works:** the chapter leaves this open: the dogs stop "as if they had forgotten what they came for" (A), and the one you beat "wags its tail, once, as if asking what it has done" (B). If A, expect more each dusk, aimed at us; if B, anyone on the road is at risk, like the family. Lead 3 (tracks come in together, straight from the north) backs A. Lead 2 (one clean bite on the farmer, from his own yard) backs B: they turn on anyone. Lead 1 (heads down when the beat stops) backs both.

### 5-3 · The herald's gate

- **Now:** A "Someone is inside." / B "Someone wanted us to think so."
- **Why it's weak:** the chapter says someone keeps it shut "now", and B is an "it's a trap" option that ends the talk.
- **Proposed:** A "The bar keeps the world out." / B "The bar keeps something in."
- **Why it works:** it answers the chapter's own question: "Who bars a dead city from the inside, year after year?" If A, whoever keeps it will meet anyone who comes in; if B, what is inside will show itself (the standing soldiers on Day 4 and the hall's doors on Day 5 move it). Lead 3 (the stair was smashed with hammers long ago) backs A: nobody was meant to climb in. Lead 2 (the bar was put up in the last few days) backs B: shut tighter just as the heartbeat began. Lead 1 (one trodden path from the bar up to the fire) backs both. The chapter backs B too: "Something on the far side is holding them up." No new lore: it does not say who bars it.

### 5-5 · Below the hall

- **Now:** A "They're counting to a date." / B "They're counting for a signal."
- **Why it's weak:** one-sided. One stroke each dusk is plainly a date, and Gathering 5 names it the next night.
- **Proposed:** A "Their count began when the Stones drank." / B "Their count was set long ago."
- **Why it works:** the chapter does not say when the count began. It gives clues both ways: the watch has gone on "every night since he could stand", and the hearth is "older than anyone sitting at it" (B), but the board holds only "rows of strokes", not centuries of them (A). If A, they are answering what we did; if B, they knew this night was coming before we were born. Lead 2 (every head lifts at the beat) backs A: their watch moves with the heartbeat, which began when the Stones drank. Lead 1 (every stroke in one hand, gone over many times, never redrawn) backs B: the whole count was written out once, long ago. Lead 3 (more bedrolls than people) backs both. "Stones drank" is already known (Gathering 4; Week 5 opening).
- *If you'd rather keep this one: it is the mildest of the eleven. The audit counted it strong. I'd still replace it.*

## Week 6

### 6-1 · The wall room (Six nights)

- **Now:** A "He looked at the window because he saw us." / B "He looked because it's the only window."
- **Why it's weak:** the chapter settles it: "you are not found."
- **Proposed:** A "They think the light was nothing." / B "They know someone's in the wall."
- **Why it works:** we're safe tonight, but what the Order believes now is open. If A, the search stops; if B, they keep hunting the wall all week. The chapter backs both: "searching by rote, they do not expect to find anything" (A), and "He saw a light. He did not see where." (B). Lead 1 (no light inside, no lantern reaches in) backs A. Lead 2 (the man with the chalk stops and looks down at the wall before he strikes) backs B. Lead 3 (old soot in a niche turned away from the slit) backs A: the room was built to hide a light. Nothing here touches the fragment's words or where anyone ran.

### 6-4 · The hill behind the hall (Three nights)

- **Now:** A "The crack is the Seekers' own door." / B "The crack is a trap."
- **Why it's weak:** the chapter already says A ("the Seekers' own way in"), and B is "it's a trap".
- **Proposed:** A "The rock waits for the dark moon." / B "The rock will open for us now."
- **Why it works:** it is the open end of the day: blank rock, and beside it "a full moon, with its whole face scored dark". If A, come back on the night; if B, find how to open it before then. Lead 2 (two foot-hollows where the soldier stood facing out a very long time) backs A: this door is about waiting. B rests on the chapter: "Every one of them has opened for you". It is backed again by tonight's fragment once it is solved ("only our blood will open"). Lead 1 (untouched grit) and lead 3 (the heartbeat is loudest at the rock) back both. Day 6 settles it ("here", and the scored moon). It never points at the other door.

### 6-5 · The moon-tower (Two nights)

- **Now:** A "The record means the moon will come back as it did." / B "The record is only a guess."
- **Why it's weak:** low stakes, and the chapter has already counted forward ("The page and the wall agree").
- **Proposed:** A "The Seekers chose the dark moon." / B "The dark moon came by chance."
- **Why it works:** the record shows that he fell on a dark moon. It does not say why. If A, the moon is part of the lock, and the night itself matters; if B, the moon is only a date to keep. Lead 2 (slates of long sums, crossed out and begun again) backs A: someone was working a night out in advance. Lead 3 (the last rows cut in a hurry) backs B: events overtook the watchers. Lead 1 (the same spacing, year on year) backs both: anyone could know when a dark moon comes. It stays clear of the banned question: whether that night ends the waking or finishes it.
