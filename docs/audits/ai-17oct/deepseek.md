# Tales of the Dark Moon — Audit Report

## The verdict in five lines

1. It is a good game with a strong story, a likeable guide, and a finale that earns its reveals.
2. The Campfire is the weakest part: it is overbuilt and replaces conversation with forms.
3. The weekday fights repeat the same silent-attacker shape too often, especially in Weeks 1–3.
4. The [PRESS ON] branches hide the best details in Weeks 5 and 6, making the optional content feel like the real content.
5. The single most important change: cut the Campfire down to one shared question, the fragment meaning, and an optional riddle. Let the friends talk.

## The whole season

### The arc

The season builds. Week 1 plants the list; Week 2 reveals the children are taken for a purpose; Week 3 reveals the king sleeps; Week 4 reveals the Stones wake him; Week 5 reveals the fifth is cold to the party's blood; Week 6 is the finale. The countdown in Weeks 5 and 6 is a strong clock. Week 3 is the sag: it repeats Week 2's rescue structure and the silent-attacker fights. The finale pays off the six weeks of fragments, maps, and rescues. "To be continued" is earned.

### The game

The core loop is clear and readable. The numbers are simple. The pass/fail difference is often soft: failing a Challenge costs gold and a worse branch, but the story reaches the same place. The gold has no visible sink. The Relics are not shown. The Campfire is overbuilt. The Gathering devices are strong, especially the United Effort and the table question. Match is a quiz, not a conversation. Ask is good but underused. The silent-attacker fights wear thin by Week 3. The [PRESS ON] branches hide the best details in Weeks 5 and 6.

### The story

The characters are strong: Tamsin, Corin, Wren, and Joss are people, not props. Garrick is warm, dry, and likeable, and his reveal is earned. The mystery is fair. The stakes are clear. The twist is strong. The ending is strong. The prose is often excellent. Some lines are over-written (Campfire openings) and some are rushed (the final choice's aftermath).

### Game and story together

They mostly support each other. The Challenges and fights give the player something to do between story beats. The fragments and maps give a sense of discovery. The Gatherings give the friends a shared climax. The Campfire is where they pull against each other: the game wants the friends to talk, but the Campfire gives them forms to fill in.

### Garrick across the season

He stays likeable. His lines are specific and warm. The risk is in the Campfire openings, which are all wry observations in the same register. If the AI small talk is not well filtered, he will feel like a computer character. The written lines are strong enough that he does not become one.

### The friends together

The Gatherings make the friends play together. The roll-call, the water-gate, and the final choice are strong social moments. The Campfire is meant to do this too, but it is too structured. The friends are more likely to talk at the Gathering than at the fire. The season is worth six weeks of evenings if the Campfire is simplified.

## The hindsight check

### Clues and Garrick's lines

**Land well:** The extra cup. "My family's been in these hills longer than the roads have." "I doubt she thought I was listening." "Every time they've had knives out at you, not one of you has died." "The old telling names a place, and a night." "We're early." "One stayed close. It always did." "The Seekers sealed the doors they knew about." "It always comes back."

**Feel like cheating:** "I know what she wrote down." It is a non-answer dressed as an answer. It is fair in the letter of his rule, but it is the line most likely to make a player feel tricked rather than fooled.

**Too hidden ever to find:** "My grandmother taught me that name." "The faithful don't." Both are fair on reread, but they are easy to miss entirely. The Week 3 doctrine ("Speak true, always") is the clue that makes them fair, but it is easy to miss.

### Loopholes

1. **The children are in real danger, but the secret says they were never in danger of dying.** The scenes are written as if they are in real danger. The recap says they were needed alive. That is a tension. Fix: either make the danger real (the Order is careless) or clearly staged (the Order is always nearby).

2. **Garrick lets his own people die.** The party kills many of the faithful. Garrick is their hidden leader. He does not mourn them. Fix: let him show one moment of grief or cost, or make the faithful's deaths clearly staged.

3. **Why does the Order attack the party at all?** The attacks are staged to keep the party moving, but the party fights back and kills the faithful. Fix: make the Order's rule explicit earlier. Let a captive say, "We were told not to kill you."

4. **The final choice is random.** The most important choice in the season is given to one random friend. Fix: let the table choose who acts, or make it a group vote.

### Fairness

An attentive party could suspect Garrick is more than an innkeeper. They could suspect he knows more than he says. They probably could not suspect he is the last of the king's house until the finale. That is the right balance. The recap is honest. The one line that strains fairness is "I know what she wrote down."

## The ten most important fixes for the whole season

1. **Cut the Campfire down to conversation-first.** Remove or merge the board, pins, case-so-far, and message in a bottle. Keep one shared question, the fragment meaning, and an optional riddle. Let the friends talk. This is the single biggest improvement.
2. **Stop repeating the silent-attacker fight.** Give each fight a distinct goal and a visible consequence. Let the outcome change what happens next, not just cost HP. This fixes Weeks 1–3.
3. **Make the "read it out" choice honest.** Either let it matter (reading aloud alerts the Order) or remove the implication. Do not tease a consequence you will not charge. This fixes Weeks 2 and 3.
4. **Move the best details out of [PRESS ON].** The stacked bars, the watch-change ritual, the eight strokes, the count of eleven, and the chisel are too good to be gated. Put them in the main pass branch. This fixes Weeks 5 and 6.
5. **Make the final choice the party's, not one random friend's.** Let the table choose who acts, or make it a group vote. The most important choice in the season should not be a dice roll. This fixes Week 6.
6. **Make the fails cost a clue, not just gold or comfort.** The ash-reading, the skiff, the lowest lane, the flame, and the carter all give the same information on a fail. Let the party lose the clue and have to find it another way. This fixes Weeks 1–6.
7. **Answer the "why don't they take us" question cleanly.** The party is marked too, but the Order only takes children. Let Garrick or the captive give a real answer: children's blood is stronger before it settles, or the party is more useful as keys than as fuel. This fixes Weeks 2 and 4.
8. **Make the "hold it back" choice have a cost.** If you hold the Stone back, Garrick has to take it from you. That is a moment of betrayal. If you give it, it is trust. Make the choice change the emotional texture. This fixes Week 6.
9. **Connect the "say a name" choice to the final action.** Let the name you say affect how the final choice plays out. Make the names matter to the action, not just the epilogue. This fixes Week 6.
10. **Vary Garrick's register.** His Campfire openings are all wry observations in the same register. Let him ask questions, be quiet, tell a small story, be wrong about something small without lying. Keep the warmth, lose the formula. This fixes the risk of him becoming a computer character.

## What must not change

1. **The extra cup.** It is the best clue in the season: warm, true, and devastating on reread. Do not explain it earlier. Do not make it a puzzle. Let it be a habit.
2. **"We're early."** The best line in the season. It is true in the moment, and it is a misdirection, not a lie. Do not add a line that explains it.
3. **The children as people.** Tamsin's counting song, Corin's names, Wren's chips, and Joss's boat treat the children as people, not props. Keep that restraint. Do not make them symbols.
4. **The roll-call.** The best United Effort in the season. It asks the party to pretend to be the dead. Do not replace it with a fight.
5. **The water-gate.** The best moral choice in the season. It asks the party to save the man who took Joss. Do not soften it.
6. **The barring of the gate.** "Whoever changes the bar has kept every one." It is the best image of the Order's patience. Do not explain it earlier.
7. **The standing soldiers.** The dead who stand mid-stride and the one helm that may have turned. Do not resolve the ambiguity. It is a chill, not a promise.
8. **Garrick's warmth.** He is warm, dry, and never bossy. Do not make him cold or smug. The reveal works because he was kind.
9. **The prose of the fragments.** The fragments are the game's best writing. Do not simplify them.
10. **"You saved every child you were sent to save."** The best final line. It is true, and it is not the point. Do not add a line that explains it.
