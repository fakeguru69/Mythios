## Week 1 — *The List*

Week 1 is a very strong opening week. It teaches the daily loop clearly, introduces a warm and useful companion, and ends with a reveal that changes the meaning of the rescue without invalidating it. Its main issues are a generic opening fight, a few low-consequence choices, and a Battle that separates the party into parallel fights.

### Day 1 — The forest road

**In one line:** A silent man attacks the player, leaves a list bearing their name, and the day asks: *who is this man, and what does he want with me?*

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Story | **9/10** — The attacker does not steal anything; he puts something in. That inversion makes the day a mystery rather than a generic ambush. |
| Play | **8/10** — Fight, rubbing, box search, fragment and leads all feed the central question. |
| Prose | **9/10** — “Like light through bottle glass” and “a sliver of winter that has forgotten to melt” are excellent. |
| Pull | **9/10** — “Somebody wants you there” gives a clear reason to return tomorrow. |

**What works:** The list is personal, unexplained, and points to a destination.

**What does not:**

- **Medium — The opening fight is filler.** The man attacks, then leaves without speaking or leaving a story-specific consequence. **Fix:** let him speak, leave a more telling object, or force a choice between pursuit and protecting the wagon.
- **Medium — The two investigation choices overlap.** Both yield clues, but neither changes the immediate route. **Fix:** make one reveal the list’s destination and the other reveal that the attacker had help.
- **Small — “One stayed close” is compelling but unanchored.** **Fix:** let the Campfire point toward the mine wall or the coming map.

### Day 2 — The Hollow Wake Inn

**In one line:** The four list-names meet, identify Meira as the author, and learn a child was held near the Widow’s Tooth.

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Story | **9/10** — Bringing the four names together is a satisfying first-week payoff. |
| Play | **8/10** — Matching the page to the notebook is tactile, clever investigation. |
| Prose | **9/10** — “He sets down one more than there are of you” introduces Garrick’s ritual perfectly. |
| Pull | **9/10** — The child at the Widow’s Tooth opens the larger mystery. |

**What works:** Garrick’s reluctance makes his agreement feel earned.

**What does not:**

- **Medium — The optional PRESS ON contains a major clue.** Meira deliberately cutting the pages establishes that she meant to send them. **Fix:** put the clean cut edges in the main branch.
- **Medium — Garrick identifies the Widow’s Tooth too easily.** **Fix:** let the party decipher part of the shorthand first.
- **Small — The extra cup is introduced but not questioned.** **Fix:** allow one friend to ask about it.

### Day 3 — The Widow’s Tooth

| Prose | **9/10** — “A low, hollow note in the split” is excellent. |

- **Medium — The route choice is uneven.** **Fix:** let one branch preserve the marking and the other reveal the cart’s direction.
- **Small — The worn path is evocative but unused.** **Fix:** connect it to the Order’s long watch.

### Day 7 — The Gathering

**In one line:** The party enters the mine, rescues Tamsin, and learns the wall’s four names are children’s names.

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Story | **9/10** — The rescue lands, and “This was never about you” is a strong reversal. |
| Play | **8/10** — Approach, Battle, Notice, United Effort and discovery all function. |
| Prose | **9/10** — Tamsin humming as she is freed is quietly devastating. |
| Pull | **9/10** — “Three more” and the horse make Week 2 necessary. |

**What works:** The United Effort is excellent: Dev is trapped, every friend takes one turn, and the two bars create a real race.

**What does not:**

- **Big — The Battle is four separate fights.** **Fix:** add a shared objective and one collective decision per round.
- **Medium — The Approach’s map bonus wording is ambiguous.** **Fix:** write “DC 6 if the party solved the week’s map; otherwise DC 8.”
- **Medium — The discovery question is answered too quickly.** **Fix:** let players propose answers before revealing the children’s names.
- **Small — The white linen is a great clue but not yet felt.** **Fix:** let Garrick react faintly.

### Week 1’s three biggest fixes

1. Make the Battle collaborative.
2. Differentiate the silent-attacker fights.
3. Move the page-cutting clue into the main branch.


## Week 2 — *The Wind Stone*

Week 2 improves on Week 1. It turns the children’s names into a pattern, introduces the Order and Malakor, and delivers the first major lore reveal. Its main issues are a second consecutive fragment-map, a Battle that remains parallel, and a dog fight that asks players to harm an animal for no reward.

### Day 1 — The second name

**In one line:** Tamsin identifies Corin as the next child, and the party learns the same cart went toward the mill.

| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Story | **9/10** — Tamsin’s testimony gives the party a lead and continuity. |
| Play | **8/10** — Farmer encounter, cart clues, fragment and leads all advance the hunt. |
| Prose | **9/10** — “Worst guests I’ve ever had. Good tippers, though” is perfect Garrick. |
| Pull | **9/10** — The mill and upriver turn give a clear next step. |

**What works:** Tamsin saying “I wasn’t asleep. I was counting” continues her character beautifully.

**What does not:**

- **Medium — Tamsin’s three choices are cosmetic.** **Fix:** let each approach reveal a different detail.

## Finale and hindsight

### Week 6, Day 7 — the finale
Garrick’s betrayal is fair and emotionally devastating. The hawser is the best Battle-integrated United Effort. The fifth Stone’s refusal creates a real final-week question.

**Fixes:** Make the Battle shared; give the final act to the table; shorten scripture; clarify Approach DC; weight DO NOTHING.

### Hindsight: what lands
- “My grandmother taught me that name.”
- “My family’s been in these hills longer than the roads have.”
- “Every time they’ve had knives out at you, not one of you has died.”
- “We’re early.”
- “One stayed close. It always did.”
- “The key that locks is also the key that unlocks.”

### Hindsight: what is too hidden
- Garrick’s leadership of the Order.
- Meira’s murder connected to Garrick.
- The party’s role in helping the Order.
- The distinction between “marked” and “moon-blood.”

## Whole-season verdict

| Area | Score | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| Game | 8/10 | Strong loop; Battle is the main weakness. |
| Story | 9/10 | Fair mystery, strong escalation, strong finale. |
| Prose | 9/10 | Consistently strong; scripture/recaps occasionally long. |
| Garrick | 9/10 | Excellent companion and reveal. |
| Would I play it with friends | 8/10 | Yes, especially with 3–5 friends. |

## Ten most important fixes

1. Make every Battle shared.
2. Give the final choice to the whole party.
3. Retire the six-fragment map.
4. Clarify why Garrick needs the party.
5. Explain the Kiln escape.
6. Reduce lethal combat against victims.
7. Move indispensable clues out of optional rolls.
8. Trim Gatherings.
9. Give Garrick a limitation and earlier rank clue.
10. Resolve or cut orphaned details.

## Bottom line

*Tales of the Dark Moon* is a strong, coherent narrative game. Its greatest achievement is Garrick; its greatest opportunity is making party cooperation as good as its mystery.
