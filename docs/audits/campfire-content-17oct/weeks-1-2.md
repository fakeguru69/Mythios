## 2. Per night

**W1D1, the forest road**
- The fork (warning or invitation) is the right question for the night. The truth is both: Meira wrote the list as a warning, and the Order's man delivered it as a lure. See E6 Gathering "My people. On the road, the night she left with those pages", and the recap for "Garrick." ("the list he has been waiting for"). Nothing ever says outright who put the list in the box, so the best payoff in Week 1 is left for players to infer.
- The Pull leans the fork towards B before anyone pins: "Somebody wants you there." (E1 D1).
- "Two sets of boots" is marked as leaning A, but two men walking off calmly points to a staged attack, not a warning. The lean is wrong. "The oilcloth" (B) is the best clue here: he cared that the list arrived dry.
- The meaning question "One stayed close. Close to what?" pays off brilliantly at the finale (E6, "One stayed close. It always did.").
- The nudge is Garrick's voice on a narrated night, before the party has met him (CT 1-1: "no Garrick").

**W1D2, the Hollow Wake Inn**
- On the evidence of the night the fork leans heavily to B. The press-on shows the stubs were "cut against a rule… She meant to send them", and the notebook says "Scattered on purpose" (E1 D2). The fork's real answer comes in Week 6, where she left on purpose, carrying pages, and was taken on the road (E4 G "the pen"; E6 G). That is strong, but six weeks away, and hurry against purpose is not where its sting lies.
- "The worn end" repeats the Aim notice ("one seat… sees both doors"), so it adds nothing new (breaks CT test 1).
- The nudge ("how would you test whether the page is hers?") is the day's own Challenge, FIT THE PAGE, which is already solved. It is filler.
- The meaning question ("wept… before they feared") pays off at G2, where they "wept for him that morning… for joy", and in E3. Good.

**W1D3, the Widow's Tooth**
- The fork (guarding the rock, or waiting for us) is never answered. E1 D4 calls him only "the lookout", who ran to shut Fenmark's doors. The honest answer is "Neither", and the clues do not push either way.
- "The worn path" round the rock and "The notched stick" (fresh tally notches) are the most intriguing objects in Week 1. Nothing pays either of them off, so they teach players that clues do not matter.
- The nudge ("what was he watching?") is answered word for word by the Aim notice: "the road both ways and the village smoke".
- The meaning question "Some locks already know their key. Whose key?" is the season's spine, paid at W2D3 (the lock opens for you) and E6 ("You were the key"). It is the best meaning question of the fortnight.

**W1D4, Fenmark**
- The chapter settles the fork: "He is not hiding what happened here. He is terrified of what happens to this village if it is known that somebody talked." (E1 D4). B is given to the player, so there is nothing to debate.
- This is a quiet night (CT §F), yet the opener is a quip ("Mostly with its shutters") and the riddle's punchline jokes at a frightened village ("Fenmark's had plenty of practice"). Both break the tone rule.
- The meaning question ("Marked by whom, and for what?") pays off strongly at G2: the birth nights, then "You're marked too".

**W1D5, the charcoal camp**
- The fork (the same man, or a way of working) is a true mystery question, and the chapter gives real evidence both ways: she says "same man", but the runner's knife is "a plain kitchen knife… nothing curved". G2's ledger answers it ("keeping books like this for years… This isn't new"), and so does E3's Ask ("Him we lost. Not by our choosing."). A satisfying payoff.
- "The knife, not the face" (B) is an excellent clue: it shows "same man" is an inference. "Older marks" (A) is fair.
- "What has been given this week, and what has been taken?" feels like homework, but it rhymes with the ledger's *given* (E2 G).

**W1D6, the ford**
- G1 answers the fork at once: the mine is "on the far riverbank". The B reading has no evidence behind it, and "Up at a run" is a weak B lean.
- The night ignores its two real hooks: the map laid out, and the courier who leaves within the hour after the map is read aloud (E1 D6). The second is a canon plant that W2D6 repeats.
- The Pull is stale. The Campfire data (migration `0025`) says "be there by morning"; canon was changed on 16 Oct to "by nightfall" (E1 D6, Designer's notes). This contradicts the story.

**W2D1, the mill road**
- The fork (keeping Corin close upriver, or upriver is only the road away) is about logistics. W2D3 and W2D4 answer A plainly.
- "Past the gate" puts a child's wooden horse "on its side in the grass". That repeats W1D3's carved horse almost word for word, so players will chase a link the story never makes.
- "Deeper coming down" is marked "both", but it reads as plain evidence that the boy was in the cart. It does not bear on the fork.

**W2D2, the mill**
- The fork (a list with birthdays, or knew him personally) is the heart of the week. A careful player can join it to W1D2 ("each with a date beside it"). G2 answers it with the ledger, the birth nights and "You're marked too", which turns the week question round.
- The nudge (how the mark was cut, and with what tool) is trivia that never returns.
- The meaning question ("Why stop counting?") answers itself: too many fires. It is weak.

**W2D3, upriver**
- The fork (the stranger wanted the same door, or was left as a warning) fights the evidence: he died with "a crude lockpick… still jammed into" the housing. Nothing ever answers it; E3 D6 mentions only "the lock the dead man could not open".
- The question players will actually want to ask is "why did it open for us?" The callback raises it, and W1D3's meaning question set it up. It pays off at G2, at G4 ("a door that only your blood could open") and at the finale, yet it is not the fork.

**W2D4, the shaft**
- The fork (now they'll hurry, or they won't change a thing) is answered by D5 ("now there is a clock"), D6 ("the hill wakes") and G2 ("broke through just ahead of you"). The deeper answer, that they follow the party, would make a fine reframe at G4 if any line tied back to it.
- "The pale scar" leans A only loosely. It mostly explains how the party was spotted.
- A boy lies bound in a ring of lamps, yet the tone is "wry" and the riddle quips "Bring a lantern. Bring two." That goes against canon's rule of no lightness while a child is in danger (CT §F rationale).
- The meaning question ("What did he do down there?") is paid at G2's Match ("He opened his palm"), and it echoes into W5 and W6. Excellent.

**W2D5, the hillside**
- The fork (erased by his enemies, or by his own people) is genuinely open. G2 answers it with "the world he saved took back his name", and E6 deepens it ("They cut his name out of everything they could reach"). That is the best reframe of the fortnight.
- "Faces turned in" (B) is subtle and strong. "Burned chips" (A) is fair.
- The callback only lists objects (button, stranger, circle) instead of linking them. The button-to-cuff link is already in the chapter.

**W2D6, the lip of the shaft**
- The two readings overlap: you dig "to get in" in order "to find something". G2's empty socket and G4's Stones pay it off well all the same.
- The night skips its sharpest question. The map was read aloud, "Sound carries", and "within the hour, the hill wakes" (E2 D6, a `[PLANT]`). That is the season's mechanism, answered at G4 ("Where the Seekers' children walk, the faithful follow").
- The callback ("emptied… still digging") frames the race well. The nudge restates the fork.

## 3. Across the fortnight

**The five biggest problems, worst first**
1. **Nothing players pin or find changes anything.** CT test 7 says "the story does not depend on it". Core Rules §11 says a beat is "never built from a player's theory". Pins are read back at the Gathering and then forgotten. Most clues (the notched stick, the worn path, the toy horse, the oilcloth, the second ring) never come back. Players will soon learn that the fire is decoration.
2. **Too many forks are settled by the chapter or the next day.** That covers W1D2, W1D4, W1D6, W2D1 and W2D4, five of twelve. Two more, W1D3 and W2D3, are never answered. Only W1D1, W1D5, W2D2, W2D5 and W2D6 have a payoff worth waiting for.
3. **The forks follow the chase, not the investigation.** Week 1's question is "Why are our names on that list?", yet only W1D1 touches it. Days 2 to 6 are all about where Tamsin is. The real chain of the investigation runs through the *meaning questions*: lock and key, marked, wept and feared, the bleeding hand, given. That chain is excellent and pays off at G2 and the finale, but it sits in a side slot, not at the centre of the night.
4. **The nudges are mostly filler.** Five restate the fork (W1D4, W1D5, W2D1, W2D3, W2D6). Two repeat a notice or the Challenge (W1D3, W1D2). One is trivia (W2D2). Only W2D4 and W2D5 point at evidence.
5. **Some clue leans are wrong, and two clues repeat chapter text.** The wrong or loose leans are W1D1 "Two sets of boots", W1D6 "Up at a run", W2D4 "The pale scar" and W2D1 "Deeper coming down". The repeats are W1D2 "The worn end" and W1D5 "One pair of boots", which echoes the Charm notice. On top of these sit the tone breaches (W1D4, W2D4) and the stale W1D6 Pull.

**Risk to the secret:** low. No clue or line points at Garrick, and the gates hold. "Who gains from a forgotten name?" and "erased by his own people" lead towards the Seekers, which is fair.

**The three best nights:** W2D2 (the question leads straight to the reveal), W2D5 (a real dilemma, and the Verse reframes the history players were taught), and W1D5 (the evidence cuts both ways, and the ledger answers it).

## 4. How to fix it

**Cheap fixes (data only)**
- Fix the W1D6 Pull ("by nightfall") in the Campfire data and migration, and check every Pull against `chapterScripts.ts`.
- Rewrite the nudges so that each points at a specific clue or notice line and asks what it means, instead of restating the fork.
- Re-mark the wrong leans, and replace the two clues that repeat chapter lines.
- Drop the quips on W1D4, and make W2D4 and W2D5 quiet nights, or give them plain openers and no riddle.
- Swap the W2D1 toy horse for a different object.
- Rewrite the weak forks (W1D3, W1D4, W1D6, W2D1, W2D3, and sharpen W2D6) so they are questions the chapter leaves open. For example: W1D6 asks who the courier was; W2D3 asks why the lock opened for us; W2D6 asks whether they heard us read the map.

**Deeper fixes (design)**
- Make the meaning question, or a fork tied to it, the centre of the night, since that is where the payoffs are.
- Let pins and clues come back. At the Gathering, Garrick could read back the pins that the night's reveal bears on, by name and without saying who was right. Each "both" clue could also get one later echo in canon (the notched stick next to Garrick's W6 tally; the oilcloth next to "those pages").
- Give each week a chain of forks: each one should narrow the week question and lead into the Gathering's table question, rather than six separate tactical prompts.
- Say plainly, somewhere in the story, who put the list in the box. It is the season's first question, and only Week 6 implies the answer.
