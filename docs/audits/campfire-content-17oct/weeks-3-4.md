## 2. Per night

**W3D1**
- The fork, "need her at home / only the first stop", follows straight from the Pull (E3 l.124). It is answered the next day: she was carried past Ashcombe in the dark (E3 l.218, l.243). B is confirmed with no surprise. The deeper answer, that a child is brought to the shard near home (E4 l.853), is never tied back to it.
- Clue 3-1-2 (a cask filled to the brim, so a long trip ahead) is real evidence for B. However, it clashes with the chapter: "The cart is riding light. They are hauling nothing but her." (E3 l.97).
- Clue 3-1-1 (Corin has a grey woollen mitten) is touching, but it does not bear on the fork. It comes back in 3-5-3 and is never explained.
- The meaning question ("whose were they, what took them?") pays off well in Gathering 3: the Verse of the Fall and its Match (E3 l.855–864).

**W3D2**
- "The manners were real / are the trick" is a real question about what the Order is. It pays off twice. At Gathering 3 the doctrine and "She has given. She may go." (E3 l.899, l.940) show the courtesy is sincere. At the finale, "the same linen. The same careful knot" (E6 l.1001) reframes that courtesy as Garrick's.
- Clue 3-2-3 (sacking over the hooves) is about stealth, not manners. Clue 3-2-1 (the boy hides his chip) says nothing about either reading. Only 3-2-2, the made bed, bears on the fork.
- The callback is sharp: one cut palm each, and "only need her the once".

**W3D3**
- The chapter has just announced the deduction: "The places Meira marked and the children in the ledger are one list." (E3 l.318). The fork asks which way the arrow points. Gathering 4 implies A ("a marked child is brought to it", E4 l.853), but no text ever says why each child lived beside a site. The payoff is partial.
- The clues are weak against their leans. A row of stones across a path (A) and a view down to the well (both) are scenery. Clue 3-3-2, "Only your hand", is the night's best evidence: nobody has touched the housing. But it belongs to the Pull ("they rode straight past") and to Gathering 4's twist (they need *you* to open housings). It does not belong to this fork.
- The nudge ("why would a scholar write down a number she doubted?") is excellent. It pays off at Gathering 4: "A fifth Stone — she wrote about it" (E4 l.943–945).
- The meaning question ("the price of what?") pays off in Gathering 3 (E3 l.868–875).

**W3D4**
- The summary card already says she drops chips "for whoever comes after" (E3 l.462). The fork, "for us / for herself", is about Wren's character and has no mystery stake. The story never answers it.
- The clues are lovely physical detail. Bare rock means each chip glints, and each chip sits beside a tall tree you can see from the last. The scratches numbered 1, 2 and 3 invite theories, but nothing ever pays them off.
- The meaning question ("We locked with what we are. What are they?") is strong: "Your blood. Their blood. The same." (E3 l.895).

**W3D5**
- The chapter decides the fork. "They left at a run" (l.538) and "Until today, these people have never once hurried" (l.557) settle it, and the designer note says the day means "we have made them run" (l.587). A also muddles who sent any message: the name was cut by Wren, and the beam was cut "long ago".
- Clue 3-5-1 ("each stroke cut twice") repeats the chapter: "each one has been gone over twice" (l.531). It also points at Wren, not the takers.
- Clue 3-5-3 (a row of keepsakes with a clean gap) echoes the row on the sill in W3D2 (l.212) and the grey wool, but nothing ever resolves it.
- The meaning question ("why would a kingdom fear a family?") is a fine misdirect. It closes at the finale.

**W3D6**
- "Go in now / watch the lamp first" is a decision the players never get to make. The Pull says "Into the Kiln, tonight", and Gathering 3 simply arrives (E3 l.716). The opener, "Sit by it before you decide anything", promises a choice that does not exist.
- The night's real mystery is left unasked. The map was read aloud into the wind (l.664–666), and "not an hour after" a lamp went into the Kiln (l.670). Who heard? DT l.276 says this is exactly Garrick's plan. The question only arrives a week later, at W4D6.
- Clue 3-6-1 ("No horses") lost its point. The v2 table had "though six rode this way" (v2 l.178), and the as-played text cuts it.
- The meaning question ("We never dug him a grave. Why not?") pays off brilliantly: "He did not die." (E3 l.881).

**W4D1**
- "He had help in the room / didn't need any" is the most gripping question of Week 4, and the chapter tilts it hard toward A. The Intellect notice says "That takes more than one pair of hands" (E4 l.46), and the press says "Whoever cut him loose came over the glass, and was waiting" (l.96). Nothing ever answers it.
- The fork and the nudge ("His own hand, or another's?") ask players whether someone among them did it, with Garrick "still at your shoulder" (l.84). Canon keeps that line of suspicion shelved (DT l.283, "Layer D … stays shelved") and keeps "nothing points at him" (E3 l.1034).
- Clue 4-1-1 (a wick just put out in the passage) supports help from *outside* the room, not "in the room", so its lean is wrong. Clue 4-1-3 (every cut on the same side) is a good ambiguous clue.

**W4D2**
- B ("the swords are the message") is weak. The Pull and the carter both put the box first (l.215, l.239).
- The payoff is the best of the fortnight: "Count the boxes" (E4 l.863–880).
- The nudge ("who scrapes a face off a coin?") and the callback pay off at E5 Day 1: "The line cut out of this stone is the same work" (E5 l.100).
- The meaning question ("the fifth had no element") pays off on the torn page (E4 l.930) and again in E6.

**W4D3**
- "Right night / right child" excludes the true answer. Gathering 4 says "We have waited for those who can open them" (E4 l.853), so the answer is "they were waiting for us". Only "Neither" reaches it, and nobody prompts players toward Neither.
- Clues 4-3-2 (lamp-oil spots to her door only) and 4-3-3 (a cleared seat on the tower, facing her window) start a sub-plot: someone is watching the grandmother's window. 4-6-3 and the W4D4 press (l.415) feed it, and nothing ever pays it off. It is a red herring with no resolution.
- The nudge ("what does a skiff tell you about its owner?") is trivia.
- The meaning question pays off in the Verse of the Four's first Match (E4 l.836–843).

**W4D4**
- "'Moon-blood' just means the marked / means something else" is the strongest fork. It is truly open on the evidence, it is about the mystery, and the finale overturns it: "Not marked. Moon-blood. It was a title … his house's." (E6 l.873).
- All three clues work. 4-4-2 ("two records, never in the same line") is fair-play evidence.
- The risk is over-steering. The opener ("She was careful with her words"), the nudge and 4-4-2 all push toward B. Yet E5's Gathering is built so that "the party draws the wrong conclusion by itself, which is the point" (E5 l.916). The Campfire may defuse that false hope a week early. It also has Garrick point at the very word that unmasks him, which goes against his "never volunteers" rule.

**W4D5**
- "Still by boat / held in one place" is answered the next morning by the map: "a flooded room under Old Reedham" (l.632). The Pull's better question, "Why would anyone keep watch on an empty tower?", is left unasked.
- Clue 4-5-1 (long, unhurried curls of wood) leans B in the data, but long hours of carving in the boat suggest he is moved often (A). The lean looks inverted. Clue 4-5-3 (three cuts of different ages) is good.
- The meaning question ("a door to what?") pays off with "The key that locks is also the key that unlocks" (E6).

**W4D6**
- "Followed us / already there" is balanced. Clue 4-6-1 (rings already spreading) and the chapter's "lying in the black water right under the tower" (l.621) support B. The Verse "closer to them than their own shadows" (E4 l.853) and DT l.485 (watchers overhear) support A.
- It pays off at Gathering 4 the next night, and again on reread at the finale.
- 4-6-3 (a lamp below her lane) extends the dead-end window thread.
- The riddle quip "Towers hand it back" fits the night.

## 3. Cross-night findings

**The five biggest problems, ranked**
1. **Nothing a player pins ever comes back.** Fork pins are read back only to later arrivals at the same fire. `gathering_case` reads back only the week lines (0079 l.113–139). No Gathering beat, Ask board or payoff moment recalls a pin. The v2 rule that Garrick asks "What would change your mind?" (v2 l.25) is missing from every night as played. Even when the story does answer a fork (W4D2, W4D4, W4D6), nobody is reminded what they bet.
2. **Many forks ask what the chapter has just settled, or will settle next morning.** W3D4, W3D5 and W4D5 are settled by the chapter's own summary card or the next morning, and W3D1 by the next day's chapter. W3D6 offers a choice the story overrides. Five of the twelve nights give friends nothing to argue about.
3. **The forks miss the real mystery, which often sits in the Pull.** The unasked questions are: who heard the map at the cairn (W3D6), what they were waiting for (W4D3, "us"), and why anyone watches an empty tower (W4D5). The Week 4 question, "What do they need Joss for?", was already answered at Gathering 3 ("To wake him", E3 l.928). Week 4's live question is the W4D1 Pull: "Why are you still alive?", which Gathering 4 answers (E4 l.853).
4. **Clues are often flavour with a lean attached.** The weak links are 3-2-1, 3-2-3, 3-3-1 and 3-3-3. Some clues repeat or contradict the chapter (3-5-1 repeats it; 3-1-2 contradicts "riding light"). Two leans look wrong: 4-1-1 and 4-5-1. Several open threads never close: the grey wool (3-1-1, 3-5-3), the chip scratches (3-4-3), the missing horses (3-6-1), and the watcher at the grandmother's window (4-3-2, 4-3-3, 4-6-3). Also, in a party of two at most two of the three clues arrive, sometimes only the "both" one.
5. **Canon risk.** W4D1 invites suspicion of someone in the room, which canon keeps shelved. W4D4 stacks three steers toward "moon-blood is not the marked" and undercuts E5's designed wrong conclusion. Neither gives the secret away outright, but both work against the season's design.

**The three best nights:** W4D4 (a real ambiguity, settled by the finale's biggest reveal), W4D6 (balanced, and answered the next night by the Verse), and W4D2 (concrete objects, the box and the coin, that pay off at Gathering 4 and E5 Day 1).

**What already works:** the meaning questions are the strongest element. Eleven of the twelve are answered in the story, most within days (W3D3, W3D4 and W3D6 at Gathering 3; W4D2 and W4D3 at Gathering 4). The callbacks connect days well. Week 4's forks build toward the turn at Gathering 4; Week 3's are a series of separate prompts.

## 4. Direction for fixing

**Cheap**
- Add Garrick's "What would change your mind?" after a pin, as v2 already says.
- At each Gathering, read back the week's forks next to the moment that answers them. For example: the W4D2 pins at "Count the boxes", the W4D6 pins at "closer than shadows", and the W4D4 pins held over to E6's moon-blood reveal. Show names only, as the read-back rule already does, and never grade.
- Fix the clues: re-lean 4-1-1 and 4-5-1; replace 3-5-1, which repeats the chapter; put "though six rode this way" back into 3-6-1; settle the cask against "riding light".
- Soften W4D4: drop the opener's steer, or make 4-4-2 truly ambiguous.
- Change Week 4's question to the "still alive" question.

**Deeper**
- Rewrite the forks for W3D4, W3D5, W3D6, W4D3 and W4D5 around the question their Pull raises. A fork should ask something the story will answer later than tomorrow morning, where each reading has a real clue and the answer is a surprise.
- Give W4D3 a reading that can be right ("they are waiting for whoever can open the way").
- Decide whether the watched-window and grey-wool threads should pay off somewhere, for example in a Gathering notice or Ask answer, or cut them.
- Decide with Fake Guru whether W4D1's suspicion of someone in the room is wanted. If it is, it needs a true, scripted resolution, such as a later notice that clears or implicates through true statements.
- Write each clue against a stated payoff. Each lead should carry "answered at: …" next to `leans`, so a clue that answers nothing shows up in review.
