# dark-moon-gameplay-report.md

*Full audit against `gameplay-audit-prompt.md`, using `gameplay-rhythm.md`, the three season play-through files, `after-the-finale.md`, and `gameplay-quality-17oct.md`. Per-day Play scores from Stages 1–3 are folded into the mechanic and pacing sections below. Claims marked **(guess)** are inferred, not read.*

---

## 1. The verdict in five lines

1. **The gameplay is promising but underpowered.** The best pieces — Seeker maps, Notice lines, Campfire pins, United Effort — are real, but too many weekday buttons are plain "go on" taps.
2. **It is more story than game, and that is right for this audience.** The problem is not too much story; it is that the taps between story beats often carry too little consequence.
3. **The single most important change:** make every weekday button carry story weight — a decision, a reveal, a cost, or something a friend will see — and make failed rolls change clues, the next day, or the Gathering.
4. **Keep the Seeker map as the model.** It is the clearest case of a mechanic being the fiction, and the twist depends on it.
5. **A small real playtest is now more valuable than more paper analysis.** The files cannot show whether the Campfire actually produces talk, whether the Gathering fits 30 minutes, or whether tapping through a fight is boring or tense.

## 3. The core loop

**The day.** A solo chapter of about 577 words with 6.5 taps, one paid beat (a Challenge or a fight), sometimes [PRESS ON], a Pull, a Seeker Fragment, the last light, then the Campfire. The player feels story, then a light game beat, then a social/theory screen.

*What works:* the day is short, readable and built around a mystery; the fragment gives a week-long puzzle; the Campfire is the right place for asynchronous theory-sharing.

*What is missing:* the day's "game" is mostly a pass/fail roll and a fragment in the Bag. The story and game are not woven: it is "story, then game, then talk." The player's main decision is often which best Attribute to roll.

**The week.** Six days of chapters and fragments, then a Gathering with votes, Battle, United Effort, Match/Ask and a table question. The week builds meaning, but too little of the weekday play changes how the Gathering actually plays.

**The season.** Six weeks build to a finale. The Seeker maps are the strongest season-level mechanic because solving them is the twist. The risk is that the finale pays off story more than six weeks of *play* unless weekday consequences, Relics, theories and failures carry forward.

**With the secret in hand (Stage 4):** the mechanics serve the twist well where it matters most. The Seeker maps are the model: solving them is how the Order finds the Stones. The Campfire's unpaid pins are thematically resonant — the party is being used — but gameplay-wise, being right should still feel good. The fifth Stone's refusal to answer your blood is perfect: the mechanic *is* the twist. The weakness is that the weekdays don't build enough *gameplay* tension toward the finale; the finale pays off story more than six weeks of play.

## 5. Pacing — story against play

The rhythm numbers are clear: a weekday is mostly story — about 577 words, 6.5 taps, longest read 409 words — so the earlier fear of "point to point with no story" is **not** supported. The problem is the opposite: the story is there, but the taps are often empty.

- **Where story needs more room:** at big turns — deaths, reveals, the Gathering's twist. The source itself suggests "Canon's quiet minute, a held line, a scene with no button."
- **Where story needs less room:** the Gathering's longest reads, 880–2,230 words, especially Gatherings 2 and 6 at about 10 minutes before talk/play. Split those reads with votes, rolls or table questions.
- **Where play should move inside the scene:** the Challenge, [PRESS ON], the fragment find and Notice lines. The fragment should not live only in the Bag; the Challenge should not only say pass/fail; Notice lines should feed the Campfire.
- **The rule:** a plain "go on" is acceptable only before a reveal, after a death, or where a pause is the point.

## 6. The eight questions, answered

1. **More game or story?** More story, and that is right. The audience wants a shared mystery, not a grind. The fix is not more game between story beats; it is heavier buttons.
2. **Enough room for story between buttons?** Yes. The rhythm numbers show long reads, not point-to-point. The issue is that too many taps are low-consequence.
3. **Woven through or after?** Mostly after, now: "story, then game, then talk." It should be more woven: decisions inside the chapter, fragment noticing inside the scene, Notice lines feeding the fire.
4. **Does every button earn its place?** No. Most are "go on"; 8 choices lead to the same text; the daily Attribute choice is usually obvious. Cut/merge plain go-ons and flavour choices; make the rest decisions, reveals or beats.
5. **Do the dice matter?** Not enough. Fails often cost gold/comfort and are forgotten; fights are 95–99% wins; [PRESS ON] is safe. Make dice change clues, next-day scenes or Gathering conditions.
6. **Do friends need each other?** Not enough yet. Notice lines are the seed; Relics are rarely needed; Campfire pins are not paid back. Pool uneven information, make Relics planned, and acknowledge correct theories.
7. **Does the week build to the Gathering?** Partly. The map +2, Match and Ask carry attention forward, but weekday play changes the Gathering's meaning more than its play. Make weekday outcomes set visible Gathering conditions.
8. **Would I come back for six weeks?** The Pull and fragment puzzle would bring me back; go-on taps and repeated fight escapes would make me drift. The daily hook must be a question I want a friend's answer to.

## 8. What to test with real players

1. **Does the day feel long or short?** Time three players on Week 1; ask where they wanted to stop.
2. **Do friends actually talk at the fire?** Count pins, replies and cross-references; observe whether anyone says "I saw something you didn't."
3. **Does the Gathering fit 30 minutes?** Stopwatch each section; mark where reading runs past 5 minutes without a vote/roll.
4. **Is tapping through a fight boring or tense?** Ask after each fight for a 1–5 tension rating; watch for fast tapping.
5. **Can a friend who missed a day still help?** Simulate one absent player at Gathering 1; see whether they have a meaningful action.

---

## 9. What must not change

- **The Seeker Fragments and maps.** They are the best mechanic and the model for "rules tell the story."
- **Notice lines as uneven information.** Do not make everyone see the same detail.
- **The Campfire pin board.** It is the mystery game at its best; simplify around it, don't replace it.
- **The United Effort's shared bars and turns.** It is genuinely social; add decisions, don't cut it.
- **Match rewarding attention.**
- **Nobody dies; the plot is fixed; failure costs gold, information, time or position, never the story.**
- **Garrick never lies.** The twist depends on it, and the mechanics already serve it: every mechanic that seems to withhold pays off in the Recap.
