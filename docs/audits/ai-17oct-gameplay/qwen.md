# dark-moon-gameplay-report.md

## 1. The Verdict
The gameplay is heavily story-driven, which perfectly suits an audience seeking a shared mystery rather than a mechanical grind. 
However, interactive elements (taps, rolls, choices) often lack narrative weight, reducing the "game" to moving between story beats. 
The most critical change is to ensure every button press carries narrative or mechanical consequence, transforming passive reading into active investigation. 
By making uneven information (Notice lines) the core social engine and ensuring failed rolls alter the story, the game will feel like a true collaborative mystery. 
Ultimately, the foundation is brilliant, but the mechanics must be woven into the fiction, not just appended to it.

## 3. The Core Loop
- **The Day:** The story is strong (~577 words), but the gameplay (taps/rolls) feels disconnected from the narrative. The player reads, then plays a mini-game, then talks. It needs to be woven together so the mechanics *are* the investigation.
- **The Week:** The fragment puzzle builds beautifully and creates a great weekly arc. However, the daily chapters don't actively feed into it until the end, making the mid-week days feel slightly detached from the main puzzle.
- **The Season:** The overarching mystery and the twist (the map reveals the cult's path) are brilliant. But the mid-season mechanics (fights, standard challenges) risk feeling repetitive and lack the narrative weight of the puzzle mechanics.

## 5. Pacing: Story Against Play
- **The Weekday Flow:** The game is mostly story (577 words, ~3 mins) broken by 6.5 taps. The reading is good, but the taps are mostly "go on". The story doesn't need to be longer; the *buttons* need to carry the story. 
- **Where the story needs more room:** Gatherings 2 and 6 have up to 10 minutes of reading before any play. Break this up with micro-decisions or held moments so players aren't just reading a script.
- **Where play should move inside the scene:** The fragment puzzle and the "last light" currently happen *after* the chapter. Weaving the puzzle clues into the chapter itself (via Notice lines) will make the story and the game inseparable.
- **Breathing room:** Replace "go on" taps with dramatic pauses. A held line, a quiet minute, or a scene with no button allows the story to land.

## 6. The Eight Questions, Answered

1. **Is it more game or more story?** It is more story, which is right for this audience. The balance is off not because there is too much story, but because the "game" buttons lack weight. 
2. **Is there enough room for the story between the buttons?** Yes, but the buttons themselves need to carry the story. It doesn't feel like "point to point" if the taps are meaningful decisions rather than just "next".
3. **Should the game be woven through the story or come after?** It should be woven through. Right now, it's story first, then game. Decisions inside scenes should yield the clues, making the investigation feel immediate.
4. **Does every button earn its place?** No. Cut "go on" and flavour choices. Every button must be a real decision, a reveal, or a dramatic beat.
5. **Do the dice matter?** Mostly no. They move gold, which buys unneeded gear. Fails must cost clues, narrative position, or alter the scene to make the dice matter.
6. **Do friends need each other?** Not enough. Notice lines create uneven info, but no mechanic forces sharing. Fix this to make them talk, trade, and plan.
7. **Does the week build to the Gathering?** Yes, via fragments and Campfire. But weekday choices should directly alter the Gathering's mechanical conditions (e.g., Battle difficulty).
8. **Would I come back every day?** Yes, the Pull and fragments are strong hooks. I might drift away if the mid-week "game" feels like a chore (e.g., pointless fights with no stakes).

## 7. Proposals, Ranked

1. **Cut all "flavour" choices and "go on" taps.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 1, 7. Forces every button to be a meaningful decision or dramatic beat.
   - *Cost:* Small (deleting text/UI).
   - *What could go wrong:* Pacing might feel too slow if the remaining story blocks are too long without careful writing.
2. **Make failed rolls lose clues or alter the scene.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 2, 3. Gives the dice real stakes and makes the mystery harder/more dynamic.
   - *Cost:* Medium (writing alternate text branches).
   - *What could go wrong:* Players might feel punished; ensure alternative paths or hints exist so the mystery remains solvable.
3. **Force pooling of Notice lines at the Campfire.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 5, 3. Makes uneven information the core social engine; forces friends to talk.
   - *Cost:* Medium (UI changes to share/pool clues).
   - *What could go wrong:* Might frustrate players if they miss a day and hold a key clue, locking the group out.
4. **Integrate Seeker Fragment clues into the daily chapter text.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 10, 4. Weaves the puzzle into the story; makes the chapter feel like an investigation.
   - *Cost:* Medium (rewriting chapters to embed clues naturally).
   - *What could go wrong:* Makes the chapter longer; need to balance reading time to stay under 12 mins.
5. **Reward correct Campfire theories at the Gathering.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 3, 13. Pays back attention; makes players feel smart and validates their social play.
   - *Cost:* Small (adding a check and a line of text/Garrick's reaction).
   - *What could go wrong:* None. This is a pure win.
6. **Add tactical choices to Fights and the United Effort.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 1, 8. Breaks the repetition of tapping; gives players agency in combat.
   - *Cost:* Medium (new UI/logic for 2 options per turn).
   - *What could go wrong:* Increases complexity; keep it simple (e.g., "Strike" vs "Defend") so it doesn't bog down the 30-min Gathering.
7. **Make [PRESS ON] rewards visible to the party.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 5, 1. Turns a solo risk into a social contribution.
   - *Cost:* Small (UI tweak to drop the reward into the Campfire).
   - *What could go wrong:* Might encourage reckless rolling; balance the stakes so it's a meaningful risk.
8. **Give classes unique Notice lines and Challenge advantages.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 12, 8. Makes character growth feel distinct; a Warrior's day feels different from a Sage's.
   - *Cost:* Medium (writing class-specific text variants).
   - *What could go wrong:* Might unbalance the party if one class dominates the clues; ensure all classes get vital, but different, info.
9. **Break up the long reading blocks in Gatherings 2 and 6.**
   - *Why it helps:* Criteria 7, 9. Keeps players engaged during the longest sessions.
   - *Cost:* Small (inserting micro-decisions or held moments into the text).
   - *What could go wrong:* Might interrupt the narrative flow if not done at natural dramatic breaks.
10. **Tie the "last light" hint to a friend's Campfire pin.**
    - *Why it helps:* Criteria 5, 6. Connects the solo evening play directly to the social Campfire.
    - *Cost:* Small (backend logic to match pins to hints).
    - *What could go wrong:* Requires complex backend logic; if no one pins a theory, the hint might fail to trigger.

## 8. What to Test with Real Players
1. **Does the day feel like a chore or a hook?** Watch session length, tap speed, and drop-off rates. If they rush through the "go on" taps, the story isn't landing.
2. **Do friends actually talk at the Campfire?** Monitor external chat logs (Discord/WhatsApp) and in-app pin interactions. Are they debating, or just posting?
3. **Does the Gathering fit in 30 minutes?** Time the live session. If it runs over, the reading blocks or the Ask board are too long.
4. **Is tapping through a fight boring or tense?** Observe player engagement and vocal reactions during fight scenes. If they are looking at their phones while waiting for others, it's boring.
5. **Do players understand the Seeker map twist?** Check hindsight feedback. Did they realize *they* were leading the cult to the Stones, or did it feel unearned?

## 9. What Must Not Change
- **The Seeker Fragment and Map mechanic:** It is the core twist, perfectly integrates mechanics with fiction, and works brilliantly.
- **The asynchronous Campfire structure:** It perfectly fits the audience's schedule and creates the social mystery vibe.
- **The pre-written, non-improvised nature of the AI storyteller:** Keeps the narrative tight, ensures the twist lands, and prevents the AI from breaking the mystery.
- **The 2-6 player fixed party size and the "nobody dies" rule:** Keeps the game accessible, social, and focused on the mystery rather than survival optimization.
